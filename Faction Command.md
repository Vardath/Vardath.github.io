# Faction Command — Master Game Design and Development Plan

Version: 0.2 | Status: foundation and vertical slice in progress | Target: Android landscape, offline-first

## Vision
Faction Command is an ORIGINAL 2D, map-driven space economy and faction management simulation inspired by the depth of space trading/empire games. It must not copy X4: Foundations code, art, text, faction/sector/ship names, maps, or distinctive UI assets. The player commands a faction rather than manually pilots ships. Gameplay centers on strategic map navigation, property management, individual ship orders, automation, production, diplomacy, policing and autonomous battles.


## Deep simulation, simple controls
- Aim for the interacting economic, fleet, station-building and faction-management depth of a large space sandbox such as X4, while keeping the setting, assets and presentation original. Simplifying the interface must not remove the underlying simulation or meaningful strategic choices.
- Lead with a clear overview, sensible defaults and the few decisions needed for the current task. Reveal deeper settings, statistics and fine control when the player asks for them; avoid making new players configure every detail before they can act.
- Make automation understandable and controllable: show its goal, current activity, costs and reasons for waiting; retain manual commands, useful overrides and safe ways to change course as those systems are developed.
- Explain consequences before the player commits resources, surface important shortages and opportunities, and connect alerts to the action that can resolve them. Keep physical goods, money, travel and outcomes consistent behind both simple and advanced views.
- Design for touch and readability from the start, with large targets, concise labels, accessible alternatives to gestures and controls that remain manageable as the number of ships and stations grows.

## Android map and object-command interface — Space RPG-inspired interaction, original design
- **Map-first presentation:** take inspiration from the quick readable galaxy node/link map and narrow selection inspector of Space RPG 1–4, not its art, trade dress, exact layout, iconography, text or code. The player is a faction commander, not a manually piloted ship. On landscape Android, the star/sector map is the dominant surface; a compact collapsible inspector occupies the side or bottom depending on available screen width. Never force the player to open a large modal merely to inspect a target.
- **One selection, one command scope:** tap a sector, station, ship, gate, asteroid, archive, resource field or other supported map object. Show name, faction, standing, location, status and an actionable summary. Distinguish *inspected target* from *active owned commander*; tapping an NPC vessel never changes ownership or inadvertently sends an order from the player's ship. Selecting an owned ship or station makes its valid orders explicit. Selection persists when panning, zooming and switching sub-tabs.
- **Contextual information tabs:** `Info`, `Orders`, `Cargo`, `Crew`, `Equipment`, `Trade`, `Production`, `Population`, `Construction`, `Research`, `Defence`, `Diplomacy`, `History` as meaningful for the selected object; do not present irrelevant empty tabs. A ship offers orders/navigation, cargo, equipment and crew; a station offers trade, storage, production, construction, workforce, schooling, finances and defence; a sector offers routes, discovered assets, traffic, hazards and owner; an archive offers records and research relevance. Keep command context and current tab stable after refresh/save.
- **Simple command grammar:** *Select an owned unit* → *inspect or select destination* → *choose a legal command* → *see order queue / duration / cost / blocking reason*. Give immediate feedback and a clear undo/cancel path where the simulation permits it. For tapping a hostile or third-party object, show information and only legally available commands (e.g. inspect, travel, scan, trade where permitted), never an owned-only action. No onscreen flight joystick, manual steering or hidden order changes.
- **Two map scales, one state:** the galaxy map shows a clean system-link network and routes, known ownership, missions, player assets and selection highlights; zooming into a sector shows a 2D tactical representation of real stations, gates, ships, resource zones and combat as those positions become available. Keep a consistent back gesture or map zoom to return. Support fog of war, search/filter and sector/ship lists for accessibility. Panning and pinch gestures must never accidentally dispatch movement commands.
- **Compact menu system:** use a bottom/side tab strip with large (at least ~44–48 dp equivalent) touch targets; sub-tabs and expandable sections take the place of long nested modal stacks. Make the primary command visible first, with advanced parameters below it, and preserve a separate searchable global property list. A limited always-visible HUD shows current faction credits, time/SETA speed, alerts and selection. High UI-scale accessibility should adapt layouts rather than overlap or squeeze fonts.
- **X4 comparison before every implementation pass:** explicitly review (1) what systemic mechanic X4 uses and the player decisions it creates; (2) what it feels like for a mobile touch user in Space RPG-style map navigation; (3) what must remain physically/financially simulated; (4) how to simplify control surfaces without erasing depth; (5) game-state, accessibility, save and Android-performance regressions. Record the decision and trade-off in `Faction Command Progress.md`. For example, X4's habitation capacity and workforce provisions remain meaningful, but Faction Command's station designer lists module types and quantities rather than reproducing a 3D snap-to-connector editor.
- **Pass 27 implementation:** the original map now drives a contextual inspector with object-specific, horizontally scrollable touch tabs. Ships, sectors, stations, archive stations and construction sites show appropriate real simulation information; taps on NPC ships remain read-only. The selected target is separate from the owned command ship; NPC stations expose a legal trade action rather than forced modal navigation, and owned property exposes only owner-appropriate management actions. Existing detailed trade, crew, build, population and research screens remain integrated and all orders require explicit confirmation via the original order control. At compact landscape widths the property directory is an Assets drawer that alternates with the inspector without hiding the map. Camera and object/tab selections persist through live refreshes. **Still planned:** more selectable object kinds, high-accessibility-scale physical device testing, equipment/defence/diplomacy tabs when their game systems exist, and further layout adaptations for other form factors.

## Non-negotiable gameplay
- Start with one owned ship, modest credits and crew; grow through trade, mining, missions and production into stations, shipyards, fleets and territory.
- All player-owned property appears in a searchable, filterable tree: ships, stations, fleets, construction, and subordinate groups. Select any asset to inspect orders, inventory, equipment, crew, finances and behavior. The implemented first stage is a searchable flat directory of owned ships, commissioned stations and active/cancelled construction sites, with selection by stable ID and context-specific management actions. Ship-specific commands, trading and supply target the selected owned ship, never a fixed starter-ship index; fleet grouping and hierarchical filters remain future work.
- No manual piloting. Player directs ships by map click/tap and context menus. Ships travel and fight autonomously. Pause and issue orders; adjustable simulation speed.
- Individual orders include fly to, dock, buy/sell, transfer cargo, mine, salvage, explore, scan, escort, attack, defend, patrol, police, repair, refit, recruit, board (where permitted), construct, and retreat. Persistent order queues; interrupt/resume; repeat and conditional orders.
- Selectable ship roles/standing behaviors: sector/local/galaxy trader, miner, salvager, explorer, surveyor, mapper, taxi, tour operator, courier, builder, supply ship, patrol, police/inspector, escort, bounty hunter, rescue, hacker/intelligence, fleet support, military roles. Each uses real targets, travel, cargo, costs and payouts, not passive income fabrication.
- Hire/fire/transfer/promote/train captains, pilots, engineers, marines, station managers and service crews; track skills, wages, morale and availability. The initial operational crew slice supports renaming player ships and hiring, discharging or transferring anonymous service crew. Hiring requires docking at a friendly/neutral NPC station with a finite persisted recruit pool, paying its real station budget per recruit. Discharge returns crew to that pool without refunded pay; transfers conserve actual people between co-docked player ships with available berths. Ship class controls service crew berth capacity, while the named captain remains a separate pilot. A second completed staffing slice assigns stable individual captain identities and profiles, supports co-docked captain swaps, tracks captain piloting skill and crew aggregate job training through real completed actions, and bills deterministic faction payroll every 60 ticks (12 CR + 2 CR per captain skill level + 3 CR per service crew). Wages transfer to an existing NPC personnel agency; arrears, paid totals, consecutive missed cycles and morale are saved. Unpaid wages accumulate without overdraft and lower morale; credit recovery pays back arrears. Actual per-person crew identities, rank progression, salary distribution to personal wallets, skill effects on job outcomes and captain hiring/firing are future work.
- Autonomous combat shown on zoomed sector map: real positions, movement, target selection, weapon ranges/cooldowns, shields/hull, missiles, repair, retreat, boarding, casualties and salvage. Police scan cargo, enforce faction laws, respond to crime and affect reputation.
- Build stations and production chains, logistics networks, shipyards and fleets. NPC factions have budgets, inventories, production, consumption, shipbuilding and territorial objectives; destruction and shortages matter.
- Unified transaction ledger for every asset: purchases, sales, wages, repairs, construction, missions, taxes and profits. Save/load full simulation.

## Galaxy and map
- Target initial authored/procedural hybrid galaxy: ~300 sectors, 20–30 distinct original factions; scalable to more only after performance validation. Start MVP at 12–20 sectors and 4 factions.
- Original sector names, topology, regions, environmental hazards, resources, ownership, laws, infrastructure, gates and contested borders.
- Continuous zoom from galaxy graph to sector 2D tactical view. Galaxy level displays nodes, trade routes, faction territory, filters, mission markers and moving asset indicators. Sector level shows ships, stations, gates, asteroid fields, resource zones, combat and traffic.
- Same underlying simulation for both views; distant objects need not be rendered. Fog of war, sensor range, discovered routes, searchable assets and map overlays.
- Touch gestures: pinch zoom, pan, tap select, long-press context order, multi-select and accessible list alternatives. Keep UI readable on a phone in landscape.
- First sector-view increment: use a clearly identified schematic of actual sector connections, stations and ships until intra-sector coordinates exist. Selecting a station opens its real market and selecting a connection navigates to that neighboring sector. Continuous tactical zoom and spatial movement remain later work.

## Economy — authoritative simulation
- Every ware has unit volume, production inputs, outputs, cycle duration, workforce/energy requirements, storage and legal status.
- Mines and energy facilities supply refineries; refineries supply component factories; components and advanced goods feed station construction, shipyards, weapons and equipment; civilian demand and upkeep create sinks.
- Stations maintain finite inventories and credits. Ships transport physical cargo and pay/receive credits; docking, travel time, storage and throughput constrain logistics.
- Pricing responds to stock coverage, demand and regional risk within bounded rules; no arbitrary infinite-money loops.
- NPC traders seek profitable feasible routes; NPC factions invest and expand from actual economic capability. Shortages halt production and slow shipbuilding. Piracy and war disrupt supply.
- Define deterministic production ticks and transaction atomicity; prevent duplicate sales, negative inventories, ghost cargo and save/load exploits.
- Track ware-level stock flow, credit flow, faction solvency, factory utilization and logistics bottlenecks. Include debug economic inspector and repeatable seed scenarios.

## Ship and station model
- Ship classes: scout, courier, fighter, corvette, freighter, miner, salvage, transport, destroyer, carrier, construction vessel. Original hull designs and names; modular engine, shields, weapons, sensors, cargo and drones. Initial acquisition slice: an NPC wharf at Vellum Foundry offers two data-driven paid hull contracts (Courier and Freighter). It consumes real Alloy Plates and transfers player credits into the NPC station's budget at order placement; production advances through one serialized assembly berth with up to five pending orders. Purchased player ships arrive at the wharf as full independent owned assets, with a bundled contract pilot/crew. Future: dedicated wharf facilities, richer components, refits, customer-supplied materials and player-owned shipyards.
- Ships have position, velocity abstraction, orders, role, captain, crew, fuel/energy if used, cargo, hull/shields, equipment, faction ownership, operating costs and history.
- Stations have modules, production recipes, dock capacity, storage, managers, workers, buy/sell rules, build queues and defense systems. Their habitation modules eventually host persistent residents, family growth and suitable life-support systems; education, health, community and recreation modules train or sustain real people and are part of the station economy rather than decorative modifiers.
- Fleet hierarchy supports commanders, subordinates, formation/doctrine, resupply and automatic replacement.
- Shipyards consume components and time to produce real hulls; production slots and equipment stocks matter.

## Population, habitation, education and professional skills — governing design
This is the governing specification for a management-first, original 2D space economy with the systemic depth of a large space sandbox. **Pass 25 implements the first conserved resident-workforce ledger; Pass 26 implements buildable power-serviced residential modules.** Existing finite recruitment pools remain the same counted residents and real hires/deliveries deduct from them. Crew, family and adaptive habitats commission through paid construction and real Alloy Plate/Energy Cell deliveries, provide bounded physical housing space, and consume Energy Cells on 60-tick service cycles. Loss of power removes operational capacity without destroying the physical module or existing residents. Births, immigration, food, medical supplies, species-specific life support, education and welfare remain future work; no habitat automatically creates people. All migration continues to preserve existing property, captain IDs, orders, skills, credits, arrears and older saves.

### Habitats generate and sustain real populations
- **Residential modules are the primary source of station inhabitants.** They supply species-appropriate living space for families, children, students, working-age adults and elders, instead of directly creating trained adults. Habitation modules can support **births over time** when there are residents of suitable age, functioning life support, available room and adequate welfare. Births add children, not instantly available pilots or marines; their maturation is represented on a longer simulation schedule. The hab's construction alone never grants free service crew.
- **Immigration and emigration must be accounted for.** Colonies, planets and other stations start with seeded, finite populations. Migrants leave a known population source, travel on actual civilian/passenger transport capacity with time and route risk, and arrive at a destination where they occupy available housing. A new empty habitat attracts settlers or takes transported residents before it can have births. People cannot exist in two stations at once, and losing a transport can have demographic consequences. No infinite free recruit pool.
- Keep population ledgers by species/biological requirements and coarse life-stage cohorts (children, learners, working-age residents, retirees), occupation and training level. Only named captains, managers, educators, exceptional specialists or story characters need individual persistent records. Large ordinary populations are aggregated cohorts for Android performance; all movements between cohorts conserve people. Named individuals are linked to a specific source cohort and must not duplicate their source count.
- **Life-support compatibility matters**: different species/cultures can require different gravity, pressure, atmospheric chemistry, humidity, temperature, radiation protection, aquatic environments or other requirements. Create adaptable hab modules and separate habitation zones if necessary; a population cannot be forced to live in an incompatible atmosphere. The setting includes worlds with primitive, advanced, unusual and inhospitable environments. Species and culture influence needs and opportunities, **not fixed intelligence or forbidden skill paths**.
- Each habitat has physical population capacity, sustainable birth/maturation and migration throughput, energy and resource upkeep, service staffing and vulnerability to shortages. Its residents consume food, water, medical supplies, consumer necessities and energy as those ware chains are implemented. Sanitation, recycling and waste management matter. During early integration use explicit balance placeholders for not-yet-authored wares, never claim nonexistent food or water deliveries took place.
- An apartment block or compact crew hab offers basic capacity; a **Family Habitat** adds space for households and births; a **City Habitat** houses larger populations at higher upkeep and social-service demand; an **Adaptive/Xenobiology Habitat** supports additional environmental needs at higher construction and life-support cost; an **Agricultural Habitat** links residential upkeep to real food production. All use actual builders, module inputs and construction time through the ordinary station planner.
- Population growth is **bounded**, slow relative to cargo cycles, and limited by housing, family formation, health, age structure, security, resources and immigration. Include aging, natural deaths and migration out, with an understandable demographic summary. A habitat does not endlessly multiply people from nothing and is not a passive credit generator.

### Work, careers, morale and mobility
- Residents may be dependants, students, unemployed jobseekers, civilian service staff, assigned station workers, teachers, scientists, doctors, managers, ship crew, pilots, marines or retirees. **Workplaces request qualified employees**. A person accepting ship or station employment leaves the local unassigned-worker pool; moving ships or changing jobs does not create a replacement person. Job vacancies and bottlenecks are displayed by station and faction.
- Workforce shortages reduce or halt the appropriate production or shipbuilding cycles only when the workforce model becomes active, never silently change already-delivered wares. Skill and morale alter efficiency within transparent, bounded limits and must not invent extra input resources. High-level specialists can unlock advanced research/shipbuilding where appropriate; lower-skill staff may take longer or require a mentor. Automation can replace tasks only when actual equipment and upkeep exist.
- Morale depends on housing quality, environmental safety, adequate nutrition and healthcare, pay reliability, working hours, security, education access, recreation, culture, social connection, faction policy and losses in conflict. Morale affects retention, immigration, absence, learning efficiency and labour productivity; do not create arbitrary direct credits. Unpaid wages and overcrowding lower morale; thoughtful station investment can counter them. Distinguish individual morale of named staff from aggregate resident satisfaction.
- Schooling is a **real service**: named or cohort instructors and actual students must have a suitable station, classroom capacity, available time, access to learning resources, relevant base skill, energy, wages and maintenance. Education consumes or licenses physical Recorded Data/Research Notes and appropriate teaching equipment where relevant. A school with no teachers or students produces no training. Qualified teachers and specialist classrooms cap throughput; repeated completion cannot mint people, infinite XP, wares or research.
- Training can be **general education, certification, profession conversion or advanced specialisation**. Work experience and school study both build skill, while practical qualifications can require a combination of the two. Use existing 0–5 skill tiers as an initial compatible scale. Different career tracks can be taught in parallel but cannot occupy the same student for overlapping full-time courses. Courses have time, prerequisites, seats, costs, completion records and optional student/teacher travel.
- An education credential is not a research discovery. **Research Laboratories** conduct projects consuming valid Research Notes; **Research Academies** train researchers; historical archives contribute actual relevant records and teach preservation, language, history and research methods. Schools may improve teams' research productivity, but do not grant finished discoveries merely because a module was built.
- Educational and welfare modules can be public, faction-run, player-owned or private. A school may enroll local residents or visiting students subject to travel and quotas. Fees and subsidies move existing money between owners, students and institutions; teachers and station workers receive wages from a funded budget. A boarding academy trains people for boarding operations, not for free instant ship capture.

### Management UI and automated education policy
- On **Station > Population & Education**, show residents, life stages, housing occupancy, species/environment needs, food/water/energy availability, job vacancies, unemployed/qualified applicants, morale, births, migration, education seats, active courses, trainees, teachers, expected graduates and current operating costs. Label absent systems as planned during implementation.
- Provide a few simple policies: **Balanced**, **Expand population**, **Train workforce**, **Attract specialists**, **Research campus**, **Naval training hub** or **Custom**. The planner can suggest numbers of habs, support facilities and schools for a target crew or production workforce; advanced users may specify course quotas, skill thresholds, species-compatible habitats and import rules.
- **Staffing:** when the player requests five marines or a three-star pilot, a search finds real candidates in the faction's employment pool; if insufficient, suggest recruit transport, housing, wages, training seats and completion times. A named hire and a cohort transfer are both sourced from a real pool, and the route is visible.
- **NPC factions use the same rules**: they house residents, build schools and hospitals, educate specialist workforces, recruit qualified employees, compete for staff and respond to displacement and shortages. Population structures become strategic civilian targets needing protection, not instant renewable crew dispensers.

### Implementation order and test gates (supersedes an isolated captain-market feature)
1. **Population ledger and save migration — implemented in Pass 25**: the existing finite recruit pools now map onto explicitly counted adult resident-worker cohorts (the same people, not new residents), with persistent stable station population IDs, save migration, strict roster mirror validation, a population overview and exact workforce deductions for ordinary hiring/discharge and paid ship delivery. Constructed stations start with empty cohorts and no fictional habitat capacity. Already-hired ships and named captains retain their exact identities and career states. More detailed age/demographic events await steps 2–3.
2. **Buildable habs and resources — initial implementation in Pass 26**: crew, family and adaptive residential modules are distinct station services. The list-based construction planner includes real input bills, builder time, capacity, explicit Energy Cells consumption and optional self-sufficient solar production. Modules become physical housing only after completion, can become unpowered on supply shortage, and create no adults or children. Actual food, water, medicine and species-environment needs must be added before live demographic growth and occupancy. Extend wares and production chains only as actual supported mechanics; surface import shortages.
3. **Population growth and movement (after civilian provisioning)**: introduce supported food, water and medical ware chains and real station consumption, then age cohorts, births, natural deaths, actual passenger immigration/emigration, housing constraints and offscreen deterministic simulation. Tests must show conservation of transfers, known birth/death events and no duplicate population after save/load.
4. **Workforce and recruitment integration**: connect station job slots, existing NPC recruit pools, ship crew hiring and paid wages to one resident workforce source; vacancies matter, staff cannot be present on a ship and at the origin station simultaneously. Migrate old abstract crew progressively without removing or duplicating personnel.
5. **Education and qualifications**: implement community school, vocational college, flight academy, station management college, research academy and marine/boarding school first. Courses require teachers, enrolled students, real resources, attendance time and wages, then increase appropriate skills/credentials. Extend to the broader curriculum by demand.
6. **Morale and civilian life**: introduce welfare facilities, healthcare, employment satisfaction, crowding, immigration attraction, population health and bounded skill/productivity impacts. Ensure player and NPC factions face equivalent economic rules and existing legacy saves remain playable.
7. **Scale and usability**: aggregate cohorts offscreen, show clear housing/workforce forecasts and education queues in the property screen, performance-test heavily populated systems on actual Android hardware, and verify savings/performance in long-running campaigns.

**Non-negotiable acceptance tests:** (1) a hab at commissioning provides capacity but no free adult workers; (2) any new child comes from a documented resident-family birth event, not a spawned pilot; (3) no cross-station migration without a source deduction and supported transit; (4) recruited and transferred staff always leave one counted pool and enter one destination; (5) teaching with no instructor, materials, power, students or free seats cannot produce XP; (6) skills and certificates remain tied to their owner after transfer, retirement or ship reassignment; (7) salaries, tuition and school upkeep move real balances; (8) morale and skill bonuses change only supported outcomes within bounded rules; (9) NPC and player population rules are consistent; (10) saving, loading, migration and destruction cannot double residents, wages or training; (11) an existing Pass-23 save still loads with all ships, crews, captains, orders and funds preserved; (12) Android headless builds stay green, followed by real-device tests before calling the system production-ready.

## Factions, law and diplomacy
- 20–30 original factions at full target: governments, corporations, guilds, frontier coalitions, pirates and independents, with distinct doctrines, home sectors, economic strengths and diplomatic relations.
- Reputation, trade permissions, embargoes, patrol jurisdictions, smuggling, contraband, scans, fines, criminal evidence, piracy, escorts, war, peace and territorial claims.
- Policing operates via patrol routes, sensors, scan decisions, legal rules, interception and reputation consequences; avoid omniscient enforcement.
- AI strategic planners make bounded decisions from economic and military state, with tunable difficulty but no mandatory resource cheating.

## Missions and narrative
- Original procedural contracts: deliveries, mining, taxi, tours, construction, escorts, defense, policing, surveys, salvage, humanitarian relief, piracy suppression and faction campaigns.
- Mission generation checks actual demand, reachable locations, stock availability and payer budget; payment on verified completion. Narrative quests must be original.
- Contracts include prerequisites, deadlines, cancellation rules, faction reputation and failure consequences.

## Android architecture
- Recommended: Godot 4, GDScript for UI/prototype; evaluate C# or native modules only if profiling proves necessary. Android landscape, offline-first, no mandatory server.
- Strict separation: deterministic simulation core; command/order engine; presentation/UI; persistence. Avoid tying simulation state to visible nodes.
- Fixed simulation ticks; event-driven jobs and sparse scheduling. Near/active sector receives detailed tactical updates; distant sectors receive coarser economic/strategic updates while preserving aggregate outcomes and consistent arrival times.
- Spatial indexing for nearby interactions; object pooling for visible markers/projectiles; cull offscreen visuals. Do not simulate thousands of rendered ships every frame.
- Battery/performance presets; cap render FPS independently of simulation; pause when backgrounded; autosave safely.
- Save versioning and migration; stable entity IDs; transactional persistence with integrity checks. Offline saves, manual save slots and crash recovery.
- Accessibility: scalable text, color-independent faction identification, readable icons, large touch targets and list-based command alternatives.

## Implementation phases and acceptance criteria
### Phase 0 — Foundation

### Phase 1 — Vertical slice

## List-based station construction and self-sufficiency planner
- The station builder is a mobile-friendly list of module types with quantity controls, rather than a manual 3D assembly puzzle. The player chooses production modules, storage, docks, housing, defenses, shipbuilding and support modules and enters quantities. New stations and existing station expansions use the same planner.
- Three resource-sourcing modes per station: **Fully self-sufficient**, **Import selected inputs**, and **Import all inputs**. The partial-import mode exposes each required ware as a toggle and permits per-ware import quotas, supplier restrictions and maximum purchase price.
- Player chooses target final output wares and desired production modules or output rate. The planner recursively expands the recipe dependency graph, calculates required intermediate production, mines/extractors, power, workforce, storage and docks, then rounds to whole modules. It must handle shared intermediates across multiple outputs, production cycle rates, alternative recipes, recycling and cyclic dependencies; cycles require explicit resolution or a warning, never infinite recursion.
- Self-sufficient means all *manufacturable* recurring inputs are produced locally at sufficient throughput; natural resources still require accessible local deposits/collectors. Warn if the sector lacks a resource or if workforce/energy is insufficient; offer import exceptions or alternative sites.
- For imported wares, omit their upstream production modules and calculate expected import throughput, transport capacity, running cost and exposure to shortages. Mixed sourcing must permit local production plus imports for a single ware.
- Planner previews module bill of materials, credit cost, estimated build time, required construction deliveries, per-minute inputs/outputs, workforce, power balance, storage days, expected profit and bottlenecks. Estimates must clearly distinguish current market prices from guaranteed prices.
- Construction is a persistent queued process: reserve budget/resources, obtain a builder, deliver physical materials, complete modules in sequence, and update station production when commissioned. No instant free modules. Allow pause, reorder, cancel with defined refund/salvage rules, and save/load mid-build.
- Implemented cancellation contract: cancelling a site is terminal for its remaining queue. Fees for never-started modules return from the builder's actual funds exactly once; any module already underway finishes using its previously consumed inputs; commissioned modules remain functional. Delivered but unspent materials stay in separate build storage at the cancelled plot and can only be recovered by a physically docked player ship with available cargo volume. Construction suppliers stop safely without losing orders or cargo. Preserve cancelled plots as recoverable locations until a later demolition feature. Reordering of never-started modules is now supported via persistent up/down queue controls; completed and actively building entries cannot move, and the operation does not transfer resources or credits. Station expansion is still planned.
- The player can assign station managers and subordinate traders/miners, define trade rules and budgets, enable auto-purchase of build materials, and expand stations later. NPC factions use the same module economics and construction constraints.
- The first station-input supplier uses a player-set purchase cap and keeps a modest buffer of net production deficits and reusable sources. It uses existing cargo or protected surplus from player stations, then buys reachable goods with real credits; it travels and delivers physically. It waits safely for funds, space or stock, reacts to exhausted deposits, and retains its cargo and orders when reassigned. Per-ware rules, multiple selected ships and automated output sales remain later extensions.
- MVP implementation: module selection and quantities, dependency calculation for three production chains, preview, construction queue and persistence. Later: full mixed sourcing, shared inputs, workforce, advanced station logic and shipyards.
- Acceptance tests: (1) self-sufficient plan covers all required recurring inputs at target rate; (2) import-all adds no unnecessary upstream factories; (3) selective imports remove only the correct dependency branches; (4) shared inputs are not double-counted; (5) missing resources and recipe cycles yield actionable warnings; (6) construction consumes materials/credits exactly once and survives save/load.

## Current design decisions
- Strategic management, **no manual piloting**.
- Galaxy map with zoomable 2D sectors and visible autonomous battles.
- Individual property commands, crew management and selectable autonomous ship occupations.
- Real shared economy for player and NPC factions.
- **Habitation as the source of population; population as the source of staff and specialists.** Education and welfare modules train and sustain people through bounded, paid simulation rather than granting instant invisible bonuses.
- Bigger galaxy and more factions than a typical initial prototype, contingent on performance.

## New game: faction origins and independent player faction
- At the start of a new game, let the player choose a starting faction from the available origins. Show the starting sector and initial relations with the other factions before the player confirms their choice.
- The choice represents the player's starting background or affiliation. The player's ships, stations, credits and other property belong to the player's own independent faction, not to the chosen NPC faction; NPC ownership and autonomy remain intact.
- Each origin defines the player's starting sector and initial relationship values with the relevant NPC factions. Those initial values are ordinary starting conditions: relations can change through gameplay as diplomacy and reputation systems are implemented. The chosen origin itself does not grant control of the NPC faction.
- Store origin definitions as game data, with stable faction and sector IDs and explicit initial relation values. Persist the chosen origin and current relations in saves once persistence exists; loading an existing game must not ask the player to choose again or reset earned relations.
- Initial prototype: offer the four starter NPC factions as origins with clear, provisional starting standings; give the player one independently owned starting ship in the selected sector. More factions and differentiated starts can be added as content develops.
- Acceptance: every available start puts the independently owned player ship in its defined sector; applies that origin's standings; leaves NPC ownership unchanged; rejects invalid or missing origins; previews the choice before starting; and, once saving is implemented, preserves the choice and evolving standings across save/load.

## Historical archives, media and research
- Replace the idea of isolated data vaults with **historical archive stations**: inhabited institutions maintained by the galaxy's NPC factions. Each civilization has its own history, world, perspectives and records; factions can contain multiple cultures and species. The independent player faction does not receive a free archive at the start.
- Build toward a rich, interconnected body of original lore with books, correspondence, chronicles, oral testimony, myths, maps, field journals, scientific works and conflicting accounts. Include fantasy-like worlds, early tool-using alien societies, worlds inhospitable to other species, advanced habitats and other distinctive environments. Cultures are complex at every technological level; leave genuine mysteries open and distinguish claims from established facts.
- Records have physical carriers, including books, carved tablets, discs, tapes, storage crystals and light drives. Preserve unique original artifacts separately from manufactured copies, with provenance, subjects, format and condition. Reading or copying an original must never destroy it. Expand formats, catalogues, permissions and preservation mechanics as the simulation develops.
- Archives are actual faction-owned stations with budgets, staff and resource needs, not passive map collectibles. Their owners fund operations from available resources; supply chains must bring real materials. Shortages, trade, damage and political conditions can affect service. A docked visitor can read the starter records even if upkeep is temporarily short.
- Data is an economic resource: copying requires an accessible original or retained source copy, writable media, energy and working equipment. Media manufacture requires physical inputs. Model the initial ware as an aggregate of copies and blank media; later distinguish individual formats, sources and cultural collections. Never imply a source-specific copy or original exists when the simulation has only an aggregate ware.
- The player can construct and expand archive facilities. The first archive scriptorium and media workshop use the ordinary station planner, builder, materials, storage and production systems. A player scriptorium needs an existing recorded-data source; a faction archive can copy from its own catalogue. Full player-owned catalogues, preservation services and individual artifacts are future extensions.
- Archives support research. Tag records and collections by subject, provenance and relevance; let research projects use appropriate accessible archives or documented copies and show exactly what each source contributes. More relevant, independent sources should help more than irrelevant or duplicate copies. Account for access, preservation quality, staff, project requirements and physical inputs; use transparent limits so repeated copies cannot create unlimited research.
- The first research slice uses a buildable Research Laboratory that turns Recorded Data and Energy Cells into physical Research Notes. One player-chosen active project consumes notes from its selected lab; progress is retained when paused or switched. Each note provides a base amount of work plus a capped bonus for distinct relevant archives the player has visited. The interface identifies each matching archive and subject. The visits are references learned by the player; aggregate Recorded Data does not yet carry provenance and must not be represented as a specific archive's copy.
- Starter research improves the speed of the player's Archive Scriptoria, Media Workshops or Ore Extractors. Effects are visible in production reports and station plans, do not apply to NPC stations, and do not fabricate inputs or deplete more resources than the actual production cycles use. Expand toward richer projects, prerequisites, scientific teams, archive access and source-specific evidence later.
- Keep the interface simple: find an archive on the sector map, dock to browse its collection, see its owner, service status, media and subjects, and later connect a relevant collection to a research project. Provide a searchable player catalogue and clear source breakdown as the number of records grows. Do not require players to manage every book individually unless they choose detailed controls.

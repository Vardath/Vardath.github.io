<h1 id="nine-disc-dragon-lattice-simulator">Interactive nine-disc dragon-lattice simulation — closed lattice, open door</h1>
<p>I am now picturing the larger structure as <strong>nine flat disc-worlds stacked above one another</strong>. The moving Möbius-like action belongs to the sky-dragon lattice rather than to the discs themselves.</p>
<p>When the door is closed, the lattice is <strong>separated and flattened into the layers</strong>. Under each disc, <strong>two twines combine to form one supporting lattice</strong>. During activation those flattened twines begin to energise, lift away from the layers, and reorganise into the opposed rising and descending weave. The crossing weave then tightens through the middle as the finger-trap / door structure. In the working sequence, plasma builds the conductive path first, atmosphere and vapour follow, water completes the bridge, and land transfer becomes possible once the geometry is fully engaged.</p>
<div class="callout warn"><strong>Working simulation.</strong> This is an exploratory visual model of the current Vardath mechanism, not a claim that the animation is established physical behaviour. The top/bottom boundary condition is deliberately left selectable because that part of the model is still unresolved.</div>

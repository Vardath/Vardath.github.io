<h1 id="26-yggdrasil-time-switch-plenum">26. Yggdrasil as the time-switch — self-to-self, Norns, moving lands and the plenum</h1>
<p>This branch began by returning to Odin’s ordeal on Yggdrasil and asking what the story would look like if the ash tree were not only a world-axis but the <strong>physical door in the Vardath cosmology</strong>. The result sharpened the door model considerably. I am no longer picturing a simple portal that removes a traveller from one place and drops them somewhere else. I am picturing a <strong>temporary switching junction in the woven finger-trap tree</strong>, where normally separated world-layers and times become physically adjacent and exchange what occupies them.</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>woven world-tree → strands converge → door / switch opens → separated times become adjacent → people and land exchange positions → weave closes → ordinary local time continues</strong></p>
<div class="callout warn"><strong>Working cosmological interpretation.</strong> The Norse sources establish Yggdrasil, Odin’s self-sacrifice, Mímir’s well and the Norns at Urðarbrunnr. They do not describe the mechanical time-switch proposed here. The switch, layered-land transfer and finger-trap geometry are my interpretation of those motifs inside Vardath Cosmology.</div>

<h2 id="odin-self-to-self-at-the-junction">Odin — “self to self” at the junction</h2>
<p>In <em>Hávamál</em>, Odin hangs for nine nights on the tree, wounded by a spear, sacrificing <strong>himself to himself</strong>, looking downward before taking up the runes. Elsewhere, the prose tradition places Mímir’s well beneath one root of Yggdrasil and says that Odin leaves an eye there as the price of wisdom. The same larger tree-complex also places the Norns beside Urðr’s well.</p>
<p>The new interpretation is that Odin is <strong>fixed to the junction while the weave changes around him</strong>. If different positions on one life-thread are temporarily brought together at the same gate, “self to self” can be read in the model as more than sacrifice: two locally separated states of the same person can become neighbours at the switch.</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>Odin(t₁) → Yggdrasil junction ← Odin(t₂)</strong></p>
<p>The mirror-self scenes that recur in modern stories became useful here as narrative analogies. <em>The NeverEnding Story</em> makes the traveller confront the self at the Magic Mirror Gate. <em>Annihilation</em> culminates in a lighthouse encounter where the humanoid double mirrors the traveller’s movements. These films are not evidence for the cosmology; they show how persistent the <strong>threshold → doubled self → identity transformation</strong> structure remains in modern storytelling.</p>

<h2 id="norns-temporal-relations-not-three-boxes">The Norns — temporal relations at the root of the tree</h2>
<p>The three named Norns — <strong>Urðr, Verðandi and Skuld</strong> — stand at Urðarbrunnr beneath Yggdrasil and shape lives. I am using their names less as three sealed boxes called “past, present and future” and more as three relations to one changing weave: <strong>what has become, what is becoming, and what is due / shall become</strong>.</p>
<p>That makes the Norns useful for the switch model. They are not merely observers looking along a timeline; in the cosmological reading they stand where routes through the tree are assigned. “Fate” then becomes analogous to <strong>which strand a person, population or piece of land follows when the junction changes</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="door-becomes-switch">The door becomes a switch rather than a hole</h2>
<p>The important correction in this conversation was <strong>switch places</strong>. The event is not only somebody entering a doorway. During the active phase, time rearranges adjacency. A piece of one world-layer can meet a piece of another, and matter can move through the temporary contact while each traveller still experiences ordinary forward motion locally.</p>

The gate could carry one person, but if the open seam extends across the lattice it could also move <strong>settlements, coastlines or larger pieces of land</strong>. <h2 id="mad-tea-party-locked-time-special-case">The Mad Tea Party — a locked-time special case, not the whole event</h2>
<p>The Mad Hatter’s tea party helped distinguish two phenomena. In <em>Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland</em>, the Hatter explains that after his quarrel with Time it is always six o’clock at the table. That is a useful image for a <strong>local loop or locked temporal address</strong>, but it is only one possible failure-mode of the larger mechanism I am describing.</p>
<p>The main event is dynamic: people and lands can move while the weave is rearranging. A locked region would instead be a strand that reconnects to the same temporal position repeatedly rather than being routed onward.</p>

<h2 id="moving-lands-through-time">Moving lands — absence is only half the prediction</h2>
<p>Once the door became an exchange switch, the disappearance of a culture or land was no longer the main thing to look for. <strong>Every removal should imply an arrival or replacement somewhere else in the larger structure.</strong> That means the stronger signature is not merely “something vanished,” but mismatched adjacency: material, people, architecture, stories or landscapes appearing in a context that does not fit the sequence around them.</p>
<p>During the conversation, <strong>Tartaria and the Maya</strong> were raised as possible examples to think with. At this stage I am treating them as candidate test cases, not confirmations. The useful question is not “can I make a vanished civilisation fit the model?” but “what physical or chronological signature would a real exchange leave that ordinary political change, migration, environmental collapse, relabelling or rebuilding would not?”</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>transfer hypothesis → predict paired discontinuity first → then inspect archaeology / geology / maps</strong></p>
<p>This keeps the hypothesis falsifiable. A completed land exchange should ideally leave some combination of paired boundary effects, abrupt material discontinuities, unusual chronological adjacency or matching displaced signatures elsewhere. If those are absent and ordinary historical continuity explains the record, the transfer interpretation loses support.</p>

<h2 id="plenum-rearrangement-not-empty-space">The plenum — rearrangement without empty space</h2>
<p>The final concept added in this conversation was the <strong>plenum</strong>: a space completely filled rather than containing true empty gaps. In René Descartes’ mechanical philosophy, material extension filled space and celestial motion was described through interlocking vortices. A body could not simply move into an empty place; motion required the surrounding matter to move as part of a larger circulation.</p>
<p>That is not being adopted as modern physics. Its importance here is conceptual. A plenum gives the Vardath switch a clean rule: <strong>if every available position is already occupied, movement through the event must be displacement, circulation or exchange rather than disappearance into nothing.</strong></p>
<p class="equation"><strong>A B C D E → E A B C D</strong></p>
<p>In that sense the plenum analogy joins the finger-trap and Yggdrasil ideas. The woven structure does not need an empty external container into which land temporarily vanishes. The weave changes its connections, and occupied regions push, trade or reroute one another as the event runs.</p>

<h2 id="current-time-door-statement">Current statement of the time-door idea</h2>
<p>The model coming out of this exchange is therefore:</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>Yggdrasil is the woven world-tree and temporary switch. The Norn-like temporal relations describe where strands have come from, what is changing now and what route becomes due next. During the event, normally separated layers can become physically adjacent. People and land can move through that adjacency and exchange positions. Local time still feels continuous to those carried along the strand. A plenum-like rule means the event rearranges occupied reality rather than sending matter into empty nothingness.</strong></p>
<p>This makes the earlier “door” language more exact. <strong>The door is not primarily an opening in space. It is the moment when the weave changes who and what is next to whom — and therefore when time can physically rearrange the world.</strong></p>

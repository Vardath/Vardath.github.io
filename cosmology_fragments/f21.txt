<h1 id="25-waterborne-child-travelling-fingertrap-v2-result">25. Waterborne-child travelling-fingertrap test — the frozen prediction is run</h1>
<p>The earlier travelling-fingertrap reading produced a concrete prediction rather than another loose resemblance. If the waterborne-child motif is structurally closer to the proposed <strong>contracted basket/ark → current → boundary/gate → reopened world</strong> sequence than exposed-child stories in general, then waterborne stories should carry a distinctive package: <strong>F1 enclosure geometry</strong>, <strong>F2 a boundary or receiver</strong>, and <strong>F3 changed household, identity or second-birth language</strong>. The feature vocabulary, proximity windows, comparison groups and statistical thresholds were frozen before the final multilingual corpus was scored.</p>

<div class="callout"><strong>Twenty-language design:</strong> discovery ran independently in English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Czech, Russian, Ukrainian, Greek, Turkish, Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Hindi, Bengali, Japanese and Chinese. Discovery queries did not contain the predicted basket/geometry, receiver or rebirth terms. Local discoveries were mapped through Wikidata to English records, full evidence was acquired in the language shards, and the final merge scored only the frozen local corpus with no network requests.</div>

<h2 id="waterborne-v2-corpus">Corpus and retrieval audit</h2>
<p>The finished run produced <strong>5,204 raw discoveries</strong>, <strong>4,599 English-mapped occurrences</strong> and <strong>3,628 unique English candidates</strong>. The rebuilt acquisition pipeline recovered evidence for <strong>4,547 of 4,599 mapped occurrences (98.9%)</strong>. After deduplication there were <strong>3,584 usable English records</strong>. The frozen eligibility rules retained <strong>399 exposed-child / mythic-context records</strong>: <strong>203 waterborne targets</strong>, <strong>160 non-water controls</strong> and <strong>36 ambiguous-water records</strong> excluded from the confirmatory comparison.</p>
<p>The canonical retrieval audit succeeded for all six stories fixed as coverage checks: <strong>Moses, Sargon of Akkad, Karna, Romulus and Remus, Danaë and Perseus</strong>. They were audit anchors only; they were not forced into the statistical sample.</p>

<p>The primary comparison is not underpowered: both groups are far above the frozen minimum of 25 records. The broad enclosure vocabulary also rose from 20.6% in controls to 36.5% in waterborne records (risk ratio <strong>1.77×</strong>, <code>p=0.00114</code>).</p>

<h2 id="waterborne-v2-meaning">What the result means for the travelling-fingertrap reading</h2>
<p>The strongest result is not simply that stories mentioning rivers also mention baskets. The pre-declared <strong>combined structural package</strong> occurs substantially more often in the waterborne group. Strict woven/hollow/sealed/flexible/reopenable enclosure language is <strong>2.43×</strong> enriched. Distinct arrival boundaries or receivers are <strong>1.53×</strong> enriched. Most strikingly, the complete <strong>enclosure + receiver + identity-change</strong> package occurs in 18 waterborne stories but only 2 controls, a <strong>7.09×</strong> risk ratio.</p>
<p>That is the direction predicted by the model sequence:</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>life / child → contracted fingertrap / basket / ark → current → boundary / gate → reception / reopening → changed household, identity or world</strong></p>
<p>One part of the prediction does not stand independently. Identity, adoption and second-birth language are already common in exposed-child traditions generally, so F3 by itself does not significantly distinguish the waterborne group. The signal appears when identity change is coupled to the enclosure-and-boundary geometry rather than treated as a unique water motif.</p>

<h2 id="waterborne-v2-boundary">Why this is strong evidence for a motif structure, but not yet a clean confirmation of the cosmology</h2>
<p>The frozen automated test passed, and the unrelated negative-control vocabulary did not show the same enrichment. That makes the result worth taking seriously as a <strong>real corpus signal</strong>. It does not establish that ancient storytellers were describing a literal fingertrap sky mechanism, nor does it prove one common historical source for every tradition.</p>
<div class="callout warn"><strong>Semantic audit warning:</strong> the automatic eligibility classifier is still too permissive. The scored target list includes genuine mythic material and canonical cases, but also obvious contaminants such as modern film/media pages, general infanticide topics, place pages and other records that happen to satisfy the frozen text rules. The completed result must therefore remain exactly what it is: a strong preregistered exploratory result. It should not be repaired post hoc by deleting inconvenient records.</div>
<p>The correct next move is an <strong>independent validation corpus</strong> restricted in advance to curated myths, fables, epics and religious narratives, with the same structural prediction tested against curated non-water exposed-child controls. If the 1.77× primary enrichment and especially the 7.09× complete-package enrichment survive that cleaner holdout, the travelling-fingertrap motif claim becomes considerably harder to dismiss as search noise or modern catalogue contamination.</p>
<p><strong>Current status:</strong> the prediction has survived its first adequately powered multilingual test, but the physical interpretation remains open. The result supports the existence of the predicted narrative geometry; it does not by itself identify the cause of that geometry.</p>

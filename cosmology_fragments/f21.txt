<h1 id="25-waterborne-child-travelling-fingertrap-v2-result">Waterborne children — the prediction meets the corpus</h1>
<p>The baby-in-the-river stories became important to the travelling-fingertrap model for one reason: they gave it a way to be wrong. The idea was no longer simply that many traditions contain a child, a basket and water. The proposed sequence was more specific: <strong>a living child enters a contracted carrier, moves with a current, reaches a boundary or receiver, and emerges into a changed household, identity or world.</strong></p>
<p>That sequence is close enough to the proposed sky mechanism to test directly:</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>life / child → contracted fingertrap / basket / ark → current → boundary / gate → reception / reopening → changed household, identity or world</strong></p>
<p>Before the search was run, the sequence was frozen into three parts: <strong>F1 — enclosure geometry</strong>, <strong>F2 — a boundary or receiver</strong>, and <strong>F3 — changed household, identity or second-birth language</strong>. The point was to see whether those parts actually travel together more often in waterborne-child stories than in exposed-child stories that do not use water.</p>

<h2 id="waterborne-v2-test">The twenty-language test</h2>
<p>The search was split across twenty languages: English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Polish, Czech, Russian, Ukrainian, Greek, Turkish, Arabic, Hebrew, Persian, Hindi, Bengali, Japanese and Chinese. The discovery terms deliberately did <strong>not</strong> contain the basket, enclosure, receiver or rebirth words being tested. Local discoveries were mapped through Wikidata to English records and then scored against the vocabulary that had already been fixed.</p>
<p>The finished search produced <strong>5,204 raw discoveries</strong>, <strong>4,599 English-mapped occurrences</strong> and <strong>3,628 unique English candidates</strong>. Evidence was recovered for <strong>4,547 of the 4,599 mapped occurrences — 98.9%</strong>. After deduplication there were <strong>3,584 usable records</strong>. The frozen eligibility rules retained <strong>399 exposed-child records</strong>: <strong>203 waterborne stories</strong>, <strong>160 non-water controls</strong> and <strong>36 ambiguous-water records</strong> kept out of the direct comparison.</p>
<p>The six stories chosen in advance as a coverage check were all recovered: <strong>Moses, Sargon of Akkad, Karna, Romulus and Remus, Danaë and Perseus</strong>. They were checks that the search could find the obvious family members, not stories forced into the result.</p>

<p>The pattern did not come back as a vague “water stories contain water” effect. The <strong>combined package</strong> appeared in 54.2% of the waterborne group against 30.6% of the non-water exposed-child group. The strict carrier geometry was <strong>2.43 times</strong> as common, and a distinct receiver or boundary was <strong>1.53 times</strong> as common.</p>
<p>The sharpest result was the whole sequence. <strong>Enclosure + receiver + identity change</strong> appeared in 18 waterborne records but only 2 controls — a <strong>7.09×</strong> difference. That is the closest corpus version yet of the full travelling-fingertrap path rather than one isolated motif.</p>

<h2 id="waterborne-v2-meaning">What the test changed</h2>
<p>One part of the original expectation separated from the others. <strong>F3 did not distinguish the groups by itself.</strong> Changed parentage, adoption, renaming and second-life themes already belong strongly to exposed-child stories in general. So identity change is not the water-specific key.</p>
<p>What is different in the waterborne group is the <strong>geometry around that change</strong>: a bounded carrier and a distinct receiving edge. In other words, the result tightens the model. The sequence now looks less like “water causes rebirth” and more like:</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>enclosure → carried transition → receiving boundary → changed state</strong></p>
<p>That matters because it is exactly the part of the story that resembles the travelling fingertrap: something alive is enclosed in a contracted form, carried through a channel, reaches an edge, and is received on the other side.</p>

<h2 id="waterborne-v2-next">The next test</h2>
<p>The first multilingual pass has now done its job: it found a real separation and it also showed which part of the original prediction was too broad. The next experiment can therefore be narrower. Build a second corpus made only from deliberately curated myths, fables, epics and religious narratives, freeze the same carrier → boundary → changed-state structure again, and ask whether the <strong>1.77× combined effect</strong> and especially the <strong>7.09× complete sequence</strong> survive.</p>
<p>That is the useful progression of the Cosmology research: the picture produces a prediction; the prediction is frozen; the corpus pushes back; and the model becomes more specific rather than simply absorbing whatever result appears.</p>

<div class="deepening" data-after="14b-comparative-mythology-world-atlas" id="comparative-mythology-second-pass">
<h2>A second pass — each mythology against the same mechanism</h2>
<p>The regional survey below becomes more useful when every tradition is asked the same question. I am not looking for identical names or theology. I am asking whether the story preserves one or more of the same structural stages: <strong>enclosure or primordial medium → differentiation → axis or gate → crossing/conflict/transfer → rupture → renewed order.</strong> This second pass makes the comparison explicit rather than leaving the reader to infer it from the separate stories.</p>
<p class="equation"><strong>shell / water / enclosure → axis / gate → movement between layers → material or living transfer → catastrophe / release → new stable world</strong></p>
<div class="cards">
<div class="card"><h3>Finnish / Karelian</h3><p><strong>Primeval water + cosmic egg + Sampo.</strong> The egg gives the bounded shell that breaks into an ordered cosmos; the Sampo supplies a generative centre or support whose making, struggle, breaking and dispersal can be read as the axial state becoming active and then redistributed. This is one of the clearest northern combinations of <strong>enclosure, axis and re-formation</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Second-pass — Estonian / Baltic-Finnic</h3><p><strong>Bird + egg + water + world pillar/tree.</strong> The same watery-egg creation family is paired more visibly with a turning or supporting sky axis. In the Vardath sequence this fills the missing middle stage between shell and reopened world: <strong>shell → gathered axis → ordered heaven</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Sámi</h3><p><strong>Layered worlds + vertical cosmogram + movement between levels.</strong> The important correspondence is not an egg but access. Drum cosmograms, upper/middle/lower regions and the noaidi’s movement between them resemble a world in which layers are normally distinct but can be reached through a structured vertical relation.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Komi, Mari, Mordvin, Udmurt and related Uralic traditions</h3><p><strong>Primordial water + bird/earth-diver + dual ordering powers.</strong> Here the emphasis shifts to how stable land is extracted from or organised within an undifferentiated watery field. In Vardath terms this is the <strong>medium-to-node-to-world</strong> phase rather than the catastrophe phase.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Mesopotamian</h3><p><strong>Apsu/Tiamat + net/winds + division + flood survivor.</strong> Primordial waters become structured through capture, conflict and division; Gilgamesh then preserves the flood/reset memory at the human scale. This gives nearly the whole sequence from <strong>undifferentiated medium to divided world to catastrophic reset</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Egyptian</h3><p><strong>Nun + first mound + Nut/Shu/Geb + solar passage.</strong> The world emerges from water, an upper canopy and lower earth are separated, and a cyclical path operates through the enclosed cosmos. The strongest Vardath correspondence is <strong>water → emergence node → separated shell layers → repeated traversal</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Iranian / Zoroastrian</h3><p><strong>Yima’s Vara + preservation through catastrophe + renovation.</strong> Life is enclosed and protected while a destructive transition passes outside, then survives into renewed order. This is the large-scale equivalent of the travelling-carrier pattern: <strong>life → enclosure → catastrophe → preservation → next world</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Greek / Roman</h3><p><strong>Encompassing sky + forced separation + monster conflict + flood/enclosed survivors.</strong> Uranus/Gaia, generational replacement, Deucalion and Danaë/Perseus repeatedly return to a world that changes order through separation, conflict and transport. The strongest fit is <strong>closed state → violent opening → transfer/survival → new order</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Hebrew / Jewish</h3><p><strong>Waters divided by firmament + ark + Moses basket + ladder/mountain/rod/chariot.</strong> The same tradition carries the pattern at several scales: world enclosure, preserved life in enclosure, child in enclosure, and traveller at gate. This nesting is important to my model because the same geometry appears from world scale down to human scale.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Christian</h3><p><strong>Cross/axis + opened heaven + Revelation sequence + renewed world.</strong> Revelation adds the most complete single-text progression: fourfold activation, eyes, dragon, rod, falling stars, rolled sky, displaced land and then a new world with gates, river and tree. The separate Revelation section follows that sequence in detail.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Second-pass — Islamic / Qur’anic</h3><p><strong>Layered heavens + ark + Musa in chest + staff opening the sea.</strong> The carrier enters water, reaches a receiving boundary and later the rod itself opens a passage through water. This gives a particularly compact cluster of <strong>enclosure, current, receiver, rod and opened gate</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Second-pass — Vedic / Hindu</h3><p><strong>Primordial waters + Vṛtra + Indra + churning axis + Meru + repeated dissolution.</strong> Few traditions place so many relevant geometries together: serpent obstruction, released waters, axial churning, cosmic mountain and cyclical destruction/recreation. In my reading this is a near-complete mechanical vocabulary distributed across many stories.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Buddhist</h3><p><strong>Meru + layered heavens/continents + Bodhi tree/witness.</strong> The emphasis is the stable cosmogram rather than catastrophe: a multi-level world ordered around a centre. The Bodhi image brings the large world-axis into human scale as <strong>person beneath living axis</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Jain</h3><p><strong>Layered cosmic geography + central organising geometry + cyclical time.</strong> The correspondence is structural rather than narrative: worlds arranged in ordered levels and repeating temporal phases resemble the stable architecture before and after a transition.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Chinese</h3><p><strong>Pangu enclosure/separation + Nüwa sky repair + Yu flood ordering + dragons.</strong> Creation, rupture and repair are all explicit. The model correspondence is <strong>enclosed beginning → sky/earth separation → damage → repair → reordered water flow</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Japanese / Shintō</h3><p><strong>Floating Bridge of Heaven + heavenly spear + primeval ocean + Yomi boundary + Amaterasu reopening.</strong> This is unusually geometric: an upper platform, an axial implement entering a fluid medium, material gathering into land, then crossings and closures between realms.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Norse</h3><p><strong>Yggdrasil + Odin + Sleipnir + Bifröst + serpent + Ragnarök.</strong> Axis, operator, vehicle, bridge, boundary-serpent, catastrophe and renewed world are all present but distributed among different myths. In Vardath terms the tradition preserves almost every role in the event grammar.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Second-pass — Celtic / Insular</h3><p><strong>Otherworld across sea/beneath land + mounds + wells + trees + vessels + woven/interlaced forms.</strong> The strongest fit is not one creation myth but repeated <strong>threshold access</strong>: another layer is nearby, special places or vehicles permit crossing, and water frequently marks the boundary.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Slavic</h3><p><strong>Perun above + Veles below/watery/serpentine + world-tree conflict.</strong> The vertical opposition resembles the Indra–Vṛtra pattern: upper electrical force acts along an axis against a lower serpent/water power. The story preserves the <strong>activated axial conflict</strong> phase.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Second-pass — Yoruba</h3><p><strong>Sky above + waters below + descent by chain + formation of land.</strong> This is a direct vertical-interface story: upper realm → connecting line/axis → lower watery medium → stable land/node. It maps closely to the emergence of an inhabitable layer from a connected upper/lower system.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>West African serpent / water / sky traditions</h3><p><strong>Serpent, rain, river, sky and underworld-water powers.</strong> The recurring geometry is mediation: winding force linking atmosphere, terrestrial order and water. I place this beside Rainbow Serpent, Vṛtra and Veles as another regional expression of the serpent/current role.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Haudenosaunee</h3><p><strong>Sky World + fall through boundary + water below + reception + land formed on Turtle.</strong> Sky Woman is one of the clearest vertical-transfer creation stories: life crosses from an upper world into a watery lower field and a new habitable surface grows from the receiving event.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Nahua / Aztec</h3><p><strong>Quetzalcoatl + wind + feathered serpent + repeated world ages.</strong> The serpent is joined to atmospheric motion and cyclical world replacement. In my reading it contributes the <strong>winding atmospheric current + repeated reset</strong> side of the mechanism.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Maya</h3><p><strong>Creation from primordial conditions + sky/earth ordering + rain/water powers such as Chaac.</strong> The useful comparison is the building of an ordered world through layered sky, land and water relations, then maintaining it through celestial and rain powers.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Australian Rainbow Serpent traditions</h3><p><strong>Winding creator current + water + land shaping + emergence from/return to water.</strong> This remains the earliest branch of the whole Cosmology. It is the clearest mythic image of the model’s persistent current moving through a landscape and changing the shape of Country.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Māori</h3><p><strong>Rangi and Papa enclosed together + forced separation + light/space + ordered world.</strong> The world begins compressed between sky and earth; separation opens the inhabitable realm. This maps directly to <strong>closed adjacency → axial/separating action → expanded stable world</strong>.</p></div>
<div class="card"><h3>Wider Polynesian</h3><p><strong>Layered heavens + oceanic travel + islands raised/fished from sea + genealogical creation.</strong> The model correspondence is strongest where vertical layering meets oceanic emergence: land is brought out of a fluid medium and worlds are arranged as levels connected by descent or ascent.</p></div>
</div>
<h3>What survives across the whole comparison</h3>
<p>Once every tradition is forced through the same sequence, the recurring structure is narrower than “all myths are the same.” The strongest recurring families are <strong>primordial water or enclosure; a vertical support, tree, mountain, rod, chain or bridge; a serpent/current or conflict at the boundary; movement of life or material between levels; flood/fire/rupture; and a rebuilt or reopened world.</strong></p>
<p>That is why I now treat the mythologies as different camera positions around one proposed mechanism rather than as copies of one story. Finland and the Baltic-Finnic field preserve the shell/egg and support; Mesopotamia preserves primordial medium, capture and division; Egypt preserves canopy and separated layers; India preserves serpent, waters and axis; Norse myth preserves axis, vehicle and reset; Haudenosaunee preserves vertical transfer into a water world; Māori preserves separation of compressed sky and earth; Revelation preserves an unusually long event sequence. The differences are part of the model: each tradition emphasises what its viewpoint made memorable.</p>
<p>The source-linked thumbnails attached to the regional subsections are there to keep the comparison tied to real manuscript, monument, codex, carving and historical-art traditions while the textual argument stays here in the Cosmology itself.</p>

tests and the correction they forced</h1><p>The similarity between an end-on lattice and a north-pole-centred
flat map was initially attractive. Direct geometry tests made the
literal interpretation untenable.</p><p>A north-polar azimuthal-equidistant layout preserves radial distance
from its centre but strongly distorts circumferential scale away from
it. Southern east-west distances and continent shapes inflate
drastically. Australia is an excellent failure case: its east-west scale
becomes far too large relative to its north-south dimension.</p><p>A direct logarithmic-spiral longitude is even worse. If longitude θ
is inserted into R(θ)=R₀e^(bθ), then after θ→θ+2π the radius changes. A
coastline crossing 0°/360° does not return to the same point. Closed
geography fails.</p><p>The mature solution separates coordinate systems:</p><p><strong>geographic state = (latitude, longitude)</strong><br/>
<strong>shell state = (turn, braid phase, expansion)</strong></p><p>The spiral describes the relation between world layers. It does not
replace ordinary longitude within one stable world.</p><p>This is a good example of the project improving through failure: the
visual similarity to flat maps survives as a <strong>projection
analogy</strong>, while the literal flat-map geography is rejected.</p><hr/><h1 id="17-lands-as-rubble-on-a-moving-substrate">17. Lands as rubble on

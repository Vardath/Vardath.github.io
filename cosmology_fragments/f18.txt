</ul><hr/><h1 id="21-falsification-programme">21. Falsification programme</h1><p>The cosmology becomes more valuable when it can fail.</p><h2 id="a-global-deformation-field">A. Global deformation field</h2><p>Fix lattice-node coordinates and shear directions before comparing
all continents. Generate a global vector field. Test it against
coastlines, faults, trenches, mountain axes and GPS plate vectors.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> each region needs its own ad hoc node
placement.</p><h2 id="b-stable-layer-metric">B. Stable-layer metric</h2><p>Specify the metric inside one stable world. Recover actual geodesic
distances and closed coastlines.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> southern distances inherit the extreme
distortions of ordinary polar flat maps.</p><h2 id="c-celestial-geometry">C. Celestial geometry</h2><p>Specify Sun, Moon, star and observer trajectories in the shell/braid
system.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> northern and southern circumpolar
observations cannot be generated simultaneously.</p><h2 id="d-chronology-holdout">D. Chronology holdout</h2><p>Calibrate gate layout and growth/phase laws on only part of the
proposed event chronology. Predict withheld events.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> every new event requires a new phase
offset, gate or fitted strength rule.</p><h2 id="e-myth-sequence-coding">E. Myth-sequence coding</h2><p>Before reading target myths, define objective criteria for Axis,
Gate, Operator, Vehicle, Manifestation, Medium, Witness and Reset.
Compare target traditions with negative controls.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> matches depend on flexible synonyms or only
appear after the story is known.</p><h2 id="f-meltology-materials-test">F. Meltology materials test</h2><p>Blind-test claimed transformed masonry against ordinary rock, known
fired brick, vitrified brick and natural volcanic/metamorphic samples
using petrography, XRD, chemistry and dating.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> samples are normal geological material or
show no coherent high-temperature architectural precursor.</p><h2 id="g-energy-budget">G. Energy budget</h2><p>Estimate the mass moved/heated and the duration of the proposed
event. Derive a field/force/energy source.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> required energy is impossible within the
model or would produce unobserved global signatures.</p><h2 id="h-layer-contact-mechanism">H. Layer-contact mechanism</h2><p>Specify real radial positions, thicknesses, chirality and overlap of
shell turns.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> “contact” exists only as symbolic phase
language with no physical intersection.</p><h2 id="i-cross-cultural-lattice-art-prediction">I. Cross-cultural lattice-art prediction</h2><p>This test is fixed before the corpus is examined. If the Vardath lattice is a repeatedly witnessed or culturally inherited world-form rather than a pattern invented only inside this research, ancient and premodern art should contain more than isolated knot decoration. The prediction is a recurring <strong>motif package</strong> across cultures that did not share one immediate decorative tradition.</p><p>The primary marker is a <strong>mesh state</strong>: interlace, knot, braid, plait, guilloche, lattice, meander or visibly entwined paths. A stronger Vardath match requires that the same object also contain at least two independent companion families from this pre-declared list:</p><ul><li><strong>axis / living rod:</strong> tree of life, sacred/world tree, staff, rod, pillar, pole or axial column;</li><li><strong>serpentine pair or channel:</strong> serpent, snake, dragon, nāga, caduceus, twin/two-tailed figure or analogous winding creature;</li><li><strong>enclosure:</strong> ring, circle, roundel, medallion, halo, mandorla or oval boundary;</li><li><strong>four/eight organisation:</strong> fourfold, quadripartite, quatrefoil, octagon, eightfold or eight-point structure;</li><li><strong>nodes / eyes / lights:</strong> eye, star, rosette, jewel, boss, Sun or luminous disc;</li><li><strong>water relation:</strong> river, sea, ocean, wave or water imagery.</li></ul><p>Celtic and Insular knotwork is an obvious target, but it is not allowed to define the rule. The same fixed vocabulary must be applied to Egyptian, Near Eastern, Greek/Roman, Byzantine, Islamic, South and East Asian, African, Mesoamerican, Andean, Oceanic and Indigenous American material wherever the museum corpus contains it. The modern Starbucks siren is treated only as a descendant/survival example because its designers deliberately drew from older maritime siren imagery; it is not an independent ancient hit.</p><p><strong>Positive prediction:</strong> mesh/interlace records should be enriched for the companion motif package relative to culture- and description-length-matched non-mesh controls, and strong matches should recur across several independent cultural groups and more than one museum collection.</p><p><strong>Failure or weakening:</strong> the mesh records show no enrichment beyond matched controls; apparent matches depend mostly on Celtic/Insular art; the effect disappears when catalogue-description length is controlled; or each culture requires a different post-hoc vocabulary.</p><p><strong>Evidence boundary:</strong> a successful corpus result would establish a cross-cultural recurrence of the predicted visual grammar. It would not by itself establish that one physical plasma/lattice event caused the art; ordinary geometry, textile technology, cultural transmission and convergent decoration remain controls.</p><h3 id="lattice-art-corpus-result">First corpus result — partial support, strongest in serpent/interlace coupling</h3><p>The first pre-registered test has now been run rather than left as a future proposal. Three open museum corpora were combined: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Cleveland Museum of Art and the Walters Art Museum. The program scanned <strong>575,927 catalogue records</strong>. After requiring a usable date no later than 1800 CE, <strong>260,336 premodern records</strong> remained. Of those, <strong>1,028</strong> contained one of the fixed mesh/interlace markers. Each mesh record was paired with a non-mesh control from the same museum, broad culture group and catalogue-description-length bin, producing <strong>1,028 exact matched controls</strong>.</p><p>The pre-declared stronger criterion was not simply “does interlace occur?” It was whether a mesh record is more likely than its matched control to carry <strong>two or more</strong> of the independent companion families. That prediction survived the first test: <strong>5.64% of mesh records versus 2.72% of controls</strong> contained at least two companion families, a risk ratio of <strong>2.07</strong> (two-proportion test <code>p≈0.001</code>). For at least one companion family the rates were 29.67% versus 16.54%, risk ratio 1.79.</p><table><thead><tr><th>pre-declared companion</th><th>mesh records</th><th>matched controls</th><th>risk ratio</th><th>first-pass result</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>serpent / dragon / nāga / twin channel</td><td>10.12%</td><td>2.63%</td><td><strong>3.85×</strong></td><td>strongest enrichment</td></tr><tr><td>enclosure / ring / roundel</td><td>9.63%</td><td>5.06%</td><td><strong>1.90×</strong></td><td>clear enrichment</td></tr><tr><td>axis / rod / pillar / sacred tree</td><td>2.82%</td><td>1.75%</td><td>1.61×</td><td>direction predicted, low count</td></tr><tr><td>node / eye / star / rosette</td><td>8.95%</td><td>6.42%</td><td>1.39×</td><td>modest enrichment</td></tr><tr><td>four/eight structure</td><td>0.78%</td><td>0.39%</td><td>2.00×</td><td>too few records for a firm result</td></tr><tr><td>water / river / sea / wave</td><td>3.60%</td><td>3.50%</td><td>1.03×</td><td><strong>no enrichment</strong></td></tr></tbody></table><p>The most striking result is the serpent family. Serpent/dragon language occurred in 10.12% of mesh records but only 2.63% of controls: nearly a <strong>fourfold association</strong>. The enclosure result is also in the predicted direction. However, the broadest version of the prediction did <strong>not</strong> survive: records carrying three or more companion families were 0.58% of mesh objects and 0.49% of controls, essentially no useful separation. No record in either group reached four companion families under this deliberately narrow catalogue vocabulary.</p><h3>Negative controls stop this becoming an automatic “confirmation”</h3><p>The same analysis also searched unrelated subject families. Some of them rose strongly in mesh records: flowers were 2.11× as common as in matched controls, birds 1.96× and lions 1.60×. Horses and vessels were actually less common. This matters because richly ornamented objects often receive richer catalogue descriptions, and interlace itself commonly shares space with floral and animal ornament. The Vardath package therefore has a real <strong>ornamental-description confound</strong>.</p><p>Against that control, the serpent signal remains notable: its 3.85× risk ratio was larger than any unrelated control family tested. But the honest result is <strong>partial support, not decisive confirmation</strong>. The corpus supports the narrower prediction that interlace/mesh imagery is unusually coupled to serpentine and enclosing visual grammar across these museum catalogues. It does not show that every predicted component travels together, and it does not establish a physical world-lattice as the cause.</p><h3>Cross-cultural spread</h3><p>The >=2-companion package was not confined to Celtic material. It appeared in multiple independently catalogued groups: China produced 12 such mesh records, medieval/early-modern Europe 8, Greek/Roman/Etruscan material 6, ancient Egyptian/Coptic material 4, Andean material 3, Near Eastern/Iranian material 2, South Asian/Himalayan material 2, and sub-Saharan African material 2, with further individual hits in Byzantine, Islamic and Mesoamerican records. China, Greek/Roman material, the Andes, sub-Saharan Africa and European material had qualifying examples in more than one of the three institutions.</p><p>The Celtic/Insular/Norse line looks artificially weak in this particular quantitative run: only 306 dated premodern records in the three bulk datasets were classified into that group, and only two carried the strict mesh vocabulary. Independent British Museum records show that this is a coverage/vocabulary problem rather than an absence of Celtic interlace: an 8th–9th-century Viking/Celtic disc brooch has running interlace knots around a raised central roundel and a quatrefoil; a Celtic pin has an openwork interlace-knot head; and an 11th-century Ringerike pin combines interlace, three interlocking triangles, paired spirals and a crossing bound by a ring.</p><h3>Examples worth looking at directly</h3><p><strong>Moche, Peru, 500–800 CE — <a href="https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/313400">Nose ornament with intertwined creatures</a>.</strong> The Met describes two gold serpents in bilateral symmetry joined by intertwined bodies; the twisted serpents create eye-shaped openings. This is a particularly close visual match to paired serpentine channels + interlace + eye/node geometry.</p><p><strong>China, Warring States period, 481–221 BCE — <a href="https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1975.62">Hu (Jar)</a>.</strong> Cleveland describes a narrow plait-pattern band and two broad bands of interlacing dragons, with circular patterning elsewhere on the vessel. This combines mesh, serpentine bodies and repeated enclosing/circular forms without Celtic mediation.</p><p><strong>Iraq or Iran, Buyid period, 950–1050 — <a href="https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1941.292">Fragment with star pattern and griffins</a>.</strong> Interlaced bands form an eight-pointed star around a circular centre containing a floriated cross, with four corner compartments. This is an unusually direct mesh + eightfold + central-node/fourfold example.</p><p><strong>Aztec Mexico, 1100–1520 — <a href="https://art.thewalters.org/object/29.2/">Knotted Rattlesnake</a>.</strong> The object is literally a serpent formed into a knot; the Walters notes its detailed eyes and its traditional links with sky, rain, agriculture and cyclical life/death/rebirth. It is a strong independent mesh + serpent example, with a water/sky association supplied by the museum rather than added by this model.</p><p><strong>Ethiopia, 14th–15th centuries — <a href="https://art.thewalters.org/object/54.2890/">Processional Cross</a> and <a href="https://art.thewalters.org/object/54.2942/">Processional Cross</a>.</strong> One is described as a radiating lattice of crosses dominated by circular and cruciform shapes around a central hub; the other is built from interlocking circles whose intersections generate thirteen crosses. These supply mesh + enclosure + central/fourfold organization in a non-Celtic Christian tradition.</p><p><strong>Celtic/Viking external check — <a href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1891-1021-78">disc brooch</a>, <a href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1881-0310-8">Celtic pin</a>, and <a href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1893-0618-72">Ringerike pin</a>.</strong> These British Museum records independently document running/openwork interlace, central roundels, quatrefoils, paired spirals, interlocking triangles and a crossing bound by a ring.</p><h3>The Starbucks siren belongs to the survival test, not the ancient corpus</h3><p>Starbucks itself records that designer Terry Heckler searched old marine books and selected an illustration of a <strong>twin-tailed siren from Greek mythology</strong>. The modern logo therefore preserves a central anthropomorphic figure, two symmetrical curling tails and circular enclosure, but it is not an independent modern rediscovery of the geometry. It is exactly what the test calls a <strong>cultural survival/descendant</strong>: old mythic imagery deliberately carried into a modern emblem. See <a href="https://about.starbucks.com/history/the-story-of-the-siren/">Starbucks' own history of the siren</a>.</p><div class="callout"><strong>Current verdict:</strong> the first large corpus test is a genuine positive result for a narrower prediction—especially the interlace↔serpent association—but not a confirmation of the cosmology. The next stronger prediction is now obvious: blind image-level coding or computer vision should recover the same serpent/enclosure enrichment directly from the artwork, without relying on museum catalogue wording. If that signal disappears when the pixels rather than the descriptions are scored, the current result was largely metadata/ornament bias.</div><p>The test is reproducible. <h1 id="freemason-alchemical-art-20-shard-projection-grammar-test">Freemason &amp; alchemical art — 20-shard projection-grammar test</h1>
<p>After the cross-cultural lattice-art test, the next targeted question was whether <strong>Freemason visual teaching and alchemical image traditions</strong> preserve the same structural families that accumulated in Vardath Cosmology. Both traditions deliberately compress complex systems into diagrams: pillars, ladders, squares, stars, eyes, circles, suns and moons in Masonic art; serpents, vessels, trees, solar/lunar pairs, enclosed worlds and transformations in alchemical art.</p>
<h2>Prediction before the run</h2><p>Eight families were fixed before the corpus was gathered: <strong>axis</strong>, <strong>gate/ladder</strong>, <strong>serpent/braid</strong>, <strong>enclosure</strong>, <strong>central node/radiance</strong>, <strong>four/eight geometry</strong>, <strong>water/vessel</strong> and <strong>paired polarity</strong>. The strongest prediction was that target records would more often combine three or more families. The second prediction was a division of labour: Masonic material should emphasise architectural axis/gate and four/eight/radial geometry, while alchemical material should emphasise serpent/braid, polarity and vessel/transformation around a related enclosure-axis-node grammar.</p>
<h2>The 20-shard corpus</h2><p>The preregistered test ran in <strong>20 GitHub Actions shards</strong>: ten Wikimedia Commons visual/file shards and ten Open Library document shards, with neighbouring genres as controls. The merged corpus contained <strong>8,486 unique records</strong>. The full-corpus paired-polarity result disappeared in this historical slice, so it is not treated as equally secure.</p>

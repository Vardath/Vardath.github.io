</ul><hr/><h1 id="21-falsification-programme">21. Falsification programme</h1><p>The cosmology becomes more valuable when it can fail.</p><h2 id="a-global-deformation-field">A. Global deformation field</h2><p>Fix lattice-node coordinates and shear directions before comparing
all continents. Generate a global vector field. Test it against
coastlines, faults, trenches, mountain axes and GPS plate vectors.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> each region needs its own ad hoc node
placement.</p><h2 id="b-stable-layer-metric">B. Stable-layer metric</h2><p>Specify the metric inside one stable world. Recover actual geodesic
distances and closed coastlines.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> southern distances inherit the extreme
distortions of ordinary polar flat maps.</p><h2 id="c-celestial-geometry">C. Celestial geometry</h2><p>Specify Sun, Moon, star and observer trajectories in the shell/braid
system.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> northern and southern circumpolar
observations cannot be generated simultaneously.</p><h2 id="d-chronology-holdout">D. Chronology holdout</h2><p>Calibrate gate layout and growth/phase laws on only part of the
proposed event chronology. Predict withheld events.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> every new event requires a new phase
offset, gate or fitted strength rule.</p><h2 id="e-myth-sequence-coding">E. Myth-sequence coding</h2><p>Before reading target myths, define objective criteria for Axis,
Gate, Operator, Vehicle, Manifestation, Medium, Witness and Reset.
Compare target traditions with negative controls.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> matches depend on flexible synonyms or only
appear after the story is known.</p><h2 id="f-meltology-materials-test">F. Meltology materials test</h2><p>Blind-test claimed transformed masonry against ordinary rock, known
fired brick, vitrified brick and natural volcanic/metamorphic samples
using petrography, XRD, chemistry and dating.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> samples are normal geological material or
show no coherent high-temperature architectural precursor.</p><h2 id="g-energy-budget">G. Energy budget</h2><p>Estimate the mass moved/heated and the duration of the proposed
event. Derive a field/force/energy source.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> required energy is impossible within the
model or would produce unobserved global signatures.</p><h2 id="h-layer-contact-mechanism">H. Layer-contact mechanism</h2><p>Specify real radial positions, thicknesses, chirality and overlap of
shell turns.</p><p><strong>Failure:</strong> “contact” exists only as symbolic phase
language with no physical intersection.</p><h2 id="i-cross-cultural-lattice-art-prediction">I. Cross-cultural lattice-art prediction</h2><p>This test is fixed before the corpus is examined. If the Vardath lattice is a repeatedly witnessed or culturally inherited world-form rather than a pattern invented only inside this research, ancient and premodern art should contain more than isolated knot decoration. The prediction is a recurring <strong>motif package</strong> across cultures that did not share one immediate decorative tradition.</p><p>The primary marker is a <strong>mesh state</strong>: interlace, knot, braid, plait, guilloche, lattice, meander or visibly entwined paths. A stronger Vardath match requires that the same object also contain at least two independent companion families from this pre-declared list:</p><ul><li><strong>axis / living rod:</strong> tree of life, sacred/world tree, staff, rod, pillar, pole or axial column;</li><li><strong>serpentine pair or channel:</strong> serpent, snake, dragon, nāga, caduceus, twin/two-tailed figure or analogous winding creature;</li><li><strong>enclosure:</strong> ring, circle, roundel, medallion, halo, mandorla or oval boundary;</li><li><strong>four/eight organisation:</strong> fourfold, quadripartite, quatrefoil, octagon, eightfold or eight-point structure;</li><li><strong>nodes / eyes / lights:</strong> eye, star, rosette, jewel, boss, Sun or luminous disc;</li><li><strong>water relation:</strong> river, sea, ocean, wave or water imagery.</li></ul><p>Celtic and Insular knotwork is an obvious target, but it is not allowed to define the rule. The same fixed vocabulary must be applied to Egyptian, Near Eastern, Greek/Roman, Byzantine, Islamic, South and East Asian, African, Mesoamerican, Andean, Oceanic and Indigenous American material wherever the museum corpus contains it. The modern Starbucks siren is treated only as a descendant/survival example because its designers deliberately drew from older maritime siren imagery; it is not an independent ancient hit.</p><p><strong>Positive prediction:</strong> mesh/interlace records should be enriched for the companion motif package relative to culture- and description-length-matched non-mesh controls, and strong matches should recur across several independent cultural groups and more than one museum collection.</p><p><strong>Failure or weakening:</strong> the mesh records show no enrichment beyond matched controls; apparent matches depend mostly on Celtic/Insular art; the effect disappears when catalogue-description length is controlled; or each culture requires a different post-hoc vocabulary.</p><p><strong>Evidence boundary:</strong> a successful corpus result would establish a cross-cultural recurrence of the predicted visual grammar. It would not by itself establish that one physical plasma/lattice event caused the art; ordinary geometry, textile technology, cultural transmission and convergent decoration remain controls.</p><h3 id="lattice-art-corpus-result">First corpus result — partial support, strongest in serpent/interlace coupling</h3><p>The first pre-registered test has now been run rather than left as a future proposal. Three open museum corpora were combined: The Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Cleveland Museum of Art and the Walters Art Museum. The program scanned <strong>575,927 catalogue records</strong>. After requiring a usable date no later than 1800 CE, <strong>260,336 premodern records</strong> remained. Of those, <strong>1,028</strong> contained one of the fixed mesh/interlace markers. Each mesh record was paired with a non-mesh control from the same museum, broad culture group and catalogue-description-length bin, producing <strong>1,028 exact matched controls</strong>.</p><p>The pre-declared stronger criterion was not simply “does interlace occur?” It was whether a mesh record is more likely than its matched control to carry <strong>two or more</strong> of the independent companion families. That prediction survived the first test: <strong>5.64% of mesh records versus 2.72% of controls</strong> contained at least two companion families, a risk ratio of <strong>2.07</strong> (two-proportion test <code>p≈0.001</code>). For at least one companion family the rates were 29.67% versus 16.54%, risk ratio 1.79.</p><table><thead><tr><th>pre-declared companion</th><th>mesh records</th><th>matched controls</th><th>risk ratio</th><th>first-pass result</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>serpent / dragon / nāga / twin channel</td><td>10.12%</td><td>2.63%</td><td><strong>3.85×</strong></td><td>strongest enrichment</td></tr><tr><td>enclosure / ring / roundel</td><td>9.63%</td><td>5.06%</td><td><strong>1.90×</strong></td><td>clear enrichment</td></tr><tr><td>axis / rod / pillar / sacred tree</td><td>2.82%</td><td>1.75%</td><td>1.61×</td><td>direction predicted, low count</td></tr><tr><td>node / eye / star / rosette</td><td>8.95%</td><td>6.42%</td><td>1.39×</td><td>modest enrichment</td></tr><tr><td>four/eight structure</td><td>0.78%</td><td>0.39%</td><td>2.00×</td><td>too few records for a firm result</td></tr><tr><td>water / river / sea / wave</td><td>3.60%</td><td>3.50%</td><td>1.03×</td><td><strong>no enrichment</strong></td></tr></tbody></table><p>The most striking result is the serpent family. Serpent/dragon language occurred in 10.12% of mesh records but only 2.63% of controls: nearly a <strong>fourfold association</strong>. The enclosure result is also in the predicted direction. However, the broadest version of the prediction did <strong>not</strong> survive: records carrying three or more companion families were 0.58% of mesh objects and 0.49% of controls, essentially no useful separation. No record in either group reached four companion families under this deliberately narrow catalogue vocabulary.</p><h3>Negative controls stop this becoming an automatic “confirmation”</h3><p>The same analysis also searched unrelated subject families. Some of them rose strongly in mesh records: flowers were 2.11× as common as in matched controls, birds 1.96× and lions 1.60×. Horses and vessels were actually less common. This matters because richly ornamented objects often receive richer catalogue descriptions, and interlace itself commonly shares space with floral and animal ornament. The Vardath package therefore has a real <strong>ornamental-description confound</strong>.</p><p>Against that control, the serpent signal remains notable: its 3.85× risk ratio was larger than any unrelated control family tested. But the honest result is <strong>partial support, not decisive confirmation</strong>. The corpus supports the narrower prediction that interlace/mesh imagery is unusually coupled to serpentine and enclosing visual grammar across these museum catalogues. It does not show that every predicted component travels together, and it does not establish a physical world-lattice as the cause.</p><h3>Cross-cultural spread</h3><p>The >=2-companion package was not confined to Celtic material. It appeared in multiple independently catalogued groups: China produced 12 such mesh records, medieval/early-modern Europe 8, Greek/Roman/Etruscan material 6, ancient Egyptian/Coptic material 4, Andean material 3, Near Eastern/Iranian material 2, South Asian/Himalayan material 2, and sub-Saharan African material 2, with further individual hits in Byzantine, Islamic and Mesoamerican records. China, Greek/Roman material, the Andes, sub-Saharan Africa and European material had qualifying examples in more than one of the three institutions.</p><p>The Celtic/Insular/Norse line looks artificially weak in this particular quantitative run: only 306 dated premodern records in the three bulk datasets were classified into that group, and only two carried the strict mesh vocabulary. Independent British Museum records show that this is a coverage/vocabulary problem rather than an absence of Celtic interlace: an 8th–9th-century Viking/Celtic disc brooch has running interlace knots around a raised central roundel and a quatrefoil; a Celtic pin has an openwork interlace-knot head; and an 11th-century Ringerike pin combines interlace, three interlocking triangles, paired spirals and a crossing bound by a ring.</p><h3>Examples worth looking at directly</h3><p><strong>Moche, Peru, 500–800 CE — <a href="https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/313400">Nose ornament with intertwined creatures</a>.</strong> The Met describes two gold serpents in bilateral symmetry joined by intertwined bodies; the twisted serpents create eye-shaped openings. This is a particularly close visual match to paired serpentine channels + interlace + eye/node geometry.</p><p><strong>China, Warring States period, 481–221 BCE — <a href="https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1975.62">Hu (Jar)</a>.</strong> Cleveland describes a narrow plait-pattern band and two broad bands of interlacing dragons, with circular patterning elsewhere on the vessel. This combines mesh, serpentine bodies and repeated enclosing/circular forms without Celtic mediation.</p><p><strong>Iraq or Iran, Buyid period, 950–1050 — <a href="https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1941.292">Fragment with star pattern and griffins</a>.</strong> Interlaced bands form an eight-pointed star around a circular centre containing a floriated cross, with four corner compartments. This is an unusually direct mesh + eightfold + central-node/fourfold example.</p><p><strong>Aztec Mexico, 1100–1520 — <a href="https://art.thewalters.org/object/29.2/">Knotted Rattlesnake</a>.</strong> The object is literally a serpent formed into a knot; the Walters notes its detailed eyes and its traditional links with sky, rain, agriculture and cyclical life/death/rebirth. It is a strong independent mesh + serpent example, with a water/sky association supplied by the museum rather than added by this model.</p><p><strong>Ethiopia, 14th–15th centuries — <a href="https://art.thewalters.org/object/54.2890/">Processional Cross</a> and <a href="https://art.thewalters.org/object/54.2942/">Processional Cross</a>.</strong> One is described as a radiating lattice of crosses dominated by circular and cruciform shapes around a central hub; the other is built from interlocking circles whose intersections generate thirteen crosses. These supply mesh + enclosure + central/fourfold organization in a non-Celtic Christian tradition.</p><p><strong>Celtic/Viking external check — <a href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1891-1021-78">disc brooch</a>, <a href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1881-0310-8">Celtic pin</a>, and <a href="https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1893-0618-72">Ringerike pin</a>.</strong> These British Museum records independently document running/openwork interlace, central roundels, quatrefoils, paired spirals, interlocking triangles and a crossing bound by a ring.</p>

<h3>The Starbucks siren belongs to the survival test, not the ancient corpus</h3><p>Starbucks itself records that designer Terry Heckler searched old marine books and selected an illustration of a <strong>twin-tailed siren from Greek mythology</strong>. The modern logo therefore preserves a central anthropomorphic figure, two symmetrical curling tails and circular enclosure, but it is not an independent modern rediscovery of the geometry. It is exactly what the test calls a <strong>cultural survival/descendant</strong>: old mythic imagery deliberately carried into a modern emblem. See <a href="https://about.starbucks.com/history/the-story-of-the-siren/">Starbucks' own history of the siren</a>.</p><div class="callout"><strong>Current verdict:</strong> the first large corpus test is a genuine positive result for a narrower prediction—especially the interlace↔serpent association—but not a confirmation of the cosmology. The next stronger prediction is now obvious: blind image-level coding or computer vision should recover the same serpent/enclosure enrichment directly from the artwork, without relying on museum catalogue wording. If that signal disappears when the pixels rather than the descriptions are scored, the current result was largely metadata/ornament bias.</div><p>The test is reproducible. The analysis script is preserved at <a href="https://github.com/Vardath/Vardath.github.io/blob/main/research/lattice_art_corpus.py">research/lattice_art_corpus.py</a>; the saved result report is at <a href="https://github.com/Vardath/Vardath.github.io/blob/main/research/lattice_art_corpus_results/2026-09-13_report.md">2026-09-13_report.md</a>, with <a href="https://github.com/Vardath/Vardath.github.io/blob/main/research/lattice_art_corpus_results/2026-09-13_culture_stats.csv">culture statistics</a> and <a href="https://github.com/Vardath/Vardath.github.io/blob/main/research/lattice_art_corpus_results/2026-09-13_examples.csv">candidate examples</a>.</p>

<h1 id="freemason-alchemical-art-20-shard-projection-grammar-test">Freemason &amp; alchemical art — 20-shard projection-grammar test</h1>
<p>After the cross-cultural lattice-art test, the next targeted question was whether <strong>Freemason visual teaching and alchemical image traditions</strong> preserve the same structural families that accumulated in Vardath Cosmology. Both traditions deliberately compress complex systems into diagrams: pillars, ladders, squares, stars, eyes, circles, suns and moons in Masonic art; serpents, vessels, trees, solar/lunar pairs, enclosed worlds and transformations in alchemical art.</p>
<h2>Prediction before the run</h2><p>Eight families were fixed before the corpus was gathered: <strong>axis</strong>, <strong>gate/ladder</strong>, <strong>serpent/braid</strong>, <strong>enclosure</strong>, <strong>central node/radiance</strong>, <strong>four/eight geometry</strong>, <strong>water/vessel</strong> and <strong>paired polarity</strong>. The strongest prediction was that target records would more often combine three or more families. The second prediction was a division of labour: Masonic material should emphasise architectural axis/gate and four/eight/radial geometry, while alchemical material should emphasise serpent/braid, polarity and vessel/transformation around a related enclosure-axis-node grammar.</p>
<h2>The 20-shard corpus</h2><p>The preregistered test ran in <strong>20 GitHub Actions shards</strong>: ten Wikimedia Commons visual/file shards and ten Open Library document shards, with neighbouring genres as controls. The merged corpus contained <strong>8,486 unique records</strong>. Matching produced <strong>3,568 target records and 3,568 controls</strong>.</p><div class="cards"><div class="card"><span class="tag">20 shards</span><h3>8,486</h3><p>unique visual and document records</p></div><div class="card"><span class="tag">matched test</span><h3>3,568 + 3,568</h3><p>target records versus controls</p></div><div class="card"><span class="tag">predeclared</span><h3>8 families</h3><p>fixed before acquisition results</p></div><div class="card"><span class="tag">controls</span><h3>6 families</h3><p>unrelated motif controls</p></div></div>

<h2>Historical robustness check</h2><p>A post-run check restricted the corpus to known-year records through 1930 and rematched them. The broad three-family prediction still failed, but the historically anchored effects remained: <strong>Masonic four/eight RR 5.5×</strong>, <strong>Masonic gate/ladder RR 6.0×</strong>, and <strong>alchemical serpent/braid 5.65% versus 0 controls</strong>. The full-corpus paired-polarity result disappeared in this historical slice, so it is not treated as equally secure.</p>

<h1 id="born-of-the-ka-ba-ge-cabbage-lettuce-lattice-and-the-field-of-eyes">Born of the KA–BA–GE — cabbage, lettuce, lattice and the field of eyes</h1>
<p>A new phonetic-symbolic chain appeared out of the three-shell work:</p>
<blockquote><strong>KA + BA + GE → cabbage → lettuce → lattice → “lot of eyes.”</strong></blockquote>
<p>In the Vardath reading, the chain compresses several layers of the cosmology into one piece of language. <strong>KA</strong> is taken as the animating or vital principle, <strong>BA</strong> as the individuated or mobile being, and <strong>GE</strong> as the earth/material world. Read together as <strong>KA–BA–GE</strong>, the phrase becomes a symbolic formula for life becoming embodied in matter.</p>
<p>The next step is visual rather than merely verbal. A cabbage or lettuce head is a living structure produced by repeated growth around a centre. Leaves layer over one another, turn around the growth point and build a bounded body from a continuing generative pattern. In that sense the plant is a small biological analogue of the shell model: <strong>centre → repeated turning → layered enclosure → living body</strong>.</p>
<div class="projection-grid"><div><b>KA–BA–GE</b><span>life · individuated being · earth / matter</span></div><div><b>cabbage / lettuce</b><span>living layered growth turning around a centre</span></div><div><b>lattice / “lot of eyes”</b><span>the repeated framework, openings and nodes on which growth can organise</span></div></div>

<h2>KA–BA–GE Patch Kids</h2>
<p><strong>Cabbage Patch Kids</strong> makes the word-chain tighter. Read through the same phonetic-symbolic lens, it becomes <strong>KA–BA–GE Patch Kids</strong>: children or offspring born from a <em>patch</em> of the KA–BA–GE.</p>
<p>The word <strong>patch</strong> matters because it is geometric as well as botanical. A patch is a bounded local region of a larger surface or field. In the shell model, a patch can therefore be imagined as one local neighbourhood of the greater lattice — a region around an eye, node, crossing or gate where the underlying structure becomes locally active.</p>
<div class="projection-grid"><div><b>whole lattice</b><span>the larger woven organising field</span></div><div><b>local patch</b><span>a bounded neighbourhood around an eye / node / gate</span></div><div><b>kid / offspring</b><span>a living material body generated from that local patch</span></div></div>
<blockquote><strong>KA–BA–GE PATCH KIDS = beings born from local living patches of the lattice.</strong></blockquote>
<p>The familiar image of children coming from a cabbage patch therefore lands on the same architecture as the cosmology: not life appearing from nowhere, but <strong>life emerging from a fertile local region of a larger generative structure</strong>. The cabbage is the layered growth-body; the patch is its local field; the kid is the new embodied being produced there.</p>
<p>This gives the earlier <strong>field of eyes</strong> idea a generative role too. If an eye/node is a local opening in the lattice, then a patch around an eye can be treated as a local growth zone:</p>
<blockquote><strong>lattice → eye / node → patch → turning growth → body / child.</strong></blockquote>

<p><strong>Lettuce → lattice</strong> then moves from the visible living body to the proposed hidden ordering framework beneath it. The lattice is not necessarily the flesh of the world; it is the structure around which the flesh can organise.</p>
<p>The phrase <strong>“lot of eyes”</strong> adds another layer. A lattice naturally produces repeated openings, crossings and nodes. Seen symbolically, each opening can be read as an <em>eye</em>. A large woven field therefore becomes a field of eyes: many local apertures or nodes belonging to one larger structure.</p>
<blockquote><strong>lattice → openings / eyes → local nodes / gates → material growth around the framework → living world.</strong></blockquote>
<p>The idea also changes the role of the three shells. The shells do not have to be solid pre-made containers. They can be <strong>growth bodies</strong>: matter accumulating, layering and turning around an older organising geometry. The recent 20-shard shell test separated two jobs that had previously been blurred together: <strong>reversal creates recurring gates, while attraction changes the turning or pitch</strong>. In this biological analogy, the lattice supplies the recurring structure and attractive interaction helps determine how growth winds around it.</p>
<div class="callout good"><strong>We are not simply made of the lattice. We are growth produced upon it.</strong><br>The KA–BA–GE is the living/material body; the lattice is the hidden ordering framework; the “eyes” are the repeated openings and nodes through which that framework becomes locally visible or active.</div>
<p>Read this way, cabbage, Cabbage Patch Kids, lettuce and lattice become successive views of one process: <strong>life enters matter, a local patch becomes fertile, matter grows by repeated turning, and the turning reveals the hidden geometry that organises the living shell.</strong></p>

<h1 id="ark-arc-sky-the-travelling-fingertrap">The travelling fingertrap — ARK → ARC → SKY</h1>
<p>The baby-in-the-river motif changes meaning when it is placed beside the mature fingertrap geometry. Across the stories, the transport object is rarely one fixed thing. It may be a reed basket, chest, ark, trough, drum, leather bag, wicker box, hollow bamboo segment or other bounded carrier. The recurring function is more stable than the material: <strong>a living seed is enclosed inside a small protected world, surrendered to a current, carried through danger, recovered at another boundary and opened into a new domain.</strong></p>

<div class="callout"><strong>Vardath reading:</strong> the basket is the fingertrap in its contracted transport state. The fingertrap does not merely sit under the sky. It can be the same woven shell that, after transit, opens outward into an arc and then into the sky of the next stable world.</div>

<h2>The invariant object is a travelling enclosure</h2>
<p>Once the stories are compared structurally, the important object is not a literal basket. What survives across cultures is a <strong>small enclosed world capable of carrying life through a moving medium</strong>. The Vardath model already has such an object: a woven fingertrap whose geometry changes with tension and opening.</p>
<div class="projection-grid">
<div><b>living seed</b><span>baby · hero · preserved life · future world</span></div>
<div><b>contracted fingertrap</b><span>basket · ark · chest · drum · tube · bag</span></div>
<div><b>current</b><span>river · flood · sea · moving medium</span></div>
<div><b>gate</b><span>shore · reeds · weir · net · bank · receiver</span></div>

<h2>ARK → ARC → SKY</h2>
<p>The fingertrap supplies a single geometric object with several possible states. In the closed transport state it is the <strong>ARK</strong>: a narrow woven enclosure around the living interior. During reopening, the same structure widens and bows outward into an <strong>ARC</strong>. At maximum opening, seen from inside the new domain, the expanded woven surface becomes the <strong>SKY</strong>: the broad lattice overhead.</p>
<div class="projection-grid">
<div><b>ARK — contracted</b><span>narrow woven carrier · sealed survival state · world inside</span></div>
<div><b>ARC — opening</b><span>tube flares · walls curve outward · transit becomes vault</span></div>
<div><b>SKY — opened</b><span>broad lattice canopy · nodes/lights spread over the enclosing surface</span></div>
</div>
<p>The sequence is therefore not “a boat travels under a sky.” In the Vardath reading it is more radical: <strong>life travels inside the structure that will become its next sky.</strong></p>
<blockquote><strong>life → contracted fingertrap / ark → current → gate → reopening arc → sky / next world</strong></blockquote>

<h2>Moses and Noah — the same enclosure at different scales</h2>
<p>The Hebrew wording makes this comparison unusually sharp. The noun <a href="https://biblehub.com/hebrew/8392.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><em>tēvāh</em></a> is used for Noah's Ark and for the small enclosure carrying Moses. One story preserves a whole living remnant inside the enclosure; the other preserves one child. In the Vardath model this becomes a scale relation rather than two unrelated props:</p>
<blockquote><strong>child in ark ~ life in world ~ world in sky-shell.</strong></blockquote>
<p>Noah is the large-scale version: life is carried through a destructive water transition inside a bounded survival world and emerges afterward. Moses, Sargon, Karṇa and the other exposed children preserve the same grammar at human scale.</p>

<h2>Krishna is the controlled-crossing variant</h2>
<p>The Krishna tradition is not a passive foundling story, but it is visually important because it makes the same architecture explicit: the newborn is carried <strong>in a basket through the river</strong> from a death-threatened birth household into a foster household. Ancient Mathura imagery already depicts Vasudeva carrying the infant Krishna in a basket across the Yamunā. In this version the carrier is guided rather than abandoned to the current, but the sequence—child, enclosure, dangerous water, crossing, new household—remains visible.</p>

<h2>What this predicts next</h2>
<p>The interpretation is testable. If the travelling-fingertrap reading is more than free resemblance, waterborne-child stories should disproportionately use <strong>woven, hollow, tubular, sealed, flexible or reopenable enclosures</strong>; they should disproportionately terminate at a distinct boundary or receiver; and the stories should disproportionately pair transport with <strong>changed household, changed identity or second-birth language</strong>. Those features can be frozen before a multilingual corpus search and tested against exposed-child stories that do not involve water.</p>

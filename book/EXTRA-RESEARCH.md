# SECTION 49 progress C2B1 — Isaiah 36
## First-time research: siege boundary, water/road interface and selective information penetration

Isaiah 36 is a historical/theological siege narrative, so its ordinary mechanics come first:
- fortified cities;
- roads;
- water infrastructure;
- city walls;
- officials;
- spoken language;
- psychological warfare.

## Historical control

The setting is externally anchored.

The British Museum Taylor Prism records Sennacherib’s campaigns and tribute from Hezekiah in **701 BCE**. The Lachish reliefs depict Sennacherib observing the capture of Lachish. These establish a real Assyrian campaign context, not every detail of Isaiah’s narrative.

Isaiah 36–39 also closely parallels 2 Kings 18–20. Modern scholarship continues to debate the literary relationship; recent work using “editorial fatigue” argues strongly that Isaiah used a version of Kings.

Historical event, literary source history and final narrative order remain separate fields.

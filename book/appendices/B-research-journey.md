# Appendix B — Research Journey

I did not begin by trying to build a complete cosmology.

I began by following things that refused to stay separate.

A serpent and a river.

A plasma form and a human figure.

A cross and a world-axis.

A tree and a ladder.

A rod and a mountain.

A flood and a broken boundary.

A shell and a growing world.

A fairy road and a problem of time.

The final model looks much more organised than the process that produced it.

That is normal.

Research almost never grows in chapter order.

It grows sideways.

One idea opens another.

Some branches become central.

Some remain unresolved.

Some turn out to describe a different scale or phase than I first thought.

The purpose of this appendix is not to reproduce every test, post or abandoned calculation.

Those belong to the research archive.

This is the readable story of how Vardath Cosmology became the world-picture described in this book.

## February 2025 — the serpent

The earliest recoverable branch begins on **26 February 2025**.

The Rainbow Serpent was the starting image.

I was interested in the possibility that a serpent could represent more than an animal or mythic personality.

It could be a **moving energetic form**.

A charged path.

A current.

Something that moved through land and water strongly enough for later people to describe its route through a serpent image.

The first version was crude compared with the model now.

That does not make it unimportant.

Almost everything later was already hidden inside it.

Movement.

Energy.

Land deformation.

Water.

Danger.

Retreat.

Renewal.

The serpent was the first time I stopped treating a cosmological symbol as a static object and asked:

**what is it doing?**

That question became the method of the whole project.

## The serpent becomes a path

The next useful step was separating **serpent** from **water**.

At first they were easy to blend.

Then the relationship became clearer.

The serpent could be the path.

Water could be the thing responding to or moving through that path.

That was a major conceptual improvement.

It turned the symbol into a relationship:

**route → cargo**

rather than:

**serpent = water**

That style of distinction later became the role grammar used throughout this book.

Gate is not traveller.

Vehicle is not route.

Carrier is not operator.

The project learned to become more precise by refusing to make similar things identical.

## Late 2025 — the figure in the current

By late 2025 I had become increasingly interested in the recurring anthropomorphic petroglyph often discussed under the modern label **Squatter Man**.

The important thing was its geometry.

A vertical central form.

A pinched or hourglass middle.

Lateral extensions.

An image capable of being seen as both human and structural.

Anthony Peratt's published plasma work became important here.

His work on high-current plasma and Z-pinch morphology, and his controversial proposal connecting some ancient petroglyph forms with extreme auroral/plasma events, supplied a physical vocabulary for shapes I had already been looking at.

I did not need Peratt to prove the cosmology.

He gave me a bridge.

A reason to consider that a large energetic structure could be perceived anthropomorphically.

That changed the way I looked at gods, giants, world trees, rods and sky figures.

A human-looking form did not have to begin as a human body.

It could be a projection of geometry.

## December 2025 — geometry becomes architecture

On **28 December 2025**, the model took a major step.

The symbols stopped floating independently and became a room.

I pictured the inhabited world at the bottom of a bell-like enclosure.

Above it was a fourfold arrangement.

Four regions.

Thin rivers.

A central circular region.

Then a central spike.

A hanging form.

A question of what the whole geometry would look like **from below**.

That last part became extremely important.

Perspective.

If one structure can look different from different angles, then mythology does not have to preserve one consistent picture.

One culture can remember tree.

Another wheel.

Another cross.

Another eye.

Another hanging figure.

The question changes from:

**which symbol is correct?**

to:

**which view of the structure is being preserved?**

That eventually became projection grammar.

## February 2026 — the world-machine

By **10 February 2026**, the enclosure had become something closer to a complete machine.

I was exploring the possibility of multiple chambers.

An inhabited floor.

An upper flooded or vapour-rich region.

A central caduceus-like structure.

Paired serpent channels.

A rod.

A cap.

An organised interior.

Some of the astronomy I explored during this period became more tentative later.

Sun and Moon positions.

A Moon-projector idea.

Celestial forms associated with the enclosure.

I did not want the book to erase those branches simply because the later model became larger.

They matter historically.

They show how the paired-serpent and central-axis language entered the cosmology before the fingertrap existed.

The early model was much more like a room.

The later model became a growing body.

## The canopy, upper water and world above

The enclosure period also brought the upper-water problem into focus.

If the world had an upper region containing water, vapour or another medium, what held it there?

What happened when the boundary failed?

Could the same interface be a ceiling from one side and floor from another?

Those questions survived every later version.

The simple canopy changed.

The **shared boundary between world-levels** remained.

That is a good example of how the research actually developed.

An early picture did not have to remain literally correct for one of its relationships to survive.

The relationship became part of the mature model even after the picture around it changed.

## August 2026 — one event, many roles

By **8 August 2026**, the mythological comparison had changed dramatically.

Instead of treating every god or mythic object as a separate cosmological mechanism, I began asking whether different figures might occupy different **roles around one event**.

That distinction eventually became one of the strongest parts of the entire project.

Apparatus.

Operator.

Vehicle.

Guardian.

Guide.

Traveller.

Carrier.

Manifestation.

Witness.

Receiver.

Once I began thinking in roles, mythology became far more useful.

A god did not need to be the machine.

A horse did not need to be the world tree.

A bridge did not need to be the gate.

The same event could contain all of them.

## Sleipnir and the transport problem

Sleipnir was especially important.

The eight-legged horse stopped looking interesting because it was strange and started looking interesting because of **what it did**.

It crossed realms.

It carried Odin.

It behaved like a vehicle.

That one conceptual shift helped separate:

**architecture**

from

**route**

from

**vehicle**

from

**traveller**

The Norse material became less like a box of symbols and more like a system.

That way of reading myth eventually spread across the entire comparative atlas.

## The side branches

During August 2026 the wider project also developed mathematical, numerical and linguistic branches.

Those branches mattered to the broader research history, but they are deliberately outside this book.

The important thing they contributed here was **discipline**.

They forced clearer distinctions between:

exact structure,

chosen interpretation,

historical source,

analogy,

and coincidence.

The cosmology benefited from that methodological pressure even though the detailed number and phonetic work does not belong in the manuscript.

This book stays with the world itself.

## Late August — layers and role-equivalence

By the end of August, the world was no longer one isolated chamber in my imagination.

The model had begun to spread into layers.

Upper.

Present.

Lower.

Nested regions.

Worlds or chambers related to one another through a central structure.

At the same time, I was comparing very different cultural images by **role-equivalence** rather than literal identity.

A mountain and a tree could belong to the same axial family without being the same thing.

A wheel and an eye could belong to the same end-on family.

A serpent and a braid could belong to the same moving-line family.

This was the beginning of the mature atlas.

## Early September — the search for a mechanical bridge

The cosmology still had one major problem.

I had too many forms.

Mesh.

Tree.

Rod.

Ladder.

Pillar.

Scroll.

Cross.

Serpents.

How could they all belong to one structure without hand-waving?

The answer arrived through an extremely ordinary object.

A fingertrap.

## 12–13 September 2026 — the fingertrap changes everything

On **12–13 September 2026**, the present mechanical core finally appeared.

A fingertrap gave me something I had been missing for more than a year.

One woven structure can be broad in one state and narrow in another.

Pull along its length and the strands change angle.

The tube becomes longer and thinner.

Relax it and it becomes broader.

Nothing has to be replaced.

The same strands become a different geometry.

That solved several problems at once.

The broad lattice could become the rod.

The rod could be compressed lattice.

The ladder could be an intermediate state.

The throat could be maximum concentration.

The world tree could be a woven body rather than one solid pole.

The sky could roll or gather without becoming a completely different structure.

That was the mechanical breakthrough.

## From three shells to one growing body

Around the same period I began thinking in terms of successive shell-like turns.

The shell analogy mattered because a shell keeps its earlier growth while adding new growth.

The previous turn does not disappear.

The next turn begins while the present structure still exists.

That allowed the layers to become something more than three stacked rooms.

They could be **successive stages of one growing world-body**.

Previous.

Present.

Next.

That relationship became far more important than any exact early shell count or timing model.

The mature cosmology is built on succession, not on one frozen three-layer diagram.

## Local flat world, larger curved growth

The flat-world branch also became easier to integrate once scale was separated.

An inhabited world can be locally disc-like and broad.

The larger world-body can still curve, spiral or grow shell-like across greater scale.

Those pictures do not have to cancel one another.

Flat locally.

Curved or spiral globally.

That integration became central enough that the world-disc and lattice dome received their own chapter in the book.

I did not want the mature model to erase flat-world geometry simply because a larger shell had appeared.

The scales can coexist.

## The lattice dome

The sky also changed during this period.

Instead of a simple canopy, it became more like a broad woven vault.

A quiet lattice.

Distributed.

Capable of supporting or maintaining the world boundary.

Then, under activation, capable of changing strand angle and becoming more axial.

That is where the fingertrap, dome and world tree finally began behaving like parts of one machine.

## Convergence

Once the fingertrap existed, convergence became the central transition.

The world did not need a permanent portal.

The broad structure could **become** the portal.

Paths gather.

A through-direction dominates.

The throat forms.

The important question becomes:

**what normally distant regions become neighbours during maximum convergence?**

That question produced the mature definition of the gate.

## The gate becomes changed adjacency

This was probably the biggest conceptual improvement after the fingertrap itself.

A gate is not necessarily a hole.

It is a change in adjacency.

Normally separated regions become capable of direct interaction.

That idea solved the constant problem of imagining whole worlds crashing physically into one another.

The relation could change without requiring ordinary surface distance to explain everything.

It also gave me a much better way to read mythology.

A ladder.

Bridge.

Well.

Cave.

Mountain.

Tree.

Mound.

Sea crossing.

All can represent **temporary access** without needing to be the same object.

## Many throats instead of one pole

The next improvement was abandoning the need for one permanent global gate.

A network can contain many nodes.

A local node can become a local throat.

A regional event can involve several.

A stronger world-scale convergence can produce a much larger axial state.

This many-throat picture immediately made fairy stories, sacred wells, mountains and local threshold traditions easier to integrate.

The world could contain many addresses without one culture needing to possess the only centre.

## Transfer

Once the gate existed mechanically, the next question was unavoidable.

What crosses?

Water.

Air.

Heat.

Sediment.

Rock.

Biology.

People.

Buildings.

Information.

The cosmology became much more physical when I stopped thinking of the gate only as a visual phenomenon and began treating it as a **transfer problem**.

Capacity.

Duration.

Direction.

Sorting.

Pressure.

Those questions gave the model constraints.

## Water becomes tracer

Water had been present from the very beginning.

The mature model finally gave it a more precise role.

Water is often not the mechanism.

It is the tracer.

It responds quickly to changed boundaries, pressure and topography.

A deep structural event can therefore produce flood without flood being the root cause.

That single correction made many flood comparisons far cleaner.

## The crust becomes cargo

The same logic eventually reached land.

If the deeper lattice is not identical with the visible surface, then land can respond to it.

Surface material becomes cargo.

Continents, sediment, cities and mountains can be carried, bent, buried, raised or lowered relative to deeper structure.

This is where the moving-land branch became central.

It also provided a place for some of the Tartaria and Old World questions that had been difficult to integrate earlier.

A city could be inherited with its ground.

A region could become displaced historically because it had become displaced physically.

That remains speculative, but it now belongs to a coherent mechanism rather than floating separately.

## Meltology finds a place

Meltology also became much more integrated once the active-world model existed.

Instead of asking whether every unusual formation proved melted architecture, I could ask a better question:

**what material traces would an intense electrical, plasma-like, thermal and water-rich reset leave?**

Vitrification.

Fusion.

Foaming.

Recrystallisation.

Rapid cooling.

Distorted structures.

Burial.

The branch became part of the **material memory of reset**.

I still do not treat every claimed site as one thing.

The category is broad.

What changed was that the cosmology finally had a physical phase in which such transformations could belong.

## The waking atmosphere

The active world also forced atmosphere into the model.

A gate cannot move water and heat without affecting air.

Pressure.

Moisture.

Electrical activity.

Oxygen.

Temperature.

Storm.

Sound.

The world before a full transition may feel very different before the land itself moves.

That became the waking-atmosphere branch.

It also reopened older stories of strange biology, giants and unusual growth as possibilities belonging to another environmental regime rather than permanent ordinary conditions.

## The primordial current grows larger

The Peratt connection also changed during September.

At first, Squatter Man had been one possible visible plasma morphology.

Then the current became deeper.

I began considering whether a vast primordial Birkeland-like current might have been involved in forming or organising the entire growing world-system.

In that extension, the first pulse could have been vastly stronger than later ones.

Matter could accumulate around the current.

That matter could damp later pulses.

Later Squatter-Man or Meru-like axial events could then be smaller descendants of a primordial world-forming fire.

This is my extension, not Peratt's claim.

That distinction is important.

But the idea gave the cosmology a deeper physical history.

## The time door

The next major change did not come from plasma.

It came from stories.

Tír na nÓg.

Fairy mounds.

Thomas the Rhymer.

Urashima Tarō.

The Seven Sleepers.

Alice.

Narnia.

Stories in which a traveller crosses a boundary and returns to a different relation with time.

The gate could no longer remain only spatial.

If previous, present and next worlds are parts of one growing structure, then they can also be read as:

past,

present,

future.

The world tree becomes temporal.

The gate becomes a natural door through time.

That idea reorganised the whole cosmology again.

## Yggdrasil becomes woven time

Yggdrasil had already functioned as world architecture.

The time-door idea gave it another dimension.

Roots.

Trunk.

Branches.

Threads.

Fate.

Past.

Present.

Future.

The world tree became a woven time structure.

The gate could be a temporary knot in that structure in which normally separated world-times become adjacent.

That remains one of the most speculative ideas in the book.

It is also one of the ideas that now ties the mythology together most strongly.

## People are not the only things that can move

Once the time door existed, another question followed.

If people can move through it, why stop at people?

What about buildings?

Cities?

Lands?

This is where the moving-land model became temporal as well as geological.

A region might not only be shifted across surface position.

It might be shifted between world-states.

That possibility is one reason Tartaria, the Maya and other historical-discontinuity questions remain open inside the model.

I do not claim they have been solved.

The cosmology gives them a category to investigate.

## The plenum

The world also became increasingly full.

I moved away from imagining isolated shells separated by meaningless nothing.

Current.

Water.

Matter.

Medium.

Lattice.

World-turns.

A full connected structure.

I began using **plenum** as a working word for that larger filled world.

The concept is still developing.

But it fits the direction of the model better than disconnected bubbles floating in emptiness.

Sequence matters more than appearance.

## Mythology becomes camera angles

At the same time, mythology became much less literal.

The strongest comparisons were no longer:

"this god equals that mechanism."

They became:

"this story preserves this role, viewed from this phase."

One story shows the boundary.

Another the operator.

Another the carrier.

Another the route.

Another the survivor.

Another the fire.

Another the renewed world.

The event can be larger than any witness.

That is the mature comparative method used throughout the 88 chapters.

## September 18–19, 2026 — the book

The book itself became another stage in the research.

At first the plan was smaller.

Then it expanded.

The main 52-chapter body explained the cosmology itself.

The next 36 chapters broadened the comparative atlas to exactly **88 chapters**.

Chapter 53 became the dedicated physical bridge through Anthony Peratt and the primordial current.

Chapters 54–88 moved through mythology, religion, cosmology, legend, fairy tale and modern fiction.

That expansion was important because the later chapters tested whether the role-and-sequence method still worked when the cultural material changed radically.

Ancient cosmogony.

Religious ascent.

Fairy time.

Children's literature.

Modern film.

The same method survived.

That does not prove the cosmology.

It shows that the framework is coherent enough to compare very different material without needing to make everything identical.

## What stayed the same

Despite all the changes, the earliest intuition never disappeared.

The world may have an active structure beneath the ordinary one.

That structure can move.

Movement can reshape land and water.

The active state can become dangerous.

The world can return to stability afterward.

The serpent already contained all of that.

The mature model simply gave the serpent a larger body.

## What changed most

The biggest change was learning not to ask:

**What is this symbol?**

but:

**What role is this form performing, in what phase, at what scale, from what viewpoint?**

That question made the whole cosmology possible.

It allowed contradictions to become perspective.

Tree and rod.

Flood and fire.

Flat world and larger shell.

Local gate and global reset.

Static mountain and dynamic axis.

They do not always have to compete.

Some belong to different layers of the same event.

## What remains unresolved

The book does not close the research.

The deepest physical questions remain.

What exactly is the primordial current?

How is the larger circuit closed?

What material forms the lattice or dome?

How is upper water supported?

What energy scale would convergence require?

How large can a coherent gate become?

Can land really move between world-levels?

What would distinguish Meltology caused by reset from ordinary geological and architectural processes?

How do Sun, Moon, planets and stars fit the breathing-sky model?

How could changed adjacency produce real temporal displacement?

Can a traveller meet another temporal version of themselves?

What does consciousness have to do with any of it?

Those are still open.

I want them open.

## Why I kept the old branches

I could have rewritten the history to make the final model look inevitable.

That would be dishonest.

The Moon-projector idea mattered.

The early canopy mattered.

The caduceus room mattered.

The numerical period mattered to the larger research even though the detail is outside this book.

The three-shell timing model mattered.

Flat-map experiments mattered.

Branches that changed still taught the model how to ask better questions.

A cosmology that hides its earlier forms looks more certain than it really is.

I would rather show the growth.

## The final model is still a working model

This is the most important point.

The book is not the gravestone of the research.

It is a checkpoint.

A very large one.

The clearest version so far.

But the method remains the same as it was when I first looked at the serpent.

Look.

Compare.

Find the strongest relationships.

Keep the differences.

Ask whether apparently conflicting ideas belong to different scales, phases or viewpoints.

Do not force certainty simply because a connection is beautiful.

Do not throw away an idea simply because it is strange.

Let the world-picture grow until a better structure appears.

That is how Vardath Cosmology became the world that opens.

And that is how I expect it to keep changing if the door opens again.

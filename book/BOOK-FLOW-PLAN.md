# BOOK Flow Revision Plan

## Purpose

Revise `book/BOOK.md` so the rest of the narrative flows more like the existing vapour-canopy sections.

The vapour-canopy material is the **style anchor**. It is not the material to be made more list-like or more compressed.

The goal is to improve flow only. Do not change the information, claims, examples, qualifications, speculative status, model relationships, mythology comparisons, unresolved questions, or chapter structure unless Stephen explicitly asks for a substantive change.

## Style target

Use the existing vapour-canopy passages as the reference for:

- connected prose;
- clear cause-and-effect movement;
- natural transitions between ideas;
- fewer research-note-style stacks;
- fewer catalogue-like sentence runs;
- paragraphs that carry an argument forward;
- retention of short standalone sentences when they are deliberately emphatic and sound like Stephen;
- Stephen's existing first-person explanatory voice.

Do not make the manuscript generically academic, polished into another author's voice, or artificially uniform.

## Pass 1 — Full-book flow pass

Work through the narrative body from beginning to end.

Convert list-like, note-like, or mechanically stacked prose into connected paragraphs where doing so improves reading flow.

Preserve every substantive element. Do not simplify the cosmology merely to make the prose smoother.

Do not rewrite the vapour-canopy sections merely for consistency; they are the benchmark. Only make a tiny adjacent transition adjustment there if it is genuinely needed after neighbouring prose changes.

Do not turn functional reference material into prose. Contents, glossary, index, structured source material, tables, and genuinely useful lists may remain structured.

## Pass 2 — Continuity pass

Read the revised manuscript in sequence.

Smooth chapter openings, chapter endings, subsection joins, and transitions where individually sound passages still feel assembled rather than continuous.

Do not add new cosmological information during this pass.

## Pass 3 — Voice pass

Compare representative revised sections from the early, middle, and late book directly against the vapour-canopy style anchor.

Correct passages that have become:

- too academic;
- too compressed;
- too generic;
- too formal;
- unlike Stephen's established voice;
- stripped of deliberate rhythm or emphasis.

## Pass 4 — Preservation audit

Compare the revised manuscript against the pre-edit version.

Verify that the revision preserved:

- all 88 chapters and their numbering;
- headings and intended structure;
- substantive claims;
- examples;
- caveats and uncertainty language;
- model relationships;
- mythological comparisons;
- open questions;
- intentional emphatic lines;
- functional reference sections.

This is an audit, not another stylistic rewrite.

## Core rule

**Make the rest of the book flow like the vapour-canopy sections already do. Do not make the vapour-canopy sections flow like the rest of the book.**

## Execution rule

Use one major rewriting pass, followed by the continuity pass, voice pass, and preservation audit. Do not attempt a single blind rewrite of the whole manuscript without these checks.

### 2. Life of Adam and Eve / Apocalypse of Moses
- Text used: Greek *Apocalypse of Moses* / *Revelation of Moses*, a major recension of the Life of Adam and Eve tradition.
- English translation: Alexander Walker.
- Edition: *Ante-Nicene Fathers*, Vol. VIII, Christian Literature Publishing Co., 1886.
- Rights basis: public domain by age; the 1886 Walker translation was used rather than a modern translation.
- Working source snapshot: `scrollmapper/bible_databases_deuterocanonical/etc/en/church_history/ante-nicene/apocrypha-revelation-of-moses/apocrypha-revelation-of-moses.md`.
- Imported as **182 numbered passages**, preserving the translation wording while normalizing the source's single-chapter address markers to sequential paragraph numbers.

### 3. 2 Enoch
- Text used: *The Book of the Secrets of Enoch* (2 Enoch / Slavonic Enoch).
- English translation: W. R. Morfill, from the Slavonic.
- Original edition: W. R. Morfill, trans.; R. H. Charles, ed., Oxford: Clarendon Press, 1896.
- Working transcription: the Morfill text as reprinted in Rutherford H. Platt, Jr., *The Forgotten Books of Eden* (1926/1927), from the English Wikisource transcription.
- Working source snapshot: `sirkitree/apoc/books/2 Enoch/The_Second_Book_of_Enoch_Morfill.md`.
- Rights basis: public-domain historical translation/edition; no modern scholarly translation was used.
- Validation at insertion: **68 chapters, 321 numbered passages**.

### 4. 3 Enoch / Hebrew Book of Enoch
- English text: fresh translation prepared for the Vardath Bible from Hebrew; it is not copied from Hugo Odeberg's English translation.
- Primary rights-safe Hebrew witness: *Sefer Hekhalot*, old Lviv Hebrew printing, National Library of Israel system no. 990018831210205171. The NLI catalogue marks this item **Any Use Permitted** and **Public Domain**.
- Additional Hebrew collation/readability witness: the Jellinek/Eisenstein transmission of the same Sefer Hekhalot material (including the "Enoch is Metatron" section derived from *Beth ha-Midrash*, vol. V).
- Historical source trail: Adolph Jellinek, *Beth ha-Midrasch*, vol. V (Vienna, 1873), pp. 170-190, preserving an incomplete printed Hebrew form of the work; an earlier Lviv printed *Sefer Hekhalot* witness is catalogued in the nineteenth century.
- Rights precaution: Hugo Odeberg's 1928 English translation is **not** being copied. Odeberg died in 1973, so that translation remains copyright-sensitive in life-plus-70 jurisdictions including Australia.
- Chapter numbering: conventional 3 Enoch sequence used only for reference; the English wording is newly translated.
- Direct Hebrew evidence used for chapters 16-17: public web transcription/searchable Hebrew witnesses of *Sefer Hekhalot* / 3 Enoch, including the Hebrew synopsis text hosted by Hebrew University and corroborating quotations of the Acher/Metatron passage. Chapters 16-17 were rendered afresh into English from that Hebrew evidence; no modern English translation was copied.
- Direct Hebrew evidence for chapters 17-20 was taken from the searchable Hebrew synoptic text of *Sefer Hekhalot*. A historical English transcription was consulted only to confirm conventional chapter/verse boundaries, not for wording.
- Chapter 21 was translated afresh from the searchable Hebrew synoptic *Sefer Hekhalot* witness (section 32), which gives the four Living Creatures, 248 faces, 365 wings, and 2,000 crowns. The public-domain Jellinek/Lviv tradition remains the publication/source basis; modern English translations are not copied.
- Chapters 22-23 were translated afresh from the Hebrew *Sefer Hekhalot* tradition: the Kerubiel section (synoptic sections 33-34) and the following *Order of the Winds* / `Seder Ruhot` section. Public-domain Jellinek/Otzar Midrashim witnesses were used as the publication basis; modern English versions were consulted only to check conventional verse boundaries and were not copied.
- Chapter 24 was translated afresh from the Hebrew *Seder Merkavot* / Order of the Chariots material preserved in the public-domain Jellinek/Otzar Midrashim tradition and corroborated against the Hebrew synoptic witness. It includes the sequence of divine chariots, the swift-cherub passage surveying eighteen thousand worlds, and the closing throne sequence including the Throne of Judgment. Modern English versions were not copied.
- Chapter 25 was translated afresh from the Ophanniel passage in the public-domain Jellinek/Otzar Midrashim Hebrew witness. That witness gives **8,764 eyes** and **2,191 per side**; later manuscript/edition traditions include other readings, notably 8,466. The Vardath text preserves the selected public-domain Hebrew witness rather than silently substituting the later English-edition number. Its additional Tzadqel/canopy sentence is retained within verse 7.
- Chapter 26 was translated afresh from the Seraphiel passage in the same public-domain Jellinek/Otzar Midrashim Hebrew witness. This branch gives the seraphim **sixteen faces, four toward each direction**, whereas later manuscript/English-edition traditions differ. The Vardath text preserves the selected Hebrew witness and uses conventional chapter/verse boundaries only as a reference framework.
- Chapter 27 was translated afresh from the following book-treasury passage in the same public-domain Hebrew witness. This branch names the prince **Dabriel YHWH**, with an explicit wordplay on `dibbur` (word/speech); later manuscript/English-edition traditions commonly read **Radweriel**. The Vardath text preserves the selected Hebrew witness while noting the variant here.
- Chapter 28 was translated afresh from the *Seder Irin* / Watchers and Holy Ones section in the public-domain Jellinek/Otzar Midrashim Hebrew witness, using the conventional ten-verse structure as a reference framework. The section presents two Watchers and two Holy Ones, their role in the heavenly court, and their execution of decrees below.
- Chapters 29-35 were translated afresh from the public-domain Hebrew *Otzar Midrashim* / *Sefer Hekhalot* material, especially the 1915 New York *Otzar Midrashim* text digitized by Sefaria and explicitly marked **Public Domain**, cross-checked against Jellinek's 1873 *Beth ha-Midrasch* witness. Conventional English editions were used only to check chapter/verse boundaries, not for translation wording.
- Chapter 29 preserves the seventy-name paragraph for the Watchers/Holy Ones.
- Chapter 30 preserves the Great Court passage with the seventy-two princes of the kingdoms and the Prince of the World.
- Chapter 31 uses the exact public-domain Hebrew reading that Justice stands at the right, Mercy at the left, Truth before the Presence, and the measure of Mercy comes forth toward the wicked person in judgment.
- Chapter 32 follows the selected Hebrew witness, which omits the later recension's extra clause about all inhabitants trembling before the sword. The Vardath text does not import that later expansion.
- Chapter 33 contains two damaged numerical readings in the selected public-domain Hebrew witness. The Vardath text marks those measures as corrupt/unreconstructed rather than silently repairing them from a later copyrighted English edition.
- Chapter 34 follows the shorter public-domain Hebrew concentric-circle description around the Holy Living Creatures and does not import later expanded layers absent from that witness.
- Chapter 35 follows the public-domain *Seder Machanot* reading: **496,000 myriads of camps**, each camp containing **496,000 angels**. It also preserves that witness's shorter row description and its sequence of beings answering “Amen.”
- Chapters 36-48A were translated afresh from the public-domain Hebrew *Seder Machanot* material in *Otzar Midrashim*, New York, 1915 (Sefaria marks the Hebrew edition **Public Domain**), cross-checked against the older Jellinek *Beth ha-Midrasch* tradition. Later English editions were used only to confirm conventional chapter/verse boundaries.
- Chapter 36 preserves the River of Fire purification before angelic song.
- Chapter 37 preserves the four chariots/camps of the Shekinah and the nested fire/storm structures.
- Chapter 38 preserves the twelve-thousand-parasang recoil of the sun and moon and the calming command of the Prince of the World.
- Chapter 39 preserves the explicit Names flying from the Throne with sixteen wings and the angelic orders prostrating three times.
- Chapter 40 preserves the three crowns for the threefold Holy, the consuming fire divided into **496,000 myriads** of portions, and the replacement of consumed angels by newly created ones.
- Chapter 41 preserves the creation-letters engraved in flame upon the Throne of Glory.
- Chapter 42 follows the selected Hebrew witness's sequence of suspended waters, fire/snow/hail, lightning, thunder/voices, and rivers of fire and water held in peace by divine Names; later expanded recensions were not imported.
- Chapter 43 preserves the souls of righteous persons who have returned and those not yet embodied.
- Chapter 44 preserves the selected witness's names **Za'afiel** and **Samkhiel** and its **36 excisions** reading rather than later variant figures.
- Chapter 45 follows the selected public-domain curtain-of-generations list and its Messiah son of Joseph, Messiah son of David, and Gog/Magog material.
- Chapters 46-47 preserve the counted courses of the stars and the spirits of ministering angels whose bodies were burned for improper song.
- Chapter 48A preserves the final public-domain *Seder Machanot* paragraph concerning the withdrawn right hand of the Omnipresent, the **955 firmaments**, five rivers of tears, and future redemption.
- Chapters 48B-48D are now inserted from public-domain Hebrew witnesses.
- **48B:** translated afresh from *Otzar Midrashim, The Book of Chanoch* (New York, 1915), explicitly marked Public Domain by Sefaria. That witness states that the underlying manuscript contained grouped lists of the individual divine names but the 1915 editor did not print them; the Vardath text preserves that lacuna instead of importing a modern edition's list.
- **48C:** translated afresh from the same public-domain 1915 Hebrew tradition, cross-checked against the Hebrew Enoch-Metatron appendix reproduced in the scholarly literature and the parallel *Bereshit Rabbati* tradition. The damaged opening verb was normalized from the parallel Hebrew reading. The selected 1915 witness gives **7,000 parasangs** for Metatron's stature; later recensions give 70,000, and the Vardath text preserves the selected witness's 7,000 reading.
- **48D:** translated afresh from *Otzar Midrashim, The Book of Chanoch* and the separately indexed public-domain *Seventy Names of Metatron* tradition. The 1915 witness gives **92 names** and explicitly notes that the full name list was not printed; the Vardath text preserves that recension rather than importing Odeberg's 70-name list. The source tradition is linked by *Otzar Midrashim* to the 1678 printing brought out by Moses b. Menachem Graf.
- Later scholarly editions also print additional variant fragments conventionally labeled **15B** and **22B-22C**. Those variants are not present in the selected public-domain Hebrew recension used for the Vardath text and have not been imported from modern copyrighted editions.
- Structural correction made during the 2026-09-26 continuation: the solar/lunar/constellation material initially placed under chapter 18 was recognized as the continuation of chapter 17. Chapters 17-20 were rewritten/re-aligned in the manuscript so chapter 17 now runs through verse 8, chapter 18 through verse 25, chapter 19 through verse 7, and chapter 20 through verse 2.


### 5. Apocalypse of Abraham
- English text: G. H. Box, with the assistance of J. I. Landsman, *The Apocalypse of Abraham*, translation from the Slavonic.
- Edition used: Society for Promoting Christian Knowledge, London / Macmillan, New York; **first edition 1918** (also checked against the 1919 second impression).
- Original scan checked: Internet Archive / Cornell copy `cu31924010244048`, which identifies the 1918 edition and preserves the chapter divisions and apparatus.
- Rights basis: historical public-domain edition. Box lived **1869-1933** and Landsman **1869-1931**; the translation is therefore beyond ordinary life-plus-70 terms in Australia and comparable jurisdictions. The 1918 scan is also catalogued as unrestricted/public-domain by major digital libraries.
- Structure verified from the original edition: **32 chapters total**; Part I = chapters 1-8, Part II = chapters 9-32.
- Editorial treatment: Box/Landsman wording is preserved apart from obvious OCR cleanup. Bold paragraph numbers are editorial navigation aids; they are not represented as original verse numbering.

### 51. I Maccabees / 1 Meqabyan
- Canonical text selected: **1 Meqabyan / The First Ethiopian Book of Maccabees**, the distinct Ethiopic work; do **not** substitute Western/Greek 1 Maccabees.
- Primary Geʽez source: Beta maṣāḥǝft textual unit **LIT1819Maccab / CAe 1819**, *The First Ethiopian Book of Maccabees*.
- Beta maṣāḥǝft records the work in multiple manuscript witnesses, including British Library Oriental 487, 489, 491, 502, 504, 505, and 506, BnF d'Abbadie 55, Vatican Aeth. 176, and EMML 652.
- Working chapter structure: **36 chapters**, following the Ethiopic text tradition.
- English wording: **fresh Vardath translation prepared directly from the Geʽez**, not copied from D. P. Curtin/Bekele Tesfaye or another modern copyrighted English translation.
- Digital-source licence: the Beta maṣāḥǝft Geʽez transcription is **CC BY-SA 4.0**. Any Vardath translation derived from that transcription must retain the required attribution and applicable share-alike notice for that translated/adapted material. This licence permits commercial reuse.
- Editorial method: translate in smaller verified sections; preserve the source chapter/verse structure; mark genuine lacunae, corrupt numerals, or uncertain readings rather than silently importing wording from a modern English edition.
- Secondary English renderings may be consulted only to check names, numerals, and difficult readings; their wording is not to be copied.

- Beta maṣāḥǝft identifies both records as parts of the distinct Ethiopian Maccabees collection and explicitly distinguishes them from Maccabees works translated from the Vulgate tradition.

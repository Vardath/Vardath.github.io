# Level 88: The Crystal Lattice

## Working discussion file

This file is a record of ideas discussed so far for the future commentary series currently being called:

# **Level 88: The Crystal Lattice**

It is **not a rules file** and does not lock the project into any particular structure.

Its purpose is to preserve the current thinking so that when work on this series continues, the ideas can be reviewed, challenged, expanded, reorganized or replaced before major editorial decisions are made.

The 88-book Bible itself is being compiled separately. This file concerns the later project that would place that Bible material alongside commentary developed from the completed Vardath research.

---

# Where the title came from

The user previously read a Bible-related work called **Level 66**, which was constructed in a somewhat similar spirit after its author's research.

That suggested the working title:

# **Level 88: The Crystal Lattice**

The title has several useful associations:

- **Level 88** immediately distinguishes the project from a conventional 66-book Bible.
- It points to the full 88-book textual field being explored.
- **The Crystal Lattice** reflects the recurring structural interpretation that emerged through the Vardath research.
- The title is compact and memorable without trying to explain the entire cosmology in the name.
- It allows the Bible compilation itself and the commentary series to remain distinct projects.

A possible distinction discussed:

- **The Vardath 88-Book Bible** — the Bible text itself.
- **Level 88: The Crystal Lattice** — the interpretive/commentary series built around the 88-book text and the completed research.

This naming remains open to revision.

# Original structural idea

The first idea was to make the commentary series:

**11 volumes × 8 Bible books = 88 books**

This has an appealing numerical architecture:

- 88 Bible books total;
- 11 commentary volumes;
- 8 source books per volume.

The initial thought was that each volume could be around **1,100 pages**.

This would create an extremely large unified series, potentially around:

**11 × 1,100 pages = 12,100 pages**

The 11 × 8 structure is elegant, but discussion then raised a major editorial issue:

> the biblical books and the research generated from them are nowhere near equal in size.

Some groups of eight contain very large biblical texts and extremely dense Vardath research.

Other groups contain mostly short texts that may need much more commentary to carry the same finished physical volume size.

Because of this, forcing exactly eight Bible books into each physical volume may work against the material.

# Page-count discussion

A working target of approximately **1,100 pages per volume** was discussed.

This is best treated as a design target rather than a fixed law.

Depending on typography, verse formatting, section breaks and commentary layout, a 1,100-page volume might hold roughly:

- about 275,000 words at 250 words per page;
- about 302,500 words at 275 words per page;
- about 330,000 words at 300 words per page.

Across eleven volumes, this could place the complete series somewhere around **3 to 3.6 million words**, although the actual figure should be determined by the finished layout rather than assumed in advance.

The existing research plus the Bible text makes this scale plausible.

A better editorial attitude may be:

> aim for substantial volumes in roughly the 900–1,100 page range, but let the material determine the final size.

There is no reason to pad a naturally shorter volume merely to hit a number, or mutilate a naturally larger one simply to stay below a rigid target.

# The important change in approach

The strongest idea that emerged from the discussion is:

> **Do not decide in advance that every physical volume must contain exactly eight Bible books.**

Instead, create the work as **one continuous 88-book master manuscript**.

The 88-book order remains fixed.

The commentary proceeds through the entire Bible in sequence.

The research is integrated wherever it naturally belongs.

Only after a substantial amount of the full work exists — possibly after the entire master manuscript is complete — should the final eleven volume boundaries be decided.

In other words:

# **build the whole series first, then discover where the eleven books naturally divide.**

This mirrors the way the original research was actually done.

The research did not force every biblical book to produce the same amount of analysis.

Some books opened enormous lines of investigation.

Others contributed only a handful of useful mechanisms or confirmations.

The commentary should preserve that natural information density instead of forcing symmetry where the source material does not have it.

# How the eleven divisions might eventually be chosen

No final method has been decided.

Possible criteria discussed include:

### 1. Preserve the fixed 88-book order

The source order should remain intact.

The commentary follows that order rather than rearranging books merely to balance page counts.

### 2. Avoid splitting individual Bible books where practical

A volume break should ideally fall between biblical books.

However, if one very large book plus commentary becomes physically impractical, splitting a biblical book may eventually need to be considered.

That decision should be made later from the actual manuscript.

### 3. Balance physical size rather than number of source books

One volume might contain:

- five very large source books;

while another contains:

- twelve shorter books.

That would be acceptable if the finished volumes are balanced and coherent.

### 4. Prefer natural changes in the argument

A volume break may be strongest where the research itself changes emphasis.

For example, a volume might naturally end when the work moves from:

- origins into Exodus;
- historical structures into prophetic structures;
- prophets into Gospel material;
- apostolic texts into final Ethiopian church-order material;

or wherever the finished commentary itself reveals a meaningful transition.

These examples are only possibilities, not a fixed plan.

### 5. Let each volume develop its own identity

Once the divisions emerge, each volume could receive a subtitle based on what actually dominates that section of the work.

The subtitle should come from the finished material rather than being imposed beforehand.

# The commentary does not need to be the same length for every verse

Another important idea:

The project should not force identical commentary density across all texts.

Some verses may require almost no added explanation.

Others may connect to:

- multiple earlier books;
- several transformation phases;
- geometry;
- mythology;
- chronology;
- cosmology;
- carrier systems;
- environmental mechanisms;
- paired figures;
- the broader synthesis.

A short biblical book could therefore receive extensive commentary if it ties together large parts of the research.

A long biblical book may require more selective commentary so the work remains readable.

The final series should follow **importance and information density**, not mechanical equality.

# Research discipline to preserve in the finished work

Although the commentary will be written in a more natural voice, several strengths of the completed research should not be lost.

The research deliberately retained:

- ordinary textual meaning;
- historical/literary context;
- Vardath interpretation as a separate layer;
- contradictions;
- failed correspondences;
- alternate interpretations;
- cases where the geometry does not fit;
- the difference between strong source-level evidence and cross-text synthesis;
- unresolved mechanics;
- timing uncertainties.

This makes the project stronger than simply collecting passages that appear to agree with the cosmology.

The finished work should remain comfortable saying:

- this matches strongly;
- this only partially matches;
- this is interesting but uncertain;
- this appears to be a different mechanism;
- this is probably ordinary symbolism;
- this remains a Vardath synthesis rather than something the text explicitly says.

That balance should remain even when the prose becomes more personal and exploratory.

# Current working concept

At this point, the most promising overall idea is:

## **Level 88: The Crystal Lattice**

A continuous commentary/exploration of the complete Vardath 88-book Bible.

It is written first as one master work.

The 88-book order remains fixed.

The commentary is developed organically from the completed research and the user's own voice.

The work is eventually divided into **eleven substantial physical volumes**.

The division points are chosen only after enough of the full manuscript exists to see:

- where the argument naturally changes;
- where the page counts fall;
- which biblical books belong together;
- where a reader would naturally want a major pause;
- which sections deserve their own identity.

The physical volumes do **not** have to contain exactly eight Bible books each.

The relationship **11 × 8 = 88** can remain part of the larger symbolic architecture even if the final bindings are uneven.

None of these questions is settled by this file.

# Current summary

The current idea is not:

**"take eight Bible books, write one 1,100-page volume, repeat eleven times."**

The current idea is closer to:

> **Build the entire 88-book commentary as one enormous coherent work, let the research determine its natural shape, and only then discover the best eleven places to divide it into books.**

That approach appears more compatible with the scale and character of the completed Vardath research.

But it remains a working idea, not a permanent rule.

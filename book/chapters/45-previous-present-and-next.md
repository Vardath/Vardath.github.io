# Chapter 45 — Previous, Present and Next

The longer I worked on the world-turn model, the harder it became to keep pretending that previous, present and next were only spatial labels.

They began as a convenient way of talking about neighbouring layers.

One below.

One here.

One above.

But that description was never quite enough.

The lower turn is not merely lower.

It is older.

The present turn is not merely between two layers.

It is the world being lived now.

The next turn is not merely higher.

It is newer.

Once that became clear, the geometry started becoming a theory of time.

Not time as a clock on a wall.

Time as **position inside the growing world-body**.

## Time as structure

The usual way of talking about time makes the past unavailable and the future nonexistent.

The past is gone.

The future has not happened.

The present is the only thing physically here.

Vardath Cosmology increasingly pushes me toward a different picture.

The past can remain physically present as older structure.

The future-relative world can already exist as physical, populated structure.

The present is the active world-region we currently occupy between them.

That does not mean yesterday is sitting intact in a room below us or tomorrow is waiting fully formed above us.

I am not picturing time as a row of complete frozen scenes.

I am picturing **world-states preserved in the growth of one larger body**.

The older turn contains what came before in structural form.

The present turn carries the current stable environment.

The next turn contains what is becoming.

The three belong to one continuous architecture.

Population is not what separates them.

**Every world-turn is populated in its own right.**

Previous, present and next are relational labels. They describe how a world sits in the larger sequence relative to us, not whether beings live there.

That is why I increasingly describe them as:

**past / present / future**

as well as:

**previous / present / next**

and:

**lower / current / upper**

Each set of words shows a different aspect of the same relationship.

## The world does not move through empty time

In this picture, the world is not one object travelling through an invisible dimension called time.

Time is partly written into the world’s own growth.

The larger structure records sequence by adding to itself.

That is why the shell image remains useful.

A shell does not carry its history somewhere outside itself.

Its history is visible in its body.

Earlier growth remains inside later growth.

The organism does not have to remember the old turn abstractly.

The old turn is still there.

This is the quality I want from the world-tree and shell together.

The world grows.

The growth remains.

The past becomes depth.

The future becomes the region still opening.

The present is the active edge of habitability.

That is a very different cosmology from a universe in which time is only a number attached to changing objects.

## The previous world as past

The previous world-turn is the easiest of the three to understand.

It has already carried a stable world-state.

That state has passed.

Regions transferred out of our world through the gate may be buried, deformed, flooded or reorganised, while the world below remains populated and present to its own inhabitants.

Life that belonged to it may be gone, transferred or preserved in altered form.

But the older turn remains part of the larger structure.

So the past does not vanish.

It becomes **inherited matter and inherited geometry**.

This gives the lower world an enormous symbolic power.

It can contain ancestors.

Old cities.

Buried worlds.

Former waters.

Dead rulers.

Lost creatures.

Old gods.

Memory.

Again, I am not saying every underworld tradition literally describes the previous world-turn.

I am saying the structural fit is natural.

What is below is what came before.

Depth becomes age.

Descent becomes movement into inheritance.

That is one reason underworld journeys and ancestor journeys sit so comfortably beside the model.

## The present as active interface

The present is not only the middle layer.

It is the **active interface between inheritance and becoming**.

We live inside a world made from what came before.

At the same time, the present is becoming material for what comes next.

That makes the present an incredibly temporary kind of permanence.

It feels complete because ordinary human life happens inside it.

But from the viewpoint of the larger world-body, it is one phase in an ongoing handoff.

The present receives from the past.

The present gives to the future.

The current runs through all three.

The lattice connects all three.

The gate temporarily changes the relationship among them.

That is why the present can never be understood as an isolated world in the mature cosmology.

Its stability depends on being separated from neighbouring states.

Its history depends on what it inherited.

Its eventual reset depends on what already exists beyond it and on how adjacency changes.

The present is the middle of a process.

## The next world as future

The next world is the strangest part of the model.

If it already exists in some form, how can it be the future?

My answer is that **existence and dominance are not the same thing**.

A seed exists before the tree.

An embryo exists before the adult.

A new shell chamber can already contain life before it occupies a later position in the growth.

A building extension can exist while the older rooms are still occupied.

The next world therefore exists structurally and is populated in its own right as the future-relative world above, while remaining present to its own inhabitants.

It is future because its stable phase has not yet become ours.

That means the future is not absolute nothingness.

It is **becoming**.

This is one of the ideas that made time travel feel less impossible inside the cosmology.

If the future-relative world is structurally related to the present before our relation changes, then a gate does not have to create the future.

It only has to make an already populated neighbouring region temporarily accessible.

That is a much more concrete idea.

Still speculative.

But concrete enough to work with.

## The door does not have to move through time

This changed how I thought about a time machine.

The ordinary image of time travel is a vehicle racing along a timeline.

Past on the left.

Future on the right.

The machine somehow moves faster than ordinary time and arrives at another date.

The Vardath model suggests a different possibility.

The traveller may not need to move through an abstract timeline at all.

The **adjacency can change**.

The same gate principle used between world-levels can also apply to world-states.

If previous, present and next are already regions of one larger structure, then time travel can become a problem of access rather than speed.

The door opens.

The relationship changes.

A region that was normally separated becomes locally connected.

The traveller crosses.

That is why I increasingly think of the Vardath gate as a natural **time door**.

Not a science-fiction machine added to the cosmology.

The same world-machine already needed a gate between turns.

Once the turns became past, present and future, the gate became temporal automatically.

## The event as a time boundary

This also changes the meaning of the world reset.

A reset is not only a catastrophe occurring at one moment.

It may be a **boundary event between world-times**.

The old world is ending.

The next world is becoming present.

For a brief interval, the two conditions overlap enough for transfer.

That gives the event a strange temporal character.

People can move.

Water can move.

Land can move.

Objects can move.

Information can move.

Stories can move.

The event does not merely happen *in* time.

It may be one of the mechanisms by which the larger world **changes which time-state is dominant**.

That is a much stronger claim than anything I began with.

I did not set out to build a time-travel cosmology.

I arrived there because the world-turns refused to remain merely spatial.

## Yggdrasil becomes temporal

This is where Yggdrasil changed meaning for me.

At first, the world tree was useful because it connects realms.

Roots below.

Trunk through the middle.

Branches above.

That already fits the axial structure.

But if the realms are also previous, present and next, then the tree is connecting **times** as well as places.

The roots reach what came before.

The trunk passes through the present.

The branches extend into what is becoming.

The tree becomes a woven continuity through world-history.

That made Odin’s strange relationship with himself much more interesting.

Odin sacrificing himself to himself on the tree is already a powerful self-referential image inside Norse tradition.

In my cosmology, the image also became a way of thinking about one being meeting another state of the same being across the world-tree.

Not proof.

Not a literal claim about the original myth.

A structural possibility.

The tree can connect versions of the world.

That means a traveller on the tree can, in principle, encounter versions of people and places belonging to different turns.

This is where myth, fairy tale and time-travel fiction began to overlap in a way I could no longer ignore.

## The mirror-self problem

Modern stories repeatedly return to the scene where a traveller meets another version of themselves.

Sometimes the meeting is literal time travel.

Sometimes it is another world.

Sometimes a mirror realm.

Sometimes a loop.

Sometimes a field or zone where identity breaks down.

The details vary.

What interests me is the repeated structure.

A person crosses a boundary.

Ordinary geography loses meaning.

Another version of the person becomes accessible.

The encounter can be terrifying because identity depends on the assumption that there is only one current version of you.

The Vardath time-door model gives a simple spatial reason why the motif works so well.

If world-states become adjacent, then two temporally separated versions can occupy one local relationship.

The person does not need to duplicate magically at the moment of meeting.

Both versions belong to different regions of the larger structure.

The gate makes the regions touch.

That is the basic idea.

Whether a real physical mechanism can ever do that is another question.

But the cosmology finally has somewhere to put the image.

## The Mad Hatter’s table and the locked moment

The idea of a place being locked in time also began to make more sense.

The Mad Hatter’s tea party is a playful example.

Time stops advancing normally.

The characters remain trapped around the same hour.

The place becomes a local time-state rather than simply a location.

Fairy tales contain many versions of this.

A feast that never ends.

A castle asleep for generations.

A traveller who spends a night somewhere and returns to find decades gone.

A hidden land where time passes differently.

A dance that lasts a century.

A child returning unchanged to an older world.

I do not think these stories all came from one event.

That would be too easy.

But they are useful because they show how naturally human imagination treats **place and time as connected**.

Cross the wrong boundary and you do not merely arrive somewhere else.

You arrive in a different temporal relationship.

That is exactly what the Vardath gate now implies.

## Lands can have time-state too

The time-door idea becomes even stranger when I stop limiting it to travellers.

Why should only people cross?

The gate already carries material.

The crust is already cargo.

The world reset already allows land to move.

So a region of land can also change which world-state it belongs to.

That means a city can be old in one world and newly arrived in another.

A mountain can inherit material from a different turn.

An island can disappear from one history and enter another.

A whole region can be shifted vertically through the world-tree during an event.

This is where my thoughts about lost civilisations, Tartaria, the Maya and moved lands began connecting to the time-door branch.

I am not saying I have proved that any particular civilisation was physically shifted between world-times.

I am saying the cosmology allows the possibility.

The event is large enough to move land.

The gate is temporal as well as spatial.

Therefore moving land can also mean moving **history**.

That is a serious implication of the model.

I will deal with it directly in Chapter 47.

## The plenum

The idea of a **plenum** also became useful here.

I use the word in the sense of a world that is full rather than empty.

The regions between things are not meaningless void.

They belong to the structure.

Current, field, medium, boundary and relation fill the larger body.

This matters for time because it means the past, present and future do not have to be imagined as isolated snapshots floating in nothing.

They are connected states inside a full world.

The tree, braid and lattice occupy the relation between them.

The gate changes that relation.

A plenum makes the time door feel less like teleportation through emptiness and more like movement through a medium whose geometry has changed.

That is much closer to the rest of Vardath Cosmology.

Nothing important happens in absolute nothing.

The world is full.

## The future is not fixed by this picture

I need to be careful here.

If the future world already exists structurally, that does not necessarily mean every event inside it is predetermined.

A developing environment can exist without every detail of its later history already being settled.

A seed exists before every branch position of the adult tree is known.

A populated world-turn can already exist before everything that will later happen in our relationship with it has happened.

So I do not take this model to mean fate is fixed.

The future can be **structurally present and historically open**.

That distinction matters.

The next world can be there as a place in the larger growth without every human choice already being written into it.

How much is fixed by structure and how much remains open is one of the deeper questions the time branch raises.

I do not have a complete answer.

## Time may be layered rather than linear

The simplest summary of the model is that time may be **layered**.

Not only linearly passing.

Older states become deeper layers.

The current state becomes the layer we call present.

Later states develop outward or upward.

The world grows through time instead of merely existing inside it.

That makes memory, geology, ancestry and future growth all versions of one principle.

The past becomes structure.

The present becomes experience.

The future becomes formation.

And the gate becomes the temporary condition in which those ordinarily separated roles can overlap.

## One event at many historical times

This also gives me a way to think about something that has bothered me from the beginning.

The same kind of event seems to appear everywhere in human memory.

World trees.

Great floods.

Sky openings.

Giants.

Serpents.

Mountains.

Fire.

Boats.

Doors.

Otherworld journeys.

People returning to changed worlds.

Lands appearing and disappearing.

Perhaps these are many events.

Perhaps some are cultural inheritance.

Perhaps some are independent symbolic invention.

But the time-door model adds another possibility.

**One natural event may intersect human history at more than one apparent time.**

If the gate temporarily connects world-states, then the event itself does not have to belong neatly to one historical date from every viewpoint.

A pulse through the world-tree could be encountered by different populations at different positions in the larger temporal structure.

The same door can appear ancient from one side and future from another.

This is still an open idea.

I do not yet know how to make the chronology work physically.

But it explains why I increasingly resist forcing the great event into one date.

The event may be recurrent.

It may also be temporally distributed by the gate itself.

That question remains alive.

## Previous, present and next are one relationship

The essential point of this chapter is not that I have solved time.

I have not.

It is that the world-turn model changes what time travel means inside Vardath Cosmology.

Previous, present and next are **relative positions in a vertical chain of populated worlds**.

Every world is its own present to the beings living there.

For any given present world, the world immediately above is its future and the world immediately below is its past.

That same rule continues upward and downward: the world above has its own present, future above and past below; the world below does too.

The lattice connects this continuing chain.

The current runs through it.

When the gate opens, a present can interact with both its relative future and its relative past. The gate changes accessibility and exchange; it does not turn one world into another.

Once I saw that, the time door stopped feeling like an extra idea.

It became the natural consequence of the cosmology I already had.

The next question is the difficult one.

What actually happens when that door opens?

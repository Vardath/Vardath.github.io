# Chapter 2 — The Figure in the Current

The serpent gave me a moving line.

Squatter Man gave that line a body.

That was the next major turn in the project, because it forced me to confront a problem that would later become central to the whole cosmology:

**one structure can look like different things from different viewpoints.**

Before that, comparison was still mostly object-based. A serpent looked like a serpent. A rod looked like a rod. A tree looked like a tree. A person looked like a person.

Squatter Man made that separation unstable.

The figure is recognisably anthropomorphic: a central body, lateral extensions, a pinched middle and an overall form that invites the eye to see a person.

But the same geometry can also be described without mentioning a human being at all.

It can be a central column.

A pinch.

Upper and lower lobes.

Transverse branches.

A luminous or structured axial phenomenon.

That ambiguity became one of the most productive ambiguities in Vardath Cosmology.

## The Peratt comparison

Anthony Peratt's work on high-current Z-pinch and plasma morphologies became relevant because it offered a possible physical vocabulary for recurrent anthropomorphic petroglyph forms.

I need to state the boundary carefully.

Peratt did publish plasma-physics work comparing high-current configurations with ancient petroglyph morphologies. The archaeological interpretation is controversial and is not established consensus.

Vardath Cosmology goes farther still.

The model eventually proposes a persistent primordial current, a world-forming structure, multiple chambers and a recurrent axial event. Those are my speculative extensions, not claims established by Peratt's work.

What I took from the comparison was more modest and more important:

**a physical structure can become anthropomorphic without being a human body.**

Once that possibility entered the project, a large symbolic landscape opened.

## The projection problem

Suppose a three-dimensional structure contains:

- a vertical axis;
- a narrow central pinch;
- wider upper and lower regions;
- paired lateral forms;
- bright local nodes;
- changing intensity over time.

How should a person draw it?

There is no neutral answer.

The observer has a position.

The event has a phase.

The drawing has a surface.

A three-dimensional and time-dependent object has to be compressed into a two-dimensional image or a narrative.

That compression is the beginning of what I now call **projection grammar**.

The same physical form can produce different symbols because the observer is not looking at the same projection.

Seen side-on, axial length dominates.

Seen end-on, length collapses and radial structure dominates.

Seen from below, an upper transverse structure can cross the vertical axis.

Seen during one phase, the form can be broad and branching.

Seen during another, it can be narrow and rod-like.

Seen in motion, the lobes and channels can become wings, limbs, serpents or many arms.

This was the first point where mythology began to look less like a list of unrelated images and more like a possible family of views.

## From body to axis

The most important change was conceptual.

I stopped asking whether Squatter Man was literally a man.

I started asking whether the **human form was a way of remembering the axis**.

That is a very different proposition.

A human body is one of the easiest frameworks for organising vertical geometry.

Head above.

Torso at centre.

Arms across.

Legs below.

If a phenomenon already contains a vertical spine and lateral structures, anthropomorphism does the rest.

This is not mysterious.

Humans see faces in clouds, figures in rock formations and animals in constellations. We are extremely good at turning ambiguous structure into bodies.

Vardath Cosmology does not require that ancient observers misunderstood a machine.

It requires only that an unusual form can be encoded through familiar biological shape.

That makes anthropomorphic mythology less of an obstacle.

A god-shaped image and a geometric structure do not have to be mutually exclusive descriptions.

One can be the humanised projection of the other.

## The hanging figure

Once the axial-human relation became important, hanging and suspended figures attracted attention.

A figure fixed to a tree.

A figure fixed to a cross.

A figure hanging from an axis.

A figure pierced by a spear.

A figure at the centre of a vertical and transverse geometry.

These are not historically one story.

Their cultural meanings are distinct.

But they created a strong structural family inside the Vardath model.

The important relation became:

> **conscious figure + central axis + transformation**

That formulation is much better than saying “all hanging gods are the same.”

It allows differences to matter.

### Odin

In *Hávamál*, Odin hangs on a windswept tree for nine nights, wounded by a spear, sacrificed to himself, before acquiring the runes.

The Norse religious and literary meaning is its own.

The Vardath comparison focuses on the geometry:

> **world tree / axis → figure fixed to centre → narrow penetrating rod → ordeal → hidden knowledge**

Odin is not merely standing near Yggdrasil.

His body is placed into the logic of the axis.

The result is a very dense visual and narrative configuration.

### Jesus and the cross

The crucifix is structurally different.

Its most useful Vardath feature is the explicit intersection of vertical and horizontal.

The body lies exactly at that crossing.

That makes the cross a natural image for:

> **axis × transverse plane**

The Christian meaning of crucifixion, resurrection and salvation remains Christian.

The Vardath layer comes afterward and asks why the form is so geometrically useful to the axis atlas.

### Buddha beneath the Bodhi tree

The Bodhi-tree comparison supplies an equally important contrast.

Siddhartha is not hanging from the tree.

He sits beneath it.

The central structure becomes a place of stabilisation and awakening rather than violent suspension.

This prevented the axis from becoming synonymous with catastrophe.

The Vardath relation becomes:

> **living axis + stable ground + conscious figure + transformation**

That is a quiet-axis state.

Odin gives ordeal.

The crucifix gives sacrifice at the crossing.

Buddha gives stillness beneath the living centre.

The commonality is structural; the transformation differs.

That difference becomes useful once the cosmology itself develops quiet and active phases.

## Tree, rod, pillar, staff

The axis atlas expanded from there.

A rod is the simplest abstraction of the axis.

A pillar is a structural rod.

A staff is a portable rod linked with an operator.

A tree is a living and branching rod.

A ladder is a narrow lattice strip that also functions as a route.

A mountain is an axis experienced at landscape scale.

A spear is a narrow penetrating axial form.

A sceptre is an axis translated into authority.

Once I saw those as roles rather than identities, many old comparisons became cleaner.

The model no longer had to claim that a sacred tree and a king's staff were historically the same symbol.

They could be different cultural uses of the same broad spatial category.

That is one of the most important methodological changes in the project:

**role before name.**

## Aaron's budding rod

Aaron's rod became interesting because it sits at the boundary between dead rod and living tree.

In Numbers 17 the rod buds, blossoms and bears almonds.

The Vardath comparison does not need to claim a physical cosmic object.

The structural transition is enough:

> **rod → living growth → sign of authority**

That maps elegantly onto the later model in which the narrow active axis and broad living tree can represent different states of one underlying structure.

The rod is not necessarily the tree cut down.

The tree can be the reopened, distributed form of the rod-state.

That interpretation only became possible after the finger-trap model, but the symbolic relationship was present much earlier.

## Meru and the mountain

Mount Meru entered the project because the axis does not have to look thin from every scale.

Near the surface, a vast convergence can look mountainous.

A central rise can have a broad base.

From far enough away or higher in the structure, the same object can narrow into a column.

From end-on, it can become a point or radial hub.

This is where the perspective problem becomes extremely powerful.

The mountain and the rod do not need to be separate mechanisms.

They can be scale-dependent views of the same axial region.

Again, this is a Vardath interpretation, not a claim about what Hindu, Buddhist or Jain traditions “really meant” by Meru.

The source traditions preserve Meru in their own cosmological systems.

My interest is in its role as an organising centre.

## Mashu and the traveller's gate

The Gilgamesh/Mashu branch added another viewpoint.

A gate does not always appear as something seen from below.

A traveller can approach it from the surface.

Mount Mashu, guarded passage and the extraordinary journey toward Utnapishtim gave the project what I came to call the **traveller's view of the gate**.

From below, an opening can look like heaven opening.

From the surface, it can become a mountain pass.

From inside, it can become a tunnel or dark route.

The object is not changing because the story is wrong.

The role is changing because the observer occupies a different relation to it.

That idea would later become central to the “many camera angles” framework.

## The eye at the end of the axis

Projection does not stop with side views.

Look down the axis.

The long form collapses.

Now radial structure matters.

A node can become:

- a ring;
- a wheel;
- a rosette;
- a star;
- a hub;
- an eye.

This became the end-on projection family.

The eye is particularly important because it introduces a second transformation:

geometry becomes agency.

A bright circular node overhead can be described as an eye.

An eye implies something that sees.

A node can become a Watcher in narrative.

That does not mean the Watchers of 1 Enoch are literally apertures.

It means the model now has a mechanism for how an aperture can be personified.

The chain is:

> **node → eye → watcher**

During a later active phase:

> **watcher / eye → opening → descending object or being**

This is speculative, but it is much more precise than merely saying “eyes are important symbols.”

## Wheels full of eyes

Ezekiel became especially interesting because the text combines radial machinery and eye imagery directly.

Wheels.

Wheels associated with wheels.

Rims.

Eyes.

Living beings.

Fire.

Radiance.

A vault.

A throne-like upper structure.

The Vardath comparison is not that Ezekiel saw the exact machine I describe.

The usefulness lies in role density.

A moving radial form and distributed eyes already coexist in the source imagery.

That is exactly the sort of relationship the projection grammar predicts if eye-like nodes belong to a larger moving lattice.

The source still belongs to prophetic literature.

The comparison belongs to my model.

## Side, end and broadside

By the time the projection grammar matured, I could organise the visual families into three major views.

### Broad or oblique

The weave dominates.

The form appears as:

- net;
- braid;
- knot;
- interlace;
- mesh;
- serpent field.

### Side or axial

Length dominates.

The form appears as:

- tree;
- rod;
- pillar;
- staff;
- mountain;
- ladder.

### End-on

Radial structure dominates.

The form appears as:

- eye;
- wheel;
- star;
- rosette;
- ring;
- hub.

These categories are not rigid.

The most interesting forms are transitional.

A mesh narrows into a ladder.

A ladder tightens into a rod.

A rod rotates toward the observer and becomes a wheel or eye.

A pair of winding channels broadens into a field.

A ring opens into a gate.

That is how the project stopped needing every symbol to be a separate object.

## The missing middle

The transitional images may be more important than the endpoints.

If the cosmology is correct on its own terms, the physical structure does not teleport from one symbol to another.

It changes continuously.

That means the model should generate mixed forms.

A rod with branching.

A ladder inside an enclosure.

An eye embedded in a mesh.

A wheel attached to an axis.

Serpents that form eye-shaped openings.

A tree whose branches become interlace.

A cross inside rings.

A central node surrounded by four or eight sectors.

These are exactly the kinds of compositions that became interesting in later visual-corpus work.

The first broad artwork test found some enrichment of serpent/dragon imagery inside mesh/interlace records, but it did not show a complete Vardath package appearing everywhere. That narrower result may actually fit the projection model better.

Different traditions may specialise in different views.

That is a more constrained claim than “ancient art contains the machine.”

## The human figure as projection

Squatter Man therefore changed the project in a way that goes far beyond one petroglyph category.

It introduced the idea that **anthropomorphism can be geometric**.

Once that is allowed, gods and giant figures can be reconsidered by role.

A radiant figure may be a manifestation associated with the axis.

A ruler may be an operator.

A many-limbed form may be a compressed human description of a complex surrounding structure.

A hanging figure may mark consciousness at the centre.

A giant may be a scale interpretation, an incoming being, or simply a later narrative personification.

Those are different hypotheses.

The model becomes better when they remain separate.

## Operator versus manifestation

This leads directly to the distinction that would dominate the next stage of the project:

**the figure is not necessarily the machine.**

A person can stand in front of an apparatus.

A rider can use a mount.

A god can command a vehicle.

A luminous form can magnify an operator.

A culture can remember the operator's identity and attach it to the entire event.

Once that distinction became explicit, comparative mythology stopped demanding impossible one-to-one equations.

Odin did not have to equal Yggdrasil.

Sleipnir did not have to equal the gate.

The gate did not have to equal the rod.

The rod did not have to equal the operator.

Roles could be distributed.

That is the point where the cosmology began to become a system.

## What Squatter Man really contributed

The lasting contribution of Squatter Man is not that a particular petroglyph proves a plasma catastrophe.

It is the **perspective problem**.

The same hidden structure can be:

- geometric in one view;
- anthropomorphic in another;
- radial in another;
- serpentine in another;
- architectural in another.

A culture does not have to preserve a blueprint.

It preserves what the structure looked like from where the witness stood, at the phase when the witness saw it, translated into the visual and narrative vocabulary available to that culture.

That idea became the bridge between physics and mythology inside Vardath Cosmology.

It also made the next question unavoidable.

If the figure belongs to an axis, what does the axis belong to?

That question produced the first world-machine.

Experiment controls

Geometry: Each full-sized circular disc has independent clockwise surface motion. In overlapping regions the discs are modeled as conceptual superposed layers, not as interpenetrating solid hardware.

Physics: Grains receive tangential acceleration from substrate drag, retain inertia, undergo centrifugal migration and random agitation. Density is calculated from actual simulated particle positions, not an image of a sand pattern.

Limit: Simplified two-dimensional granular transport. Individual grain collisions and three-dimensional piles are not modelled.

About the experiment

This model keeps every disc as a complete circle, including its overlapping portions. Solid discs cannot occupy the same plane, so the configuration is a conceptual superposition of independently moving surfaces. With uniform sand rain onto featureless horizontal spinning discs, rotation alone does not guarantee spiral ridges. Depending on friction, particle inertia and edge confinement, sand can remain broadly distributed, migrate to edges, or collect along shear and convergence regions.

Choose Single overhead sand stream to trace grain motion, or switch to Tangent discs for a physically realizable hexagonal array. Pause, reset, advance the simulation and record a 10-second WebM video from the controls.

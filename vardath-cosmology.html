2. The original three-shell idea

The starting picture was three spiral-shell organisms carrying world-disc sections. Two remained active and moved at slightly different rates; a third was dead, reversed and structurally dominant.

Model

Living shell A

One active spiral organism. Its world-disc sections preserve successive eras and its motion supplies one changing influence.

Model

Living shell B

A second active spiral organism at a different phase/rate, supplying a second changing influence and relative-cycle structure.

Model

Dead / reversed shell

An older shell no longer actively growing. It remains as the stronger structural scaffold. Tiamat was proposed as its mythic analogue.

Later refinement: the model no longer needs three permanently separate stacked bowls. The three apparent shells can instead be three stabilized generations or turns of one larger growing geometry, while the active mechanism is a two-directional braid between them.
Stage 1 — three shells: three spiral organisms with preserved world-disc sections.
Stage 2 — timing problem: simple pairwise rotations failed to produce Phoenix, Dark Satellite and Nemesis together.
Stage 3 — growth problem: a golden-ratio growth law gave interesting near-fits but not exact shared timing.
Stage 4 — geometry shift: the shell concept became a growing braided spiral: part rises, part descends, the layers intertwine, then stabilize.
Stage 5 — surface consequence: rubble/crust riding on the moving lattice is pushed, sheared, rotated and jammed into large S-shaped land forms.

3. Chronology tests: what fitted and what did not

The Archaix chronology was used as a set of target intervals for an internal-consistency test. The important result was not that the first model worked; it was that it forced the geometry to become more specific.

138 yPhoenix interval≈395 yDark Satellite track792 yNemesis X track144,000 d≈394.26 mean Gregorian years
Failed test

Three simple pairwise rotations

With one fixed shell and two constant-rate active shells, the third relative period is constrained by the other two. Assigning 138, 395 and 792 years in every pairwise arrangement failed. The minimal one-contact-per-turn model was rejected.

Near relation

The 395 / 792 clue

2 × 395 = 790, only about 0.25% below 792. This suggested a half-phase / full-phase interpretation, but by itself it did not generate Phoenix's 138-year period.

Constructed fit

Eight gates and 2:3 relative motion

A later toy model placed eight encounter positions around the dead shell, with two stronger opposite gates. One active shell crossing eight positions produces 138-year pulses when its turn is 1104 years; relative A/B motion supplies a slower envelope. This was a fitted kinematic example, not evidence for shells.

Growth experiment

Golden-ratio growth

Using R(t)=R₀ φ^(t/552), a √2 scale change occurs in about 397.56 years and a doubling in about 795.11 years. These are close to the 395/792 targets but drift, miss the phase relation and were chosen after seeing the target numbers.

T(q) = 552 ln(q) / ln(φ)   →   T(√2) ≈ 397.56 y,   T(2) ≈ 795.11 y
Result: the numerical work preserved a useful clue—a near half-cycle/full-cycle relationship—but rejected the idea that the three event tracks were already explained by three simple rotations. The model needed a real spatial growth and folding rule.

4. The braided ammonite geometry

The major change was to treat the shells as a growing spiral envelope and the active sky lattice as a two-strand braid. The braid can naturally send one strand upward while its partner moves downward.

Growth law

Large-scale shell

R(θ) = R₀ e^(bθ)

A logarithmic spiral is the simplest ammonite-like growth law: every full turn multiplies the scale by a constant factor. Fibonacci/φ growth is a possible special calibration, not assumed by the geometry itself.

Braid law

Two opposed strands

zA = pθ + a sin(kθ)
zB = pθ − a sin(kθ)

The derivatives are p ± ak cos(kθ). Whenever the oscillating braid term is stronger than the slow pitch, one strand moves upward while the other moves downward at the same time.

Interpretive gain: “upper world,” “present world” and “lower world” can be consecutive stabilized regions of the same growth process. The braid is the moving transition zone; the apparent shells are its stabilized record.

5. The sky rolls into a scroll / finger-trap rod

The finger-trap analogy supplied the missing transformation: width can be converted into axial length and winding. A broad braided surface can contract toward an axis without the lattice disappearing.

S² = L² + (2πNa)²   →   a = √(S²−L²)/(2πN)

For a strand of nearly fixed length S, increasing axial length L reduces braid radius a. The same material can therefore pass between an open sheet/tube state and a long thin braided rod state.

7. Flat maps, continent edges and the correction they forced

Comparing the lattice to flat-Earth map families was useful mainly because the edge geometry exposed which versions of the model cannot work.

Fails

Literal north-pole azimuthal disc

A Gleason/azimuthal-equidistant style layout resembles an end-on view of an opened radial lattice, but it badly stretches southern east-west distances and continent shapes. Australia becomes far too wide; the South Pole is spread around the perimeter.

Fails

Spiral used directly as longitude

If r(θ)=r₀e^(bθ) is ordinary longitude, a full 360° circuit changes radius. Coastlines crossing the seam do not close. A continent becomes multivalued between spiral turns.

Refinement

Closed geography inside each layer

Latitude/longitude must still close normally within a stabilized world layer. Spiral turn, braid phase and expansion are separate shell coordinates, not replacements for ordinary geography.

Projection clue

Why old flat maps still resemble it

Looking down the axis of the expanded braid naturally produces rings, radial strands and convergence points. A flat polar map can therefore resemble a projection of the mechanism without being the literal surface geometry.

geographic state = (latitude, longitude)   separate from   shell state = (turn n, braid phase σ, expansion a)

8. Old navigation lines, convergence circles and the 33rd direction

Portolan charts introduced a closer visual analogue than latitude-longitude grids: repeated convergence centres connected by families of rhumb/wind lines, often built from circular divisions associated with 16 points and 32 compass directions.

Historical structure

32 around, 1 through

The historical compass rose uses 32 horizontal bearings; medieval chart makers did not call the centre a “33rd compass direction.” In the Vardath lattice, however, the 32 circumferential orientations are supplemented by the axis through the convergence point.

32 planar bearings + 1 axial line = 33 structural directions

The axis has two senses—up and down—but remains one geometric line. That matches the two braid strands: one can travel +z while its partner travels −z.

Key interpretation: when the lattice contracts toward the axis, angular distinctions collapse. In polar coordinates all angles meet at r = 0. The model therefore has a natural symbolic transition 32 → 1 → 32, with the reopened side potentially carrying the opposite braid phase.

9. The lands as rubble pushed by the moving lattice

The latest step treats continents and island arcs not as the lattice itself, but as fractured material riding on top of it. A moving substrate can advect, shear, rotate, compress and jam the overlying material.

v = vᵣ(r,θ) r̂ + vθ(r,θ) θ̂

If the tangential component reverses sign across neighbouring lattice cells, trajectories bend first one way and then the other. The natural large-scale trace is an S-curve.

Americas

The proposed reading sees North and South America as material pushed around a major hinge/shear region near Central America and the Caribbean, creating a large-scale S tendency rather than two unrelated outlines.

Europe, Africa and Arabia

The Mediterranean–Anatolia–Arabia–Zagros region is treated as a bend where moving material is compressed, rotated and diverted sideways. The Arabia projection becomes a wedge driven into resistant material while rifting occurs behind it.

India to Thailand / Sunda

India is interpreted as a strong northward projection into Eurasia, continuing east through the curved Burma–Thailand–Sumatra–Java deformation chain. In ordinary geology this region already records collision, subduction and oblique motion.

East Australia, New Guinea, Indonesia

Eastern Australia and the complex New Guinea–Banda–Indonesia region become another place to look for curved flow and jamming around lattice cells rather than treating coastline bends as arbitrary.

Critical control: conventional plate tectonics already explains large-scale rifting, collision, subduction, transform motion and island arcs in these regions. The Vardath model only becomes scientifically interesting if one fixed lattice flow predicts coastline curvature, mountain/fault orientation and measured plate motion without moving its nodes after seeing the answers.

What “three shells” means now

At a given epoch, the lower shell can be the previous stabilized turn, the present world the current stabilized turn, and the upper shell the next turn being formed. The true active machine is the two-directional braid between them.

What forms the lands

The crust/rubble layer rides above the moving mesh. Divergence separates it; convergence thickens/uplifts it; oblique motion shears and rotates it. Alternating shear can imprint S-shaped continental and island-arc geometry.

What the “33rd direction” means

The opened lattice has circumferential orientations. At a convergence centre they meet the hidden axial degree of freedom—the direction through the sheet rather than around it.

What Tiamat means in the mature version

Not merely an upper sky. Tiamat is the proposed ancestral living lattice/shell whose divided, stabilized body becomes the structural scaffold of the cosmos.

12. Current concise statement

Vardath Cosmology proposes that the apparent world layers are successive stabilized turns of a larger growing spiral structure. Between them is a braided lattice whose two opposed strands can simultaneously move upward and downward. Under tension the expanded lattice can contract into a scroll-like finger-trap rod at convergence centres, carry phase and material through an axial transition, and reopen on another turn. Fragmented surface material riding on the lattice is then pushed, sheared and rotated into large-scale curved and S-shaped landforms. Ancient myths, portolan convergence networks and alternative chronologies are treated as comparison material; mathematics, geography and geology are used as attempts to falsify or refine the mechanism.

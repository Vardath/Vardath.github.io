# Ezekiel 13:17–23 — bands/veils, trapping, tearing, release

This is highly relevant to the user's web/fingertrap interest but requires unusually strict philological control.

The Hebrew terms often translated:
- bands/cushions/amulets;
- veils/coverings;

are rare and difficult.

Modern scholarship disagrees over:
- their exact form;
- whether they are magical bands;
- apotropaic/healing objects;
- necromantic paraphernalia;
- prophetic ritual objects;
- how literally “hunting/trapping lives” should be understood.

Daewook Kim, Jonathan Stökl, John F. Evans and others have shown that the passage cannot safely be reduced to a simple picture of malicious “witches tying people up.”

### Safe structural core

The text as transmitted gives:

**bands attached**
+ **head coverings/veils**
→ associated with **hunting / trapping lives**.

Then divine response:

**bands torn away**
→ trapped lives released.

**veils torn away**
→ people no longer prey.

### Vardath relevance

At a minimum this gives:

**binding structure ON**
→ capture/retention state.

**binding structure torn/released**
→ retained units go free.

This is one of the closest flexible-structure state changes in Ezekiel.

### Hard limit

Because the key Hebrew nouns are hapax/obscure and the ritual background is debated:

> **Do not reconstruct a literal fingertrap from Ezekiel 13:17–23.**

Use only the general state grammar:
**binding / trapping / tearing / release**.

# SECTION 53B1 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Ezekiel 12 is a prophetic sign-act representing exile/escape; its wall aperture is historically/politically grounded.
2. Ezekiel 12:13's “Babylon yet not see it” is traditionally associated with Zedekiah's blinding before transport; therefore the face/vision imagery has a strong historical control.
3. Net/snare in Ezekiel 12 and 17 is capture imagery; do not automatically equate it with the user's broad world-web.
4. Ezekiel 13's wall/whitewash image is metaphorical criticism of false security/prophecy, but its structural logic is unusually concrete.
5. The exact building material behind the translated “whitewash/flimsy wall/untempered mortar” is debated; retain the safe contrast **surface treatment vs inadequate structure**.
6. Ezekiel 13:17–23 is textually and lexically difficult. The terms for bands/coverings are hapax or obscure and have produced competing interpretations involving prophecy, magic, necromancy and ritual healing/protection.
7. Therefore only the broad **binding/trapping → tearing/release** grammar should enter the physical comparison.
8. Ezekiel 14 deliberately separates several judgment causes that can yield similar desolation.
9. Ezekiel 15 focuses on vine wood's lack of ordinary timber utility and on further damage by burning; do not reinterpret it as a literal description of cosmic vine material.
10. Ezekiel 16 is a violent marital/city allegory; body/birth/clothing mechanics should be used only as morphology/process comparison.
11. Ezekiel 17 explicitly interprets its eagles/vine politically: Babylon, Judah/Zedekiah and Egypt. Root/branch geometry is comparative secondary analysis.
12. Ezekiel 18 is a major theological statement about responsibility and change of state; scholarship debates how it relates to corporate identity, Ezekiel 20, Torah and Jeremiah 31.
13. Ezekiel 19 uses lion/vine metaphors for rulers/polity; net/collar capture is real ancient hunting imagery used politically.
14. Ezekiel 20 reworks Israel's past into a strongly patterned historical-theological cycle; scholarship debates its relation to Pentateuchal traditions and its harsh theological features.
15. “Pass under the rod” is commonly understood as shepherd counting/inspection, supported by Leviticus 27:32 and Jeremiah 33:13.
16. The following phrase often translated “bond of the covenant” is difficult; the Old Greek tradition can reflect a reading closer to precise numbering/counting. Do not build exact fingertrap mechanics on the English phrase alone.
17. The strong safe result is **controlled passage + accounting/selection + purge + admission**.
18. Ezekiel's textual tradition remains pluriform; Old Greek and MT differences should continue to be checked before making one phrase foundational.

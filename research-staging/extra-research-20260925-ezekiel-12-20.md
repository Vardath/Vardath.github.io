# SECTION 53B1 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Ezekiel 12 is a sign-action depicting exile/royal escape and capture; historical tradition about Zedekiah's blinding explains the “Babylon but not see it” paradox without exotic physics.
2. The wall-digging sequence is still structurally useful because it cleanly separates aperture formation and cargo transfer.
3. Ezekiel 13's *kĕsāṯôt* and *mispāḥôt* are hapax legomena; “bands” and “veils” are plausible but not secure.
4. Scholarly interpretations of the women range across false prophecy, ritual/magic, necromancy and related practices. Exact physical reconstruction is impossible.
5. Therefore use only the secure constraint→release logic from Ezekiel 13.
6. Ezekiel 13's wall/whitewash image is a rhetorical judgment image, but its distinction between cosmetic coating and structural integrity is physically coherent.
7. Ezekiel 15 intentionally denies useful structural function to vine wood; shape alone is not function.
8. Ezekiel 16 is explicit city-as-woman allegory and strongly demonstrates anthropomorphic projection.
9. Ezekiel 17 itself identifies the eagle/vine riddle with Babylon, Judah/Zedekiah and Egypt; political interpretation controls the Vardath comparison.
10. Ezekiel 18 strongly resists deterministic inheritance of moral state across generations.
11. Ezekiel 19 is a lament for rulers; lion/net and vine/stem geometry is allegorical.
12. Ezekiel 20's “pass under the rod” is commonly understood against shepherd counting/inspection imagery, with Leviticus 27:32 and Jeremiah 33:13 as close parallels.
13. The phrase rendered “bond of the covenant” in Ezekiel 20:37 contains a difficult/rare Hebrew expression and ancient versions differ. Do not extract exact binding mechanics from that phrase.
14. The secure process is gathering → under-rod accounting → rebel purge → differential destination entry.
15. Ezekiel 20 therefore supplies an exceptionally strong **sorting-threshold topology**, not evidence of a literal cosmic staff or fingertrap.

# SECTION 53C2B textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Gog/Magog in Ezekiel 38–39 is prophetic-apocalyptic enemy imagery assembled from named peoples/geographies; later Jewish, Christian and Islamic receptions expand it differently.
2. The text itself presents Gog's multinational coalition as a coordinated military network.
3. “Hooks in jaws” is control/subjugation imagery and parallels earlier Ezekiel language; it does not describe literal cosmic hardware.
4. The target's “no walls, bars or gates” belongs to fortification/security vocabulary.
5. The “centre/navel of earth” phrase is centralizing symbolic geography, not a measured geodetic claim.
6. Cloud/storm imagery describes the invading horde's scale/coverage.
7. The great shaking and universal wall/mountain response are prophetic cosmic-judgment imagery; use as a cross-domain effect sequence, not direct geophysical observation.
8. Internal sword conflict is a distinct organizational-collapse mechanism from the external storm/judgment imagery.
9. The seven-year weapon-burning and seven-month burial are post-conflict processes, not established cosmological recurrence periods.
10. Searchers marking bones before burial is a secure text-level information/material distinction.
11. The animal feast is sacrificial/judgment imagery; ecological “processing” is an analytical abstraction.
12. Ezekiel 39 ends with renewed gathering and spirit outpouring.
13. Papyrus 967 places chapters 38–39 before 37 and omits the substantial end-unit of chapter 36 found in MT.
14. Lilly and Crane treat these differences as meaningful for reading the restoration sequence; scholarship debates how to reconstruct the literary history.
15. Therefore both process orders must be preserved without privileging whichever fits Vardath better.

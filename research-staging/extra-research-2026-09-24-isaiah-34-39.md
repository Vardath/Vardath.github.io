# Literary / historical control

Do not flatten Isaiah 34–39 into one eyewitness sequence.

Modern scholarship commonly treats:
- **34–35** as a closely paired judgment/restoration complex, often associated with a later layer of Isaiah and language resembling Isaiah 40–55;
- **36–39** as Hezekiah/Sennacherib/Babylon narrative material closely parallel to **2 Kings 18–20**.

The Assyrian campaign in Isaiah 36–37 is historically anchored in **701 BCE** by independent Assyrian evidence. That makes these chapters unusually useful for distinguishing:
- real historical movement and siege;
- literary/theological interpretation;
- geometric/process analogies.

# Chronology control — Merodach-baladan and the order of Isaiah 36–39

Merodach-baladan is historically identifiable as **Marduk-apla-iddina II**.

Assyriological resources place his major Babylonian rule in **721–710 BCE**, with later attempts to recover power.

The Metropolitan Museum chronology notes Sennacherib defeating Marduk-apla-iddina in **703 BCE**, followed by the western campaign in **701 BCE**.

Therefore scholarship often treats the Babylonian embassy material as related to circumstances earlier than the narrative order might suggest.

For the 88-book timeline:

> preserve **narrative sequence** and **historical sequence** separately.

Do not derive event intervals from chapter order alone.

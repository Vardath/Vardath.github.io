# SECTION 57B2 — Jonah 3–4
## The source command is issued a second time and Jonah’s route is corrected from Tarshish-flight to Nineveh-entry; the city is described by a three-days-walk scale while Jonah penetrates only one day before the warning propagates through the population; a forty-day destruction branch is announced, but human and animal fasting, sackcloth, prayer and behavioral turning create a system-wide response that results in the branch being cancelled. Jonah 3–4 is therefore exceptionally strong for route reset, conditional-warning, feedback cancellation, nested protection, ecological dependency, micro-environment manipulation and scale comparison. # SECTION 57B2 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Working base text: Jonah 3–4 NRSVUE.
2. Modern scholarship commonly treats the book as a highly literary narrative whose composition may be later than the 8th-century Jonah son of Amittai mentioned in 2 Kings 14:25. Preserve historical figure and literary composition as separate questions.
3. Jonah 3’s “three-days’ walk” is debated. NET notes that archaeology does not fit a simple literal wall-to-wall three-day walk and lists environs/idiomatic/business-trip alternatives. Charles Halton’s BBR article argues figurative size language better fits the evidence.
4. A 2020 HTS study argues that Jonah’s day counts have literary/symbolic functions, but this is an interpretation, not a license to assign new cosmological values.
5. Jonah’s one-day penetration followed by rapid city response supports a literary information-propagation reading; it should not be turned into a literal one-third geometric rule automatically.
6. “Forty days” is one announced warning interval. The destruction is not carried out because the narrative explicitly says God relents/changes mind after behavior changes.
7. Jonah 3:10 should be compared with Amos 7:3,6 and Jeremiah-style conditional prophecy logic: warning information can participate in changing the future branch.
8. Humans and animals participate in the Nineveh fast/sackcloth scene; scholarship often notes the story’s irony/humor and extremity. The animals should nevertheless remain part of the explicit narrative system.
9. Jonah 4:6’s Hebrew **qiqayon** does not securely identify a species. “Plant/bush” is safer than insisting on castor bean/gourd/vine.
10. NET notes the recurring “appoint/provide” verb across fish, plant, worm and wind. This supports operator-role comparison but not common physical mechanism.
11. The worm attacks the plant, not Jonah; Jonah’s heat stress is therefore indirect through loss of protection.
12. The east wind has translation variation (“hot,” “scorching,” “sultry”). Its secure function is environmental heat/stress.
13. Jonah 4:10’s one-night growth/death emphasizes plant transience; it does not establish globally accelerated biological time.
14. “120,000 who do not know right from left” has multiple interpretations: children, ignorance/helplessness and others. Do not assert one as certain.
15. “And many animals” is textually explicit in the base reading and is important to the chapter’s final scale comparison; scholarly interpretation of its rhetorical role varies.
16. Jonah’s response to the final question is not recorded. Do not invent a reconciliation phase.
17. Strict fingertrap status remains negative; carrier/environment/conditional-branch relevance remains very strong.

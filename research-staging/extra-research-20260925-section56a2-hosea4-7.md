# SECTION 56A2 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Hosea 4–7 belongs to a metaphor-dense prophetic collection. Graham R. Hamborg’s 2023 New Cambridge Bible Commentary overview treats Hosea 4–11 as prophetic material of judgment/restoration and highlights Israel’s foreign alliances with Assyria and Egypt as a major theme.
2. The Neo-Assyrian background is historically relevant: the later eighth century BCE saw increasing Assyrian pressure and severe political instability in Israel. Enter the Bible notes the rapid succession and murder of kings after Jeroboam II.
3. Hosea 4:12’s divining rod belongs to cultic/divinatory critique; it is not evidence for a physical axial rod.
4. Hosea 4:3’s land/animal/bird/fish decline is a literary/theological ecological cascade. The text does not specify a Vardath physical cause.
5. Hosea 5’s snare/net/pit are trapping images. They do not satisfy the strict fingertrap criterion because the trapping apparatus is not shown contracting from a broad connected supporting weave into a throat.
6. Hosea 5:2 has textual uncertainty in the Hebrew; do not build mechanics from the disputed phrase.
7. Hosea 5:11 and 5:13 also carry translation/textual uncertainties in major versions.
8. Hosea 6:2 has a long reception history, including later resurrection readings. Bo Lim’s 2026 commentary argues from the healing context and 2 Kings 20 that recovery from near-death/severe injury is the better immediate analogue.
9. Older and modern scholarly discussions show disagreement over the later use of Hosea 6:2 in resurrection tradition. Therefore later typology must be kept separate from the primary Hosea process.
10. Hosea 6:7 is textually ambiguous: “at Adam,” “like Adam,” and related possibilities must remain open.

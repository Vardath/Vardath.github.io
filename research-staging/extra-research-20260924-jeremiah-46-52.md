# SECTION 50E textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Jeremiah 46–51 constitutes the Oracles Against the Nations in MT, but the Old Greek places this corpus earlier and in a different internal order.
2. Exact MT OAN order:
   **Egypt → Philistia → Moab → Ammon → Edom → Damascus → Kedar/Hazor → Elam → Babylon**.
3. Exact Old Greek order:
   **Elam → Egypt → Babylon → Philistia → Edom → Ammon → Kedar → Damascus → Moab**.
4. The OAN's different placement/order is one of the strongest pieces of evidence that Jeremiah circulated in significantly different ancient literary editions.
5. Scholarship disagrees over how to reconstruct the relationship between those editions; the project should preserve both forms rather than treating canonical order as physical chronology.
6. Jeremiah 46's Nile-rise and Jeremiah 47's flood imagery are military/invasion metaphors at source level.
7. Jeremiah 48:11–12 uses genuine wine-storage/decanting vocabulary; scholars disagree over whether long settling on lees improves or degrades the wine, but the transfer/non-transfer and vessel imagery itself is clear.
8. Recent archaeological-linguistic work on the rare Jeremiah 48:12 term proposes an Iron Age wine-store official/inspection context, potentially sharpening the jar-culling/breaking imagery. Treat this as a recent proposal, not consensus.
9. Jeremiah 48's “mouth of gorge,” eagle, staff/horn/arm, and vine-crossing-sea images are poetic/political geography first.
10. Jeremiah 49's “no gates or bars” is an explicit socio-geographic description of a secure/isolated group and should not be converted into literal planetary boundary absence.
11. Jeremiah 50–51 is a literary mosaic drawing heavily on other prophetic language; repeated physical metaphors should not be forced into one literal event.
12. Jeremiah 51:36 “dry up her sea/fountain” and 51:42 “sea has risen over Babylon” are a particularly strong control against overly literal harmonization.
13. Jeremiah 51:59–64 is an explicit symbolic action: written oracle + attached stone + Euphrates sinking represents Babylon's fall.
14. “Thus far are the words of Jeremiah” in MT 51:64 marks a literary boundary before chapter 52.
15. Most scholarship treats Jeremiah 52 as an appended/epilogical historical narrative closely related to 2 Kings 24:18–25:30, though detailed models of dependence differ.
16. Henk de Waard argues that Jeremiah 52 was derived from Kings and that the Old Greek/“Alexandrian” form often preserves an earlier Jeremiah 52 form than MT, with later MT harmonization/expansion.
17. Jeremiah 52 is not identical to 2 Kings 25; its differences have literary and ideological significance.
18. Jeremiah 52's deportation count list is absent from Old Greek and should not be treated as textually universal.
19. Jeremiah 52:21–23's pillar/lattice dimensions describe historical/cultic temple architecture and are a high-value geometric control, not direct cosmic architecture evidence.
20. The final Jehoiachin release scene is shared with the Kings tradition and functions as a transformed end-state after the catastrophe.

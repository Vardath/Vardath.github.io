# SECTION 57B1 — Jonah 1–2
## The source command sends Jonah toward Nineveh, but Jonah chooses a counter-route toward Tarshish and repeatedly moves “down”: to Joppa, into the ship, into its inner hold, then into the sea and poetically to the roots of mountains / barred land / Sheol-Pit domain. A source-generated wind destabilizes the sea/ship system; cargo is discarded to reduce load; sailors use lots and interrogation to identify a target variable; Jonah is removed from the ship and the sea immediately changes state; then a large fish becomes an externally provided living enclosure/carrier that retains Jonah for a defined three-days-and-three-nights interval. Inside that carrier Jonah describes surrounding water, depth, weeds, mountain roots and earth-bars; the route reverses from “down” to “brought up”; source–carrier communication then causes the fish to discharge Jonah onto dry land. This is one of the reconstructed corpus’s strongest enclosure/cargo/transfer analogues, but the fish is a carrier moving Jonah through/within the sea—not a broad supporting lattice reorienting into a fingertrap throat.

This continues:

> **SECTION 57 = Obadiah, Jonah, Micah**

and follows:

> **SECTION 57A1 — Obadiah 1–21**

### Strict verdict

**Jonah 1–2 is one of the strongest enclosure/carrier/cargo analogues encountered so far, but it is not a strict Vardath fingertrap text. The story explicitly builds a downward vector from wrong-route choice through Joppa, ship, hold, sea, deep, mountain roots and barred Pit/Sheol imagery; then an external operator reverses the vector and returns Jonah to dry land. # SECTION 57B1 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Working base text: Jonah 1–2 NRSVUE.
2. Jonah son of Amittai is also named in 2 Kings 14:25, placing that prophetic figure in the reign-era of Jeroboam II. The date/composition of the book of Jonah itself is separately debated; a 2023 review notes a strong modern scholarly tendency toward post-monarchic/Persian or later composition. Historical figure date ≠ literary book date.
3. Jonah 1’s downward-motion pattern is explicit in Hebrew-oriented literary analysis; TheTorah.com highlights repeated **y-r-d** descent through Joppa/ship/interior/sea.
4. Tarshish’s exact location remains debated and is not necessary for the directional-process claim. The narrative function is “away/opposite from assigned Nineveh route.”
5. The great wind/storm and ship-breakup threat are marine-environment/carrier mechanics before any cosmological analogy.
6. Cargo jettison is explicit weight/load reduction in an endangered ship.
7. Lots are a narrative selection procedure; do not treat them as a scientifically validated causal diagnostic outside the story.
8. Sea/dry-land pair is explicit in Jonah 1:9.
9. Jonah’s removal and immediate sea-calming are narrative causal coupling; the text does not provide a natural physical mechanism.
10. NRSVUE calls the animal a “large fish”; “whale” is a later/common retelling and should not replace the base text.
11. Hebrew chapter numbering places the fish verse differently (NRSVUE footnote: English 1:17 = Hebrew 2:1).
12. Three days/three nights should not be converted into a gate-cycle constant. The 2020 HTS study argues symbolic/completion/death associations, while other readings keep ordinary narrative duration.
13. Jonah 2 is a prayer/psalm rich in conventional deep/Sheol/Pit language. It can project Jonah’s experience beyond anatomical description of the fish without proving literal travel through every named underworld image.
14. Cambridge commentary treats “roots/bottoms of mountains” as poetic deep-sea mountain-root imagery and “earth bars” as locked-gate/underworld confinement imagery.
15. Seaweed wrapping Jonah’s head is local marine entanglement, not evidence of a broad cosmic weave.
16. The fish is best treated structurally as an enclosure/carrier in this project, but the text does not describe its internal biological survival mechanism.
17. Fish discharge is triggered by divine speech to the fish; Jonah does not self-open the carrier.
18. Strict fingertrap status remains negative, while carrier/enclosure relevance is exceptionally strong.

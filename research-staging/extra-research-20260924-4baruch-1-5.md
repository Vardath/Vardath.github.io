# SECTION 52 progress A — 4 Baruch / Paralipomena of Jeremiah 1–5
## Jeremiah's prayer is described as a solid pillar and an indestructible wall around Jerusalem; the invading army cannot enter until the city's gates are deliberately opened, making access-state rather than enemy strength the decisive threshold variable; before destruction, upper angels descend with torches to the wall while temple vessels are transferred into the earth, which is addressed as founded upon waters and sealed, and the earth literally swallows the cargo for preservation until a future gathering; Jeremiah, Baruch and Abimelech are then assigned three different spatial roles—Jeremiah travels with the exiled population to Babylon, Baruch remains behind, and Abimelech is routed along a mountain road into a protected shadow-zone carrying a basket of figs; once he sits under a tree he experiences what seems to him a short sleep while the narrative says sixty-six years pass outside, yet his body remains effectively continuous and the figs remain biologically fresh, so the first evidence of elapsed time is not internal decay but mismatch with the transformed city, vanished population, aged witness and agricultural season

This begins **SECTION 52 — Rest of Baruch / Paralipomena of Jeremiah / 4 Baruch**.

The text is especially important for four project variables:

1. **gate causality**;
2. **preserved cargo hidden outside normal destruction**;
3. **split spatial roles for paired/triple figures**;
4. **subjective/external time discontinuity**.

It is also one of the places where controls must be unusually strict because:

# 4 Baruch textual identity and working edition control

The work is known as:
- **Paralipomena Jeremiou**;
- **Paralipomena of Jeremiah**;
- **4 Baruch**;
- in the Ethiopian-oriented working list, **Rest of Baruch**.

The University of Pennsylvania / Kraft text preserves:
- a **Longer Version**;
- a **Shorter Version**.

Dale C. Allison's 2019 full commentary divides the work into three large movements:

## I. Destruction and exile — chapters 1–4
- destruction announced;
- Jeremiah/Baruch lament;
- vessels and Abimelech preserved;
- Jerusalem falls and population is exiled.

## II. Exile and its completion — chapters 5–7
- Abimelech's sixty-six-year sleep;
- preserved figs;
- letters carried between Jerusalem and Babylon.

## III. Return and Jeremiah's ending — chapters 8–9
- return;
- Samaritan origin story;
- Jeremiah's vision/martyrdom in the received Christian form.

Allison notes that the narrative is broadly chronological:
- opening catastrophe around a compressed day-scale;
- **66 years** in the central section;
- return/final events over roughly the following months.

### Permanent version rule

Every spectacular mechanism in chapter 9 must be marked:
- Jewish core?
- Christian addition?
- Longer-version-only?
- Shorter-version parallel?

before it can enter the master Vardath map.

# SECTION 52A textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. 4 Baruch/Paralipomena Jeremiou is a pseudepigraphon, not a work by Jeremiah or Baruch.
2. Jens Herzer and Dale Allison both place its basic literary world in the early second century CE, using the Babylonian exile to address post-70 CE Jewish concerns.
3. Herzer argues for a fundamentally Jewish work later preserved/expanded by Christians; Allison likewise reconstructs a Jewish edition before a Christian edition but allows Christian activity in more places than only the ending.
4. The work survives in multiple versions, including Longer and Shorter Greek-related textual forms and other translations; precise wording must be version-tagged.
5. The gate-opening sequence is internal narrative theology, not independent military history of 586 BCE.
6. The “earth founded on waters / seven seals / seven epochs” language is cosmological-symbolic and version-sensitive.
7. The hidden temple-vessel tradition is part of a wider Jewish corpus of sacred-object preservation legends.
8. Allison emphasizes that Abimelech's 66-year sleep and preserved figs function symbolically for bodily resurrection/restoration.
9. Allison classifies the talking eagle, long sleep, opening earth and related phenomena as overt miraculous/fablesque features.
10. The exact historical significance of **66 years** is debated. Older proposals connecting it precisely to post-70 chronology are suggestive but not secure.
11. Barbara Crostini notes the figs function as tangible proof of miraculous elapsed time and links Abimelech's sleep to death/resurrection typology in later reception.
12. The “Jerusalem above” language belongs to heavenly-Jerusalem/escha­tological tradition and cannot simply be identified with the user's future-world disc.
13. Therefore 4 Baruch is extremely valuable as a **phenomenological/time-topology comparison**, but weak as evidence for a specific physical mechanism.

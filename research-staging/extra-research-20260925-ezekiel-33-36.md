# SECTION 53C1 textual / scholarly controls

1. Ezekiel 33 functions as a major literary hinge from Jerusalem’s fall toward restoration.
2. Watchman imagery is military/social communication before any sensor-network abstraction.
3. The fugitive’s arrival is a narrative closure of Ezekiel 24’s prediction; textual chronology discussions around Ezekiel’s dates should remain visible.
4. Ezekiel 34’s shepherds are political/governance metaphor; flock topology is comparative secondary analysis.
5. “Sort” language in 34:11–13 is useful but does not describe a mechanical sieve.
6. Sheep fouling water is concrete pastoral imagery and a strong medium-disturbance control.
7. Ezekiel 35 and 36 form an inverse terrain pair in the MT literary shape, but P967 has meaningful textual differences already in chapter 35.
8. Finsterbusch and other textual scholars show that LXX/P967 Ezekiel 35 likely reflects a non-Masoretic Hebrew Vorlage with significant differences in structure/content.
9. Ezekiel 36:23bβ–38 is absent from P967; the exact delimitation of the minus is described somewhat differently in scholarship/verse notation, so preserve the broad unit rather than pretending one universally agreed micro-boundary.
10. The missing material includes most of the familiar clean-water/new-heart/new-spirit/Eden-like rebuilding sequence at this point.
11. P967 also reorders chapters 37 and 38–39, so its transformation narrative differs materially from MT.
12. Ingrid Lilly treats P967 and MT as variant literary editions; Tracy McKenzie’s 2025 work likewise analyzes compositional/theological motivations behind the two shapes.
13. Therefore any Vardath comparison that depends on “what happens first” in Ezekiel 36–39 must state the textual edition.
14. MT’s clean-water/new-heart language is theological restoration imagery, not literal biological organ replacement.
15. “Eden” in 36:35 is a comparison for restored cultivated land in context, not proof of physical return to primordial Eden.

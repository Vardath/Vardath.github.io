## Distress resolves into release; yokes break and a dispersed population returns to quiet; an “incurable” wound is later healed; the same city is rebuilt on its mound rather than on a new substrate; scattered people return by marked roads and a straight non-stumbling path; the book’s opening destroy/pluck grammar is explicitly reversed into build/plant; information moves from external scroll into internal “heart” storage; fixed day/night and sea cycles are used as persistence controls; Jerusalem’s future boundary is laid out by a measuring line that runs straight and then turns; property information is preserved as sealed/open legal copies inside a jar through siege; a covenant ritual physically divides a calf into two halves and people pass between them; intergenerational Rechabite information persists across generations while their tent-living mode remains stable; a scroll is progressively cut and burned but its information is reconstructed from surviving source memory and rewritten with additions; Jeremiah is lowered into an empty cistern by ropes, sinks into mud, and is later lifted out with the same kind of tensile system but with rags inserted as load-spreading padding; finally Jerusalem’s wall is breached, foreign officials occupy the middle gate, and a fleeing group exits through a gate between two walls

## 31:22 — “a woman encompasses a man” is a real encircling word but a highly uncertain text

MT says roughly:
**the female/woman will encompass/go around the man**.

But interpretation is famously difficult.

Major scholarly proposals include:
- encompass;
- protect;
- encircle;
- court;
- role reversal;
- utopian gender inversion.

Paul A. Kruger argues the saying belongs to a broader **reversal-of-normal-order** pattern.
Danilo Verde emphasizes that the exact nuance is unresolved and that the Old Greek presents a substantially different wording/idea.

### Research classification

**P1/P2 — low-confidence encircling geometry, high-confidence reversal motif.**

Do **not** use this as direct evidence for:
- a woman-shaped lattice around a man;
- literal fingertrap geometry.

The strongest secure result is:
**future “new thing” expressed as an inversion of normal relational order**.

# SECTION 50C textual / historical / literary controls

1. Jeremiah 30–31 is commonly treated as part of the “Book of Consolation” restoration complex.
2. Jeremiah 31:22 is notoriously difficult Hebrew. Its exact “encompass/protect/go around” meaning is unresolved, and the Old Greek has a substantially different wording; it must remain low-confidence geometry evidence.
3. Jeremiah 31:38–40 describes a restored/expanded Jerusalem. The exact Gareb/Goah geography is uncertain.
4. Jeremiah 31's fixed celestial ordinances are covenant/persistence rhetoric, not an astronomical mechanics manual.
5. Jeremiah 32's deed has strong parallels with ancient legal document practice; “sealed/open” should be treated as document-authentication/storage mechanics before symbolic extension.
6. Jeremiah 33:14–26 is absent from the shorter LXX edition and is one of Tov's largest examples of MT edition-II expansion. Tag all cosmological/cycle claims from that passage as edition-specific.
7. Jeremiah 34's split-calf passage belongs to covenant/treaty ritual. Its geometry is real, but it is not a woven-lattice description.
8. Comparative scholarship notes both similarities and important differences between Jeremiah 34, Genesis 15 and ancient Near Eastern animal rites.
9. Jeremiah 35's tent-living Rechabites reflect a social/traditional lifestyle, not a cosmological world-surface.
10. Jeremiah 36 is central evidence for textual rewriting, but the chapter itself also exists in different recensional forms in MT/LXX.
11. The destruction/reconstruction of the scroll is an unusually strong information-carrier example, not proof that all biblical textual differences arose from one event.
12. Jeremiah 38's rope/padding sequence is physically concrete and can safely inform generic mechanics of tensile lifting.
13. Jeremiah 38's “three/thirty men” has textual uncertainty; the core rope/padding mechanics are unaffected.
14. Jeremiah 39:4–13 is another major MT edition-II plus absent from the shorter LXX form. Treat detailed escape/capture/release material as MT-edition-specific.
15. Jeremiah 39:1–3's siege/breach/middle-gate transition is the stronger cross-edition core.
16. Ordinary city walls, gates and military corridors remain the source-level explanation before any Vardath structural analogy.

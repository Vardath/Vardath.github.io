### Route selection must remain separate from throat formation

Ezekiel 21 sharpens:

**one route → junction → two possible destinations → information/decision step → selected branch**

versus:

**broad connected field → narrowed passage/throat**.

A gate model may require both operations, but the road fork demonstrates routing/selection, not contraction.

### Pair analysis should distinguish common source from identity

Ezekiel 23 gives:

**one mother → two daughters → parallel but non-identical trajectories**.

This is a strong control for later paired-figure work:
two figures can be related branches/states without being the same object or one literal mechanism.

### Information can activate a remote relation before physical contact

In Ezekiel 23, representation/image precedes message/contact.

Retain:

**representation/information arrives locally**
→ **receiver state changes**
→ **message/relationship activates**
→ **later physical/political contact**.

This belongs in the information-versus-material transfer taxonomy.

### Centre occupant, operator, guardian and apparatus must remain separate roles

Ezekiel 28 sharpens this distinction.

A ruler can occupy or claim the centre without being the centre itself.
The king/guardian-cherub relation is textually unstable across witnesses/translations, reinforcing the need to ask separately:
1. who occupies the centre;
2. who operates/rules;
3. who guards the threshold;
4. what structure constitutes the centre/gate.

Do not merge these roles simply because one anthropomorphic figure stands at the axial location.

### External-force and internal-instability failures form a useful pair

Ezekiel 27:

**directional east wind → externally driven carrier failure**.

Ezekiel 28:

**fire from within → internally generated destruction**.

The final failure taxonomy should preserve:
- external load/forcing;
- internal instability;
- overload;
- fracture/disassembly;
- melting/fusion;
- connectivity-preserving contraction.

### Axis morphology must be evaluated by load path, not appearance

Ezekiel 29's reed staff fails precisely when loaded.

Permanent physical test:

**rod/tree/pillar/staff shape alone does not establish support function**.

Ask:
- what load is carried;
- where reaction forces/anchors are;
- how load redistributes after member failure;
- whether deformation remains reversible.

### The same subject can carry multiple state-dependent projections

Across Ezekiel 29–32 Pharaoh is rendered as:
- ruler;
- Nile dragon;
- reed staff;
- arms;
- cedar/world-tree analogue;
- lion;
- water monster.

Track **what process each form performs** rather than requiring the person to correspond to one fixed geometric object in every passage.

### Net/mesh imagery must be role-classified before comparison with the lattice

Ezekiel 32 is a strong control:

**monster ≠ net**.

The net is an external capture apparatus thrown over the monster.

Therefore any biblical:
net / web / mesh / snare
must first be classified as:
- transforming body/lattice;
- boundary;
- external capture apparatus;
- social network;
- trap.

Mesh vocabulary alone is not evidence for the fingertrap.

### Active-body removal and medium-state change should be tracked separately

Ezekiel 32 gives:

**active monster disturbs/muddies water**
→ **monster/traffic removed**
→ **water becomes clear/smooth**.

This is useful for the waking/quiet medium-state comparison, but the source reaches quiet through destructive removal, not through a demonstrated reversible gate closure.

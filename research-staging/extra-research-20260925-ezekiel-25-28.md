# SECTION 53B2b-1 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Ezekiel 25–32 is a structured foreign-nation oracle collection; chapter 25 addresses immediate neighbors before Tyre/Sidon and Egypt.
2. Ezekiel 25’s geopolitical ring is historical-literary ordering, not evidence of literal concentric worlds.
3. Ezekiel 26:2’s broken “gateway of peoples” is best read within Jerusalem/Tyre commercial-political rivalry.
4. Tyre historically had an insular/maritime identity, making sea-city and ship imagery especially apt.
5. Ezekiel 26’s destruction sequence contains historical and rhetorical idealization; later history of Tyre is complex and should not be flattened into simplistic “never rebuilt” claims.
6. Ezekiel 27 explicitly develops Tyre as a ship; this is metaphor, not a claim that the city physically transformed into a vessel.
7. Jacqueline Vayntrub argues that Tyre’s weight/glory and totalizing description matter to the ship’s demise; a heavily laden vessel in storm becomes a powerful internal literary logic.
8. Ian Wilson, Inchol Yang, Mahri Leonard-Fleckman and others show that the Tyre cycle has substantial literary-historical complexity and may preserve different compositional perspectives.
9. Ezekiel 27’s trade list is invaluable for network structure but not a complete neutral map of historical world trade.
10. Ezekiel 28:11–19 is one of the most textually/reception-historically difficult passages in Ezekiel.
11. Hector Patmore shows that Hebrew/Greek recensions and later interpretations yield very different identities for the figure; neither “Satan” nor “Adam” nor “cherub” should be assumed as the uncontested original meaning.
12. Therefore the safe Vardath comparison uses only robust process features: high state, covering, expulsion, downward movement, and internal fire.
13. “Stones of fire” should not be converted directly into plasma/meteor/electrical objects without independent evidence.
14. The fire-from-within motif is literary judgment imagery but is still a valid abstract internal-failure analogue.
15. Ezekiel 28:25–26 returns explicitly to historical restoration: gathering, settlement, house-building and vineyard planting.

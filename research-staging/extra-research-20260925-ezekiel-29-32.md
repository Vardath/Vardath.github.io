# Papyrus 967 / Old Greek control

Papyrus 967 is one of the most important ancient witnesses to Ezekiel.

Relevant findings from scholarship:

- dated broadly to late 2nd / early 3rd century CE;
- preserves a substantial Old Greek Ezekiel text;
- differs meaningfully from MT in places;
- famously:
  - lacks much of Ezekiel 36:23b–38;
  - places chapter 37 after chapters 38–39;
- this alternate sequence is also independently reflected in the Old Latin Codex Wirceburgensis.

Ingrid Lilly argues that P967 and MT can be treated as **variant literary editions** rather than merely one correct text and one corrupt text.

More recent work by Tracy McKenzie continues to analyze the compositional/theological implications of the two textual shapes.

### Relevance to Ezekiel 29–32

The major famous P967 reorder does **not** move Ezekiel 29–32 itself.

But it is critical before we proceed into Ezekiel 35–39 because the **restoration → Gog/Magog → dry bones/unification** order differs.

Therefore:

> **the next Ezekiel continuation must not assume MT chapter order is the only ancient process sequence.**

For the final event timeline, record both:

### MT:
36
→ 37
→ 38–39
→ 40–48.

### P967 / related Old Latin trajectory:
abbreviated 36
→ 38–39
→ 37
→ 40–48.

That difference could materially affect the project’s reconstructed transformation sequence.

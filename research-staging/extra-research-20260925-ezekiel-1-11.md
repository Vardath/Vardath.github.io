# Ezekiel textual / redactional control — chapters 1 and 10 must be read together

Modern scholarship strongly cautions against treating every visual detail of Ezekiel 1 as one untouched eyewitness description.

Christoph Uehlinger and Susanne Müller-Trufaut argue that:
- Ezekiel 1 and 10 show layered literary/redactional development;
- the living creatures, wheels, eyes and cherub descriptions have been coordinated through later textual work;
- some details in chapter 1 may have been harmonized from chapter 10 and vice versa;
- Babylonian cosmological/iconographic traditions provide important comparanda for the support-creature + firmament + divine-abode structure.

Emanuel Tov's work on Ezekiel likewise identifies:
- shorter readings in the Old Greek;
- MT pluses that can be classified as explanatory, harmonizing or exegetical;
- specific additions in Ezekiel 1, 8 and 10;
- evidence that the Hebrew text underlying the Old Greek was not identical to MT.

Later textual work, including study of Papyrus 967, supports the broader conclusion that Ezekiel circulated in more than one literary/textual form.

For this project:

> **geometry repeated in both Ezekiel 1 and 10 is stronger than a geometry dependent on one MT-only expansion.**

Do not build the physical model on:
- “eyes on wheels” alone;
- one harmonizing phrase;
- one late explanatory gloss.

# SECTION 53A textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Ezekiel 1 and 10 are related but textually/redactionally layered; not every feature belongs to the same compositional stage.
2. Uehlinger/Müller-Trufaut argue for significant Babylonian cosmological/iconographic context, especially support beings, firmament and four-quarter symbolism.
3. Their work also cautions that wheels/eyes should not be reconstructed as straightforward literal ancient machinery.
4. Old Greek Ezekiel is often shorter than MT; Tov identifies explanatory/harmonizing MT pluses in chapters 1, 8 and 10.
5. Papyrus 967 and other witnesses support a pluriform textual history for Ezekiel overall.
6. Therefore repeated architecture across independent/earlier textual forms carries more weight than one expanded phrase.
7. Ezekiel 1 is visionary/theophanic literature; it is not an engineering drawing.
8. “Throne-chariot / merkabah” is a useful later interpretive label but can over-domesticate the stranger cosmological geometry of the text.
9. The four living beings have strong ancient Near Eastern iconographic comparanda; exact zodiacal/astral identifications remain debated.
10. Ezekiel 4 explicitly demonstrates symbolic scale modelling, so its day/year numbers should not be read as literal event duration without context.
11. Ezekiel 8:3 explicitly states the transport occurs “in visions of God”; this is a major control against claiming ordinary bodily flight.
12. Ezekiel 8's fire/man wording has textual history; the safe structural core is an anthropomorphic/figure-like appearance divided at the loins with fire/brightness imagery and hand-like extension.
13. Ezekiel 9's forehead mark is theological/narrative selection, not demonstrated physical tagging technology.
14. Ezekiel 10:2's fire action is debated; Goldingay argues for purification by sprinkling rather than simple destructive scattering.
15. Ezekiel 10–11's departure sequence is widely read as progressive movement from more sacred to less sacred space.
16. The user-model comparison should therefore focus on topology/state transition rather than claiming Ezekiel directly describes the physical Vardath mechanism.

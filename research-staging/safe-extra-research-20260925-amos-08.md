**Amos 8 is not a strict spiderweb→fingertrap→gate→reopening text. Its chief value is threshold, measurement and information architecture. The basket of *qayits* becomes *qets* (“end”) through deliberate sound-play; this marks a finality threshold already prepared in Amos 7. # SECTION 56C8 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Working base text: Amos 8 NRSVUE.
2. NRSVUE footnotes that the Hebrew words for “summer fruit” and “end” sound alike. This is a secure wordplay and should be preserved explicitly.
3. The fruit vision is the fourth vision unit following Amos 7’s three visions; its finality language continues the no-more-sparing threshold.
4. “I will spare them no longer” can be rendered more literally “I will not pass by/over them anymore.” In context the phrase concerns forbearance, not physical crossing.
5. Amos 8:3’s temple/palace song wording has translation variation; the secure transformation is celebratory/public song → wailing.
6. Amos 8:5 gives a coherent three-part commercial fraud scheme: quantity measure reduced, payment standard increased, balance falsified. The exact ancient unit magnitudes are not needed for the process claim.
7. “Sweepings/refuse of wheat” translation details vary; the secure function is inferior/degraded grain being sold.
8. Amos 8:8 uses Nile rise/fall as an analogy for land trembling/heaving. Do not read the simile as literal conversion of land into river.
9. Earthquake imagery is plausible in Amos’s wider historical setting, but this verse itself is poetic/prophetic and does not uniquely identify one earthquake event.
10. Amos 8:9’s noon darkness has often been linked to solar eclipses. The 763 BCE Assyrian eclipse is historically important, and older scholarship also discussed a 784 BCE eclipse. Whether Amos 8:9 directly refers to either is not settled.
11. Therefore noon darkness should be logged as illumination-state inversion first, possible eclipse allusion second.
12. Amos 8:10’s feast→mourning and song→lament transitions are explicit social-state reversals.
13. Amos 8:11 explicitly denies that the defining famine is bread/water famine and recasts scarcity as lack of hearing YHWH’s word. Material and information scarcity must remain distinct variables.
14. Amos 8:12’s sea-to-sea / north-to-east wandering is extensive geographic search. It does not independently imply world-layer travel.
15. Amos 8:13 reintroduces literal/physical thirst imagery after the information-famine metaphor, showing the text can move between scarcity domains.
16. Amos 8:14 has textual/interpretive variation around “Ashimah/guilt/sin of Samaria” and “way/god of Beer-sheba.” Preserve these variants rather than building exact geometry from one translation.
17. Dan and Beer-sheba are cultic/geographic route nodes in Amos’s critique. Their importance does not turn them into cosmic poles.
18. “They shall fall and never rise again” is terminal-collapse language and echoes Amos 5:2; it does not describe a reopening phase.
19. Strict fingertrap status remains negative.

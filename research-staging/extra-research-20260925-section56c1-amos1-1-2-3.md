# Amos 1:1 — archaeology supports a mid-eighth-century quake horizon, but exact reconstruction remains uncertain

A long-running archaeoseismic discussion has linked destruction/rebuilding evidence at sites across Israel/Jordan to the earthquake remembered in Amos and later Zechariah.

Older work proposed:
- a single very large regional event;
- around the middle of the eighth century BCE;
- sometimes assigned approximately 750 or 760 BCE;
- with very high magnitude estimates.

More recent work, including a 2023 reassessment by Alexandra Wrathall and new Jerusalem work published in 2026, argues that Judah also preserves earthquake/rebuilding evidence and emphasizes that archaeological signatures vary by site and recovery behavior.

## M56C1-2 — HISTORICAL EVENT EXISTENCE CAN BE BETTER SUPPORTED THAN ITS EXACT YEAR / MAGNITUDE / EPICENTRE

Therefore record:

**secure-ish broad frame:**
mid-eighth-century BCE regional earthquake tradition + archaeological seismic evidence.

**less secure:**
precise year;
single-event assignment of every destruction layer;
exact magnitude;
exact epicentre.

### Vardath timing control

Do not build a recurrence interval from:
“two years before the earthquake.”

It is a relative timestamp,
not a cycle period.

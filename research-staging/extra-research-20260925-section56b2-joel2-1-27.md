# SECTION 56B2 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Hamborg treats Joel 1:1–2:27 as one literary movement: plague, communal appeal, response and restoration. This block should therefore be read as continuation of Joel 1, not a wholly separate event by default.
2. Scholars continue to debate literal locust plague versus military-invasion metaphor, while multiple commentaries read Joel 2’s army imagery as an elaboration of the locust catastrophe.
3. Working Preacher commentary notes that the dark cloud imagery can correspond to locust swarms covering the sky. This is a strong visual mechanism control against automatically reading sky-darkening as astronomical transformation.
4. Joel 2:6 contains uncertain Hebrew. Fine facial-state mechanics should not be built from the disputed phrase.
5. Joel 2:7–8 contains textual difficulties in exact verbs, though separate courses/no-jostling ordered movement is secure at broad level.
6. Earth/heavens/sun/moon/stars imagery belongs to Day-of-YHWH/theophanic language as well as possible observed swarm effects. Physical causation remains underdetermined.
7. Joel 2:18 is a tense/temporal crux. NET notes that the Hebrew preterites naturally support a past response, while several English versions render future. The state-transition placement must remain explicitly uncertain.
8. Joel 2:20’s “northern one/army” has interpretive history. Traditional commentary often identifies a locust swarm and treats front/rear/east/west dispersion as an imaginative picture of complete removal rather than a literal simultaneous three-vector event.
9. Joel 2:23 contains uncertain Hebrew in the early-rain phrase. The broad agricultural restoration through seasonal rain remains secure.
10. NET notes the same four locust terms recur in 2:25 in a different order than 1:4, weakening a rigid four-developmental-stage interpretation.
11. “Repay/restore the years” concerns compensation/restoration after prolonged loss. It does not establish a physical time-reversal mechanism.
12. Strict fingertrap status remains negative.

# SECTION 55E textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Daniel 12 completes the Daniel 10–12 revelation and should not be isolated from the messenger/prince/book-of-truth framework.
2. Michael is a heavenly representative/protector in Daniel's apocalyptic angelology. “Arise/stand” should not automatically be geometrized as a physical pillar.
3. Daniel 12:1–3 is widely regarded in modern scholarship as the first clear/indubitable resurrection statement in the Hebrew Bible and is usually read against the Antiochus IV/Maccabean persecution context.
4. Resurrection imagery should not be collapsed into adjacent-world migration without independent evidence.
5. Star-like brightness is comparative/glorification imagery; it does not explicitly say righteous humans become physical stars.
6. Daniel 12:4 has significant translation uncertainty. NRSVUE prints “evil shall increase” on Greek evidence while noting Hebrew “knowledge”; “running back and forth” is also marked uncertain. Modern-technology prophecy claims must acknowledge this.
7. The two-bank figures in 12:5 are textually secure as a bilateral spatial pair.
8. The linen figure's spatial relation is translation-sensitive. NRSVUE “upstream” differs from many translations' “above the waters.” The Hebrew *mimmaʿal* supports an above/over sense, but the archive must preserve both models.
9. The linen clothing strongly links the figure narratively to Daniel 10:5, but exact angelic identity should not be asserted beyond the text.
10. The two raised hands are explicit and unusual. They support bilateral human-axis geometry but not fingertrap continuity.
11. No text states that the two bank figures correspond to the figure's two hands. That attractive mapping remains unproven.
12. Daniel 12:7 repeats Daniel 7's time/times/half formula and links completion to the end of shattering of the holy people's power.
13. Daniel's continued lack of understanding is explicit even after extensive revelation. Modern totalizing interpretations should retain that humility of evidence.
14. Daniel 12:11–12 gives 1,290 and 1,335 days. The 45-day extension is arithmetic fact; the text does not explicitly identify the event occupying that interval.
15. Michael Segal's 2018 peer-reviewed study treats Daniel 7:25, 8:13–14, 9:27, 12:7 and 12:11–12 as an internally developing chronological corpus and proposes that their relations illuminate the literary development of Daniel 7–12.
16. Segal/Wadler's later work argues 2,300 evenings/mornings may be calculated in a way that maps to the 3.5-year tradition; this is one scholarly model, not a universally settled calculation.
17. The chronology can therefore plausibly reflect successive interpretive/calendrical/historical recalculation rather than one concealed physical cycle.
18. Strict fingertrap status remains negative.

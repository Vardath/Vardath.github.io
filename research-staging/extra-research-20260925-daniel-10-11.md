# Daniel 10:1–3 — a three-week observer-preparation interval

## Important number control

The chapter later gives:
**twenty-one days**
of messenger opposition.

But the text assigns them to two different processes:

### Human process

### Messenger process

These processes overlap temporally in the narrative.

Do not collapse them into:
“one 21-day gate cycle.”

# SECTION 55D textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Daniel 10–12 is one continuous final revelation unit; chapter division should not obscure that.
2. Daniel's three-week mourning and the messenger's twenty-one-day delay overlap numerically but are narratively distinct processes.
3. The Tigris is explicitly a named geographic river in Daniel 10. River imagery should not automatically be cosmologized.
4. The linen-clothed figure's body is described through similes; gemstone/metal/fire/light language is visionary representation, not a material assay.
5. Daniel alone sees the vision while companions feel terror. The text gives differential perception but no physical frequency/filter explanation.
6. “Prince of Persia,” “prince of Greece,” and Michael belong to Daniel's apocalyptic national-prince/angelology framework. Scholarly treatments of Michael/Gabriel and national princes support this narrative-level reading.
7. The twenty-one-day delay is explicitly caused by opposition in the story. It is best classified as communication latency/contested transfer, not periodic gate timing.

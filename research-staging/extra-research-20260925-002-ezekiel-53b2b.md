# Ezekiel 25 — the surrounding nations form a ring of hostile boundary relations around Judah

The chapter turns successively toward:
- Ammon;
- Moab/Seir;
- Edom;
- Philistia.

This creates a simple but important spatial relation:

**central Judah/Jerusalem field**
↔ **multiple surrounding neighboring fields**.

The chapter does not describe those neighbors as one mechanical shell.

Its value is relational:
**a centre can be affected from several distinct surrounding directions rather than by one single opposing force**.

That remains useful for the user's two-force model because it warns against confusing:
- the underlying two-direction lattice dynamics;
with
- the many local historical agents that can act around one centre.

# Ezekiel 26 — Jerusalem is explicitly called a gateway whose break changes Tyre's opportunity

Tyre's speech begins from a broken gateway relation.

Jerusalem is described as:
**gateway of the peoples**.

Tyre interprets Jerusalem's destruction as:
**gateway broken/opened**
→ **traffic/opportunity displaced toward Tyre**
→ **Tyre expects replenishment**.

This is one of the clearest passages so far in which:
**failure of one node changes flow through another node**.

That is important.

The model should not treat every opening as creating more total flow.
An opening/failure at one route can **reroute** an existing network.

Abstractly:

**node A fails**
→ **network accessibility changes**
→ **node B anticipates increased throughput**.

This belongs beside Ezekiel 21's branch-selection logic.

# Ezekiel 27 — Tyre becomes an integrated ship-body assembled from a distributed world network

This chapter is one of the strongest **carrier-body** constructions in the Bible.

Tyre says:
**I am perfect in beauty**.

The lament then constructs Tyre as a ship from components sourced across a large network.

Among the materials/functions:
- planks from Senir;
- cedar from Lebanon for the mast;
- oaks of Bashan for oars;
- deck material associated with Cyprus/Kittim;
- Egyptian linen as sail;
- dyed fabric as awning;
- Sidonian/Arvadian rowers;
- Tyrian skilled pilots;
- Gebal elders/experts as caulkers/repairers;
- soldiers and shields contributing to the ship/city's splendor.

This is not merely “Tyre has ships.”

The literary city itself is rendered as:
**one composite vessel assembled from many regions and specialist roles**.

That makes it extremely useful for the user's apparatus/role grammar.

## Ezekiel 27 — one carrier-body contains several distinct functional subsystems

The ship-body separates:
- hull/planks = primary enclosure/body;
- mast = vertical axis;
- oars = propulsion interface;
- sail = force-capture surface;
- pilots = guidance;
- rowers = motive agents;

# Ezekiel 27 — the integrated system is driven into a high-load environment before failure

## Ezekiel 28:14 — king and guardian cherub must not be mechanically fused without textual control

The Hebrew of verse 14 is difficult.

Modern translations divide.

The NRSVUE reads approximately:
**you were a cherub; I placed you on the holy mountain**,
while older NRSV-family forms and other witnesses can support:
**with an anointed cherub as guardian I placed you**.

The NRSVUE footnote explicitly notes Greek/Hebrew divergence involving “anointed guardian.”

Therefore two readings must remain live:

### Reading A
**king = cherub/guardian figure**.

### Reading B
**king is placed with/beside a guardian cherub**.

This difference is crucial for the Vardath role map.

It controls whether:
**occupant = guardian**
or
**occupant + guardian are distinct roles**.

Do not build physical mechanism on one reading while the textual relation is uncertain.

# SECTION 53B2B textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Ezekiel 25:9's “lay open the flank/side” is territorial-military boundary language, not an explicit cosmic gate.
2. Ezekiel 26 explicitly calls Jerusalem a “gateway of the peoples”; Tyre's response concerns redirected commercial/political opportunity.
3. Wave imagery in Ezekiel 26 is simile for successive attacking nations.
4. Bare rock/net-spreading language depicts stripping/desolation; it should not be turned into a literal lattice becoming a fishing net.
5. The descent to the Pit/world below belongs to ancient death/underworld imagery and prophetic rhetoric.
6. Ezekiel 27's ship is an extended city-personification/lament. Its value is the unusually precise separation of carrier components, operators, repairers, cargo, medium and external force.
7. Tyre's wide trade network is historically plausible in broad outline; Phoenician Tyre was a major long-distance commercial centre.
8. Nebuchadnezzar's siege is historically remembered as prolonged; later Tyrian history prevents simplistic reading of every destruction phrase as a permanently completed physical state.
9. Ezekiel 28 moves from historical royal critique into highly mythic Eden/mountain/cherub imagery. Interpretive proposals include royal, temple, Adamic, mythic and other backgrounds.
10. Ezekiel 28:14 is textually uncertain. NRSVUE and older NRSV-family renderings differ on whether the king **is** the cherub or is **placed with** an anointed guardian cherub. Do not fuse king and guardian as a secure datum.
11. The NRSVUE explicitly notes Greek/Hebrew variation at this point.
12. “Stones of fire” is a powerful image but its exact referent is not mechanically specified.
13. Internal fire in 28:18 is judgment imagery; use it only as an internal-origin failure analogue.
14. The closing Israel restoration is ordinary settlement language: gather → soil → houses → vineyards → safety. It is useful precisely because it gives a stable-world endpoint without requiring exotic physics.
15. Papyrus 967's famous large differences lie especially later in Ezekiel (notably its ordering **36 → 38–39 → 37 → 40–48** and omission of a substantial part of Ezekiel 36). It also has other shorter minuses. Do not invent a special P967 variant in 25–28 without direct manuscript evidence.

# SECTION 56C9 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Working base text: Amos 9 NRSVUE.
2. Amos 9:1 can be rendered source standing beside or on the altar; the structural process remains altar-associated command → capital/threshold failure.
3. Capitals/thresholds are temple/building elements. Top→bottom propagation is structurally interesting but does not identify a cosmic support lattice.
4. Sheol/heaven and Carmel/seabed form rhetorical extreme pairs for exhaustive escape denial. Do not equate them automatically with the user’s past/future inhabited world-turns.
5. The sea-bottom serpent is a commanded pursuit agent in context; serpent imagery must be classified locally rather than globally.
6. Amos 9:5 repeats Amos 8’s Nile-like land rise/fall motif and adds “melting.” Treat motion profile and material-state image separately.
7. Amos 9:6’s Hebrew architectural terminology is difficult; NIV explicitly footnotes uncertainty. Preserve broad upper-chambers/earth-vault relation without over-precise geometry.
8. Amos 9:6 repeats Amos 5:8’s sea-water→land-surface transfer wording, strengthening this as an intentional Amos motif.
9. Amos 9:7 places Israel’s migration beside Philistine and Aramean migrations and compares Israel with Cushites. This undercuts simplistic exceptionalism in the chapter’s rhetoric.
10. Caphtor is commonly associated with Crete in modern scholarship/translations; Kir’s precise geographic identification is less certain. The migration-comparison function is more secure than exact ancient geography.
11. Amos 9:8 distinguishes destruction of the sinful kingdom from non-total destruction of Jacob. Political/institutional deletion must not be equated with total population extinction.
12. Amos 9:9’s sieve/pebble/grain wording is difficult. Cambridge and modern translations preserve different pictures of what passes or stays. The secure mechanism is shaking/sorting among nations.
13. Sieve mesh does not itself change geometry; strict fingertrap status therefore remains negative.
14. Amos 9:11–15 has a well-known textual/reception history. Some scholars have debated whether the epilogue is secondary/post-Amos; preserve this compositional debate without deleting the passage from the reconstructed 88-text reading.
15. Amos 9:11 uses booth/breach/ruin/rebuild imagery. “As in days of old” expresses restored configuration, not literal temporal reversal.
16. Amos 9:12 differs significantly in Septuagint/Acts 15 reception: Hebrew “remnant of Edom” versus Greek/Acts “remnant of humanity/people” type wording. Keep these textual traditions separate.
17. Amos 9:13’s plowman/reaper and treader/sower overlap is agricultural hyper-abundance. Do not infer literal acceleration of time.
18. Mountain/hill wine flow is abundance imagery; it is not independent evidence of mountains physically melting into fluid.
19. Amos 9:14 explicitly joins rebuilt cities with habitation and productive agriculture with actual consumption, making restoration operational rather than merely architectural.
20. Amos 9:15 ends with stable emplacement/no-uprooting language.
21. Strict fingertrap status remains negative, but successor-state correspondence is strong.

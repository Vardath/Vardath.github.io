The superscription frames the work as a message from Jeremiah to people about to be taken to Babylon.

Sequence:

**home state**
→ exile to Babylon
→ long residence
→ “up to seven generations”

### Vardath relevance

displacement is not a momentary threshold only.

**many years of inhabited intermediate state**.

This reinforces:

# LETTER OF JEREMIAH — REPETITION / EVIDENCE-WEIGHT CONTROL

Fraade describes the work as:
**10 unequal stanzas**,
mostly recycling the same anti-idol argument,
with repeated refrain.

This matters greatly for the 88-text research.

### Permanent evidence rule

Ten repeated occurrences inside one intentionally repetitive composition are:

**one literary witness with internal repetition**,
not
**ten independent confirmations**.

Likewise:
- repeated phrases within Jeremiah;
- repeated acrostic structures;
- copied Isaiah language in Baruch

must be weighted for literary dependence.

### Vardath research implication

Evidence strength should increase most when the same constrained process appears in:
- independent books;
- independent traditions;
- different vocabulary;
- different literary contexts;

not simply when one source repeats itself.

This is one of the project's most important methodological refinements.

# SECTION 51C textual / canonical / historical controls

1. The Letter/Epistle of Jeremiah is not considered a genuine letter by Jeremiah in modern scholarship; the superscription creates a Jeremiah-exile frame.
2. It is a Septuagintal/deuterocanonical work not present in the Hebrew Bible.
3. In some traditions it is attached to 1 Baruch as chapter 6; in others it stands separately, often near Lamentations/Jeremiah.
4. Steven D. Fraade notes that it has no direct literary connection with 1 Baruch despite shared exile setting.
5. A lost Hebrew or possibly Aramaic original is widely inferred from Semitic features/mistranslation evidence, though only Greek/versions survive.
6. Dating is Hellenistic and imprecise; Fraade considers a broad 300–100 BCE range difficult to narrow and regards the Babylonian/Jeremiah setting as literary fiction.
7. The “seven generations” should not be treated as a secure literal chronological datum.
8. The work's anti-idol argument is deliberately repetitive and draws heavily on earlier Scripture, especially Jeremiah 10, Isaiah 44/46 and anti-idol psalms.
9. A claimed Greek fragment among the Dead Sea Scrolls supports antiquity of the tradition but does not validate Jeremianic authorship.
10. Recent scholarship such as **Which Idols? The Criticism of Idolatry in the Epistle of Jeremiah (Bar 6)** emphasizes the Hellenistic diaspora-facing polemic rather than literal reportage of Babylonian cult.
11. Ethiopian-oriented canon/list sources distinguish:
   - **Rest of Jeremiah = Letter of Jeremiah**
   from
   - **Rest of Baruch = Paralipomena/4 Baruch**.
12. Therefore this project is correct to close the current cluster with the Letter and defer 4 Baruch to the next section.

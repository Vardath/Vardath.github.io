# Amos 3:3 — “two walking together” is a pair-control text

NRSVUE:
> “Do two walk together unless they have made an appointment?”

The Hebrew verb from **yʿd** can denote:
- meeting;
- meeting by appointment;
- a pre-arranged encounter.

Scholarly translations therefore vary between:
- “agreed”;
- “made an appointment”;
- “met.”

Yisca Zimran’s 2022 study emphasizes that the verse presents two figures in a reciprocal/equal relation, unlike the predator/prey pairs that follow.

## P56C3-A — TWO FIGURES MOVING TOGETHER REQUIRE A RELATION/MEETING CONDITION, NOT SHARED IDENTITY

two agents
→ prior meeting/appointment relation
→ joint path.

This is highly useful for the project’s paired-figure method.

Two figures moving together may be:
- coordinated;
- mutually related;
- previously brought into contact;

without being:
- two halves of one being;
- upper/lower presentations of one object;
- two strands of one fingertrap.

## M56C3-2 — CO-MOTION IS EVIDENCE OF COORDINATION/RELATION, NOT AUTOMATIC ONTOLOGICAL UNITY

That rule should be applied across Adam/Eve, Cain/Abel, paired kings, paired messengers and later prophetic pairs.

# Amos 3:3–5 — paired questions change relation type while preserving conditional logic

The sequence moves through several pair classes.

## Pair 1
**person / person**
→ reciprocal co-motion after meeting/appointment.

## Pair 2
**lion / prey**
→ predator has prey
→ roar.

## Pair 3
**young lion / capture**
→ something caught
→ cry from den.

## Pair 4
**bird / trap**
→ trap present
→ bird falls/is caught.

## Pair 5
**snare / captured object**
→ capture/trigger condition
→ snare springs up.

Zimran notes that the first pair is reciprocal/equal while the following naturalistic pairs are asymmetric predator/prey or apparatus/target relations.

## M56C3-3 — PAIR COUNT ALONE HIDES DIFFERENT RELATION TOPOLOGIES

“Two things” can encode:
- reciprocal coordination;
- predator/prey;
- apparatus/target;
- cause/effect;
- source/output.

Therefore the archive must record **relation type**, not merely “pair present.”

# Amos 3:4–6 — the rhetorical sequence is about constrained inference between event and condition

The questions repeatedly imply that an observed output normally has an antecedent condition.

Examples:

prey/capture
→ lion vocalization;

trap
→ bird capture;

capture/trigger
→ snare motion;

trumpet signal
→ population fear/alarm.

Classic scholarship such as Shalom M. Paul describes Amos 3:3–8 in terms of an “irresistible sequence of cause and effect,” while later work including Zimran refines the unit’s substructure and warns that not every question has exactly the same directionality.

## M56C3-4 — CAUSAL QUESTION CHAIN CAN TEACH DEPENDENCE WITHOUT CLAIMING ONE MATERIAL MECHANISM

The text juxtaposes several everyday systems:
- animal predation;
- trapping;
- city signaling;
- disaster;
- prophecy.

Their shared role is inferential/rhetorical.

They are not one machine physically changing from:
lion
→ bird
→ snare
→ trumpet.

# Amos 3:15 — ivory houses are materially anchored in Iron Age Samaria, but archaeology does not prove every textual detail

Excavations at Samaria recovered a very large assemblage of ivory carvings/inlays, especially furniture decoration, broadly dated to the ninth–eighth centuries BCE.

The University of Bern Samaria Ivories project describes them as the largest assemblage of Levantine ivory carving found in a Levantine capital before Assyrian incorporation and stresses that older interpretations were shaped by simplistic biblical/Phoenician attribution models.

## M56C3-21 — MATERIAL CULTURE CAN SUPPORT THE PLAUSIBILITY OF A TEXTUAL LUXURY CLASS WITHOUT VALIDATING THE WHOLE PROPHECY AS ARCHAEOLOGICAL REPORT

Secure:

- ivory was a prestige/luxury material;
- Amos’s “ivory houses” belongs to a plausible elite-material environment.

Not secure merely from the finds:
- exact owner of every ivory;
- exact house referred to by Amos;

# SECTION 56C3 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Yisca Zimran, “Do Two Walk Together Unless They Have Made an Appointment? Complementary Contradictions in Amos 3:1–8” (*Journal of Hebrew Scriptures*, 2022), treats Amos 3:1–8 as a structured unit with distinct subunits and emphasizes different relationship types inside the rhetorical questions.
2. The Hebrew verb in Amos 3:3 can mean meeting or meeting by appointment. “Agreed” can overstate ideological agreement; the secure idea is prior encounter/appointment enabling joint movement.
3. Shalom M. Paul’s influential study describes Amos 3:3–8 as an irresistible sequence of cause/effect, while later scholarship debates exact directionality and subunit structure. Preserve both the causal logic and the refinement that the questions are not mechanically identical.
4. Amos 3:6’s disaster/city statement is theological agency language. It does not specify the physical carrier/mechanism of every calamity.
5. Hebrew **sod** in 3:7 can mean secret/counsel/confidential council knowledge; translate the information-flow relation without building a literal hidden chamber/council geometry.
6. NRSVUE notes textual variation at Amos 3:9 (“Mount” / Hebrew “mountains”) and uncertainty in the furniture wording of 3:12. Do not build precise geometry on those uncertain readings.
7. Exodus 22:13 supplies a legal/shepherding parallel in which torn remains can be brought as evidence. Many commentators therefore understand Amos 3:12’s legs/ear as proof-like remnants of near-total loss; the exact legal dependence remains interpretive.
8. The core rhetorical force of 3:12 is minimal rescue/remnant, not intact survival.
9. Altar horns were real projecting cultic features with ritual importance; their removal is ordinary cultic-architectural destruction before any cosmological analogy.
10. Winter/summer houses are best treated as elite seasonal residence categories or structures; exact architectural arrangements can vary.
11. Archaeology at Samaria has recovered a very large ninth–eighth-century BCE ivory assemblage, much of it furniture decoration. This supports a luxury-material context but does not directly identify the individual “ivory houses” Amos had in mind.
12. The University of Bern/related Samaria Ivories project specifically warns against older simplistic ethnic/provenance readings of the ivories and aims to reassess their local/cultural history.
13. Strict fingertrap status remains negative.

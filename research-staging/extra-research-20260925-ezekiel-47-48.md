# Ezekiel 47:1–2 — centre-generated water and human observer follow different routes

The guide brings Ezekiel back to the temple entrance.

Water is already issuing:
- from below the temple entryway/threshold;
- toward the east;
- from the south side of the temple;
- past the south side of the altar.

The human observer is then:
- brought **out by the north gate**;
- led **around on the outside**;
- brought to the **outer east gate**.

There he sees the water trickling out on the south side.

This sequence matters because the immediately preceding gate rules have already established the outer east gate as shut after the divine entry.

The secure textual sequence is therefore:

**water originates inside**
→ moves eastward
→ crosses/reaches the exterior east side;

while:

**human observer inside**
→ exits north
→ circles externally
→ reaches the east exterior from another route.

## Differential-transfer result

Do not overstate the exact hydraulic path under or through the wall.

But the narrative securely distinguishes:

**water route**
from
**observer route**.

This is one of the strongest Vardath-compatible transfer patterns in Ezekiel because the user's model already requires the possibility that:
- water;
- atmosphere;
- debris;
- land;
- living beings

may not all traverse a changing boundary in the same way.

### Strong control

The text never says:

“Ezekiel cannot use the east gate because water alone can pass.”

That causal explanation is inferred from the gate context.

What is explicit is:
- Ezekiel is taken out north and around;
- water goes east;
- water is found emerging outside beside the east gate.

So classify:

**strong differential-route topology**
/
**mechanism unspecified**.

Stephen L. Cook's translation/commentary likewise renders the guide as taking Ezekiel out through the outer north gatehouse and around to the outer east gatehouse while the water emerges beside it.

# Ezekiel 47:3–5 — four equal distance measurements produce nonlinear depth increase

The measuring figure moves eastward.

At each stage he measures **1,000 cubits**.

Sequence:

1. 1,000 cubits → ankle depth;
2. another 1,000 → knee depth;
3. another 1,000 → waist depth;
4. another 1,000 → water too deep to cross on foot, deep enough for swimming.

The distance increments are equal.

The depth response is not.

## Process significance

This is an explicit example of:

**constant spatial increment**
→ **increasing system magnitude**
→ **threshold beyond ordinary crossing**.

The current begins as a small outflow and becomes an uncrossable river.

No tributaries are named in this vision.

Therefore the literary image presents **outward amplification** from the temple source.

### Vardath relevance

This can be compared to a post-activation expansion phase:

**localized source**
→ growing flow
→ widening/deepening influence
→ ordinary traversal threshold exceeded.

But it is not strict fingertrap reopening because:
- no broad web first contracts;
- the flowing water is not identified as the same material as the lattice;
- no narrow woven throat is shown changing orientation into a broad weave.

Classification:

**strong outward-amplification analogue**
/
**not strict reopening of the lattice**.

# SECTION 53C3b-2 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Ezekiel 47:1–12 is a visionary temple-river scene whose literary themes include divine presence, fertility, life and healing.
2. The text explicitly routes Ezekiel out by the north gate and around to the east exterior while the water moves east. It does **not** explicitly explain this as a physical permeability difference between human and water; that is the systems abstraction.
3. The outer east gate has already been declared shut in Ezekiel 44. Commentators commonly connect the north detour with that gate state, but preserve the distinction between explicit narrative and explanatory inference.
4. The four 1,000-cubit stages are explicit. No tributary is named in the vision, but absence of a named tributary does not establish a literal conservation-law anomaly.
5. Dubbink reads the river vision as a fertility/growth counterpart to the restrictive architecture and access material around it. This supports the literary transition from bounded centre to outward life, not a modern hydraulic model.
6. Cook treats the river as the “Sacred River” within the climactic 40–48 vision and translates the observer's north-gate detour and east-side emergence of the water explicitly.
7. Ezekiel 47:9 has a meaningful textual issue: the NRSVUE footnote records Hebrew “the two rivers go” against singular-style ancient-version readings. Bodi argues for a double-current tradition and ancient Near Eastern parallels. Preserve this as a textual/motif question, not proof of the Vardath paired lattice.
8. Sea-freshening, abundant fish, monthly fruit and healing leaves are visionary/theological images of restoration. They are structurally comparable to environmental activation but not independent geological evidence.
9. The retained salty marshes are explicit and provide a valuable negative control against universalized transformation.
10. Warren argues that 47:13–48:29 resembles temple-land tenure grants and concretizes relations among temple, people, priests, prince and land. This reinforces the importance of stable allocation as part of the vision's endpoint.
11. Kopilovitz identifies Ezekiel 47:22–23's land grant to resident aliens as a distinctive feature of the restoration program and explores a Neo-Babylonian economic background.
12. The parallel strip map and twelve-gated city are idealized visionary geography. They should not be treated as a surveyed modern map or a literal count of cosmic portals.
13. The final name “The Lord Is There” closes the vision with enduring presence. It is a theological endpoint, not by itself a physical measurement.
14. Strict fingertrap status remains negative: no connected broad weave is shown contracting into a narrow axial throat and then reopening as the same weave.

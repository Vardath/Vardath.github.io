### Strict verdict

**Obadiah is not a strict Vardath spiderweb→fingertrap→rod/throat→gate→reopening text. Its strongest process correspondences are vertical false-security reversal, boundary penetration, road-junction control, refugee interception, reciprocal deed-return, lineage/national pair recurrence and successor land redistribution. The high rock/eagle/star imagery ends in imposed descent rather than a connected weave narrowing into a throat. The Jerusalem gate is a city threshold; the crossroads are strategic chokepoints; the trap in verse 7 is an alliance/capture device; and Mount Zion’s final ascent belongs to restoration/governance. Jacob and Esau are a genuine paired lineage that Obadiah projects onto national houses, but the text does not show them as two strands of one physical lattice.**

# SECTION 57A1 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Working base text: Obadiah NRSVUE.
2. The book contains only 21 verses and is centered on Edom, Jerusalem’s calamity, reciprocal judgment and restoration/redistribution.
3. Obadiah 1–4 has close literary parallels with Jeremiah 49:14–16; Obadiah 5–6 also parallels Jeremiah 49:9–10 and v8 parallels Jeremiah 49:7. Scholarship has long debated whether Jeremiah used Obadiah, Obadiah used Jeremiah, or both drew from an older Edom oracle.
4. A modern Cambridge Dictionary of Judaism and Jewish Culture summary dates the book likely shortly after Babylon’s destruction of Jerusalem in 586 BCE and suggests Obadiah/Jeremiah may both use an older Edom oracle. Older scholarship and some conservative traditions have proposed earlier historical crises; do not pretend the date is unanimously settled.
5. The immediate historical referent of 10–14 is most commonly associated in modern scholarship with Jerusalem’s Babylonian destruction in 587/586 BCE, but Edomite participation and the exact historical degree of collusion remain subjects of reconstruction rather than direct neutral chronicle.
6. The rock/height language fits Edom’s mountainous terrain and rock shelters. Petra/Sela is relevant background, but Obadiah 3 should not be reduced to a technically exact description of Petra alone.
7. NRSVUE notes “clefts of Sela” as an alternate handling of v3.
8. The eagle/star nest is rhetorical extreme-height imagery. No literal astronomical location is implied by the text.
9. Verse 7’s rare Hebrew trap term is uncertain; NET notes possibilities including trap/net/fetter/stumbling block. Preserve capture-function without making exact apparatus geometry certain.
10. Jacob/Esau is both ancestral brother tradition and later national-house rhetoric. Genesis tradition identifies Esau with Edom and Jacob with Israel; Obadiah itself calls Jacob Edom’s “brother.”
11. Verse 11’s gate is Jerusalem’s city interface during invasion. It is a strong negative control against lexical gate = cosmological gate.
12. Verse 14’s crossroads/fork wording denotes strategic route control against fugitives. Some translation traditions use “breach” or “crossway,” but the secure function is interception of escapees.
13. Verse 15’s deed-return principle is moral/prophetic reciprocity; it is not evidence of physical material reversal.
14. Verse 16 has a grammatical/referential difficulty in the initial plural “you drank”; NET preserves several options. Do not over-map the drinkers.
15. Verse 17 changes Mount Zion into escape/holy/restored-possession space.
16. Verse 18 assigns fire/flame/stubble roles to national houses; this is poetic process imagery, not direct evidence of plasma physics.
17. Verse 20 is textually difficult. NRSVUE’s “Halah” is a conjectural handling; NET preserves fortress/army possibilities and notes uncertainty in Sepharad’s location.
18. Verse 21 has a textual variation: NRSVUE “those who have been saved,” with Greek/Syriac supporting “saviors”; other translations use “deliverers.”
19. Territorial redistribution in vv19–21 should be treated as geopolitical/restoration mapping before any cosmological interpretation.
20. Strict fingertrap status remains negative.

# SECTION 53B2b-2a textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Strine and Crouch argue that Ezekiel systematically recasts Egypt/Pharaoh using chaos-monster imagery, especially in chapters 29–32.
2. Therefore Pharaoh's *tannin* is mythopolitical imagery before any zoological/cosmological identification.
3. “Crocodile” is a plausible cultural comparison, but the safe structural result is large central river creature.
4. Fish attached to scales function as dependent people/system elements in the political metaphor.
5. Egypt as reed staff is explicit failed-support rhetoric tied to Judah's reliance on Egypt.
6. Historical reconstruction of Ezekiel 29's forty-year devastation is difficult; there is no secure basis for using it as a literal cosmological cycle.
7. Lydia Lee's work on Ezekiel's foreign-nation oracles emphasizes the close rhetorical relation between Egypt's fate and Judah/Israel's identity/restoration.
8. Ezekiel 29:17–21 is later-dated than surrounding material, demonstrating topical rather than strict chronological arrangement.
9. Ezekiel 30's arm imagery is a political/military capacity metaphor.
10. The broken arm “not bound for healing” is nevertheless a very concrete repair-state image and can safely support abstract mechanics comparison.
11. Old Greek/MT differences remain active controls in this section; no single unusual phrase should bear the physical model alone.

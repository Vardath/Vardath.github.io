### Strict verdict

**Amos 7 is not a strict spiderweb→fingertrap text. It is, however, one of the most methodologically important chapters so far because its apparently strongest geometry—the plumb-line wall—is philologically uncertain. The secure process sequence is stronger than the object identification: two catastrophic futures are shown, intercession cancels each, then a third symbolic vision marks a threshold after which YHWH will no longer pass over/spare Israel. The wall is secure, but *’anak* may be plumb/lead, tin, a tin-wall symbol, or part of another wordplay; therefore vertical-axis measurement can only be a conditional reading. The second half adds institutional routing mechanics: prophecy is reclassified as conspiracy, the message is treated as more than the land can bear, the royal sanctuary attempts to eject the messenger geographically, and Amos grounds his authority in an external role reassignment. None of this supplies a broad connected weave narrowing through retained strands into a throat and reopening.**

# SECTION 56C7 textual / historical / scholarly controls

1. Working base text: Amos 7 NRSVUE.
2. The first vision is specifically timed to the latter growth after the king’s mowings. Exact agrarian administration is debated; preserve the phase sequence without inventing taxation detail.
3. Visions 1 and 2 are explicitly cancelled after Amos’s intercession. They must not be entered into a literal physical timeline as realized events.
4. “Jacob is small” functions as a vulnerability/resilience argument.
5. The second vision’s fire consumes the “great deep” and land. This is extreme visionary imagery; no physical mechanism is specified, and the outcome is then cancelled.
6. The third vision’s Hebrew **’anak** is a major lexical problem. NRSVUE and many traditional translations retain “plumb line”; NET translates “tin.”
7. Hwi Cho (2015) summarizes the post-Landsberger debate and states that an uncontroversial meaning has not been reached. His own Assyrian-judgment proposal is one contribution to the debate, not a settled solution.
8. NET notes, citing Shalom M. Paul and Andersen/Freedman, preserve multiple options including tin wall, tin/grief wordplay and the traditional plumb-line interpretation.
9. Therefore any vertical measurement geometry derived from Amos 7:7–8 must be labelled **conditional on the plumb-line reading**.
10. The secure object relation is wall + repeated rare noun + object in operator’s hand + placement among Israel.
11. The Hebrew behind “spare no longer” can be represented literally as no longer “pass over” them. In context this is forbearance/judgment idiom and must not be confused with literal Passover geometry.
12. Bethel is explicitly called the king’s sanctuary and temple of the kingdom in 7:13. Working Preacher highlights the coupling of royal and cultic power.
13. Amaziah’s report says Jeroboam himself will die by sword, whereas the vision in 7:9 targets the “house of Jeroboam.” Preserve the distinction as a possible relay sharpening/paraphrase.
14. Amos 7:14 has a tense ambiguity reflected in NRSVUE footnotes (“am”/“was”). Do not overstate whether Amos had never been a prophet, was not a professional prophet, or was describing prior occupation.
15. Herding and sycamore tending are secure elements of Amos’s self-description; source reassignment to prophecy is explicit in 7:15.
16. “The land cannot bear all his words” is political/information-load metaphor, not literal geophysical loading.
17. Land being parceled “by line” in 7:17 must not be assumed to use the same object/word as disputed *’anak* in 7:7–8.
18. Exile remains the dominant social-historical transfer mechanism in the chapter.
19. Strict fingertrap status remains negative.

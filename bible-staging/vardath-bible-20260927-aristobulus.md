### Aristobulus son of Hyrcanus

When Aristobulus became king, he displayed the splendor and authority of kingship and wore a great crown.

He drew his brother Antigonus close to himself and entrusted many of the affairs of the kingdom to him.

Antigonus went out against peoples who had rebelled, subdued them, and restored them to obedience.

He then returned victorious to Jerusalem.

While Antigonus had been away at war, King Aristobulus had become gravely ill.

When Antigonus heard of his brother's sickness, he did not go first to the palace.

Instead he went to the house of God to give thanks for the victory God had granted him and to pray that his brother the king might recover.

It was the time of the Feast of Tabernacles.

Antigonus entered Jerusalem wearing a finely made gilded coat of armor and carrying his weapons.

As the Jews saw him walking through the city in this splendid equipment, they marveled at his appearance, his strength, and his bearing, and many praised him.

Among the Jews was an aged man counted among those regarded as righteous.

When he saw Antigonus walking through Jerusalem while the people looked upon him with admiration, he said to his disciple:

“My son, I wish that I had died before this day and had not lived to see the death of this young man.

“For I was shown that he would be killed at Strato's Tower.”

Strato was then known as a city on the coast in which there stood a famous tower, and the name was not commonly applied elsewhere.

The disciple answered:

“How can this be?

“Strato's Tower is far from Jerusalem, much of the day has already passed, and Antigonus is here in the holy city.

“How then can he be killed there today?”

The old man lamented and wondered whether what had been shown to him would fail.

Meanwhile certain men who hated Antigonus and wished to destroy him went to Aristobulus.

They said:

“Your brother Antigonus has set his heart upon your throne.

“He did not come to you when he entered the city, but went instead to the temple so that he might win the people to himself.

“He is there with his companions, armed and prepared, arranging what he will do if your illness grows worse.”

These words entered the heart of Aristobulus.

He ordered armed guards to take positions along every passage leading into his palace.

He commanded them to kill anyone who attempted to enter while carrying weapons and not to hesitate or ask questions.

Then Aristobulus sent a messenger to Antigonus, ordering him to remove his armor and weapons and come to him without delay.

But the wife of Aristobulus hated Antigonus bitterly and desired his death.

She bribed the messenger whom the king had sent and commanded him not to deliver the message as it had been given.

Instead he was to reverse its meaning and tell Antigonus:

“The king has heard how splendid your armor looked when you entered the holy place, and he desires to see you dressed in it.

“Come to him at once in that same equipment. Do not remove any of it and do not delay.”

The messenger went to Antigonus and delivered the false message.

Antigonus believed that it had come from his brother.

He therefore came toward the palace still wearing his armor and carrying his weapons, knowing nothing of the order Aristobulus had given to the guards.

Beside the palace of Aristobulus there stood a tower that had recently been built.

It too was called Strato's Tower because it resembled the famous tower in the coastal city.

When Antigonus reached that place, the guards saw him armed and killed him according to the king's command.

Thus the words of the aged man concerning the death of Antigonus at Strato's Tower were fulfilled.

### The Death of Aristobulus

When Aristobulus learned that his brother had been killed through deception and that the command he had given in suspicion had caused his death, grief overwhelmed him.

His sorrow increased, and he was seized by severe illness.

His chest became diseased, and he began to vomit large quantities of blood.

His servants tried to care for him, but the bleeding did not stop.

They placed some of the blood in a basin and sent a servant to carry it to the physician so that he might examine it and determine what treatment could be given.

As the servant passed the place where Antigonus had been killed, he slipped.

The basin fell from his hand, and the blood of Aristobulus poured out upon the place where the blood of Antigonus had been shed.

When the king's servants saw what had happened, they marveled and said:

“Glory to the just King, who has caused the blood of the oppressor to fall upon the blood of the wronged.”

When Aristobulus heard of this, his remorse became still greater.

He continued grieving and condemning what he had done until, after a short time, he died.

All his people mourned him.

The account remembers him as a man who had been formidable against his enemies and had shown care toward his nation.

Aristobulus ruled for one year.

After him his brother Alexander became king.

In this way the dream of Hyrcanus was fulfilled, in which he had seen that Alexander would reign after him.

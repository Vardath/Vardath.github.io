## 1 Meqabyan 3

**1.** A voice came from heaven, saying, “Abya, Sila, and Fentos, servants of the Most High God: just as you have seen these who died and rose again before you, so you also shall rise after your death, and your faces shall shine in the kingdom of heaven.

**2.** “Go with these men, and there become witnesses unto death.”

**3.** Then they prayed, gave thanks, and bowed before the LORD. Neither death nor the judgment of the king made them afraid.

**4.** They went to those lawless men without fear, becoming like innocent sheep. When they reached them, the soldiers seized them, struck them, bound them, scourged them, brought them to the king, and made them stand before him.

**5.** The king answered them, saying, “You rebels! Why will you not sacrifice to my gods or bow before them?”

**6.** The holy, blessed, chosen, and joyful brothers—Abya, Sila, and Fentos, precious like costly pearls—answered him together with one voice.

**7.** They said to that wicked king, “We will neither sacrifice nor bow down to unclean gods that possess neither heart nor understanding.

**8.** “They are gold and silver, wood and stone, works made by human hands. They have no soul, no mind, and no knowledge. They cannot reward one who does good to them, nor harm one who does evil against them.”

**9.** The king answered, “Why do you behave in this way and insult my honored gods? They know those who wrong them and those who speak against them.”

**10.** They replied, “We speak against them and will not honor them, because before us they are nothing.”

**11.** The king said, “According to the evil of your conduct I will judge you. I will destroy your beautiful appearance through great torment, scourging, and fire.

**12.** “Tell me now whether you will sacrifice to my gods or not. Otherwise I will bring against you the scourge, the binding cord, and the sword.”

**13.** They answered him, “We will not bow down or sacrifice to unclean gods.” Then the king ordered them beaten with heavy rods, afterward scourged, and then torn open until the inward parts of their bodies could be seen.

**14.** After this they were bound and placed in the prison house, within confinement, while the king considered how he might condemn and kill them.

**15.** They were taken into prison and bound with severe bonds without mercy, and they remained there three days and three nights.

**16.** After the third day the king ordered a herald to go about and summon the nobles, counselors, governors, and elders of the land.

**17.** When King Tsiruts-Saydan sat in the public court, he commanded that the blessed Abya, Sila, and Fentos be brought. They stood before him bound and wounded.

**18.** The king said to them, “During these three days have you chosen what is better, or do you still remain in your former evil?”

**19.** The blessed servants and soldiers of the LORD answered, “We remain steadfast. We will not say yes to what you demand, for your words are blasphemy filled with evil and uncleanness.”

**20.** The tyrant became enraged and ordered them suspended and their wounds reopened, and their blood flowed upon the ground.

**21.** Again he ordered burning brands to be applied to them so that their flesh was scorched, and it was done. The blessed men said to him, “Speak on, fool! In the same measure that you multiply our torments, our reward is multiplied.”

**22.** Again he commanded that savage beasts be brought—leopards, bears, and lions—and that they be kept without food before being driven against the brothers, so that they might consume them entirely, even their bones.

**23.** He ordered the keepers of the beasts to release them upon the martyrs. They did as they were commanded. The holy witnesses were bound fast, and they were secured to the ground with stakes.

**24.** The beasts rushed upon them roaring; but when they reached the martyrs, they bowed before them and did them homage.

**25.** Then the beasts turned back toward their keepers, still roaring, terrified the guards, and drove them until they came before the king.

**26.** There they killed [seventy-five] of the lawless men. *The numeral is damaged in the Beta maṣāḥǝft transcription and is restored from parallel witnesses.*

**27.** Many others were crushed as, in terror, they pressed one against another. Even the king abandoned his throne and fled. With great difficulty the beasts were seized and returned to their enclosures.

**28.** Then the two younger brothers of Abya, Sila, and Fentos came and released them from their bonds, saying, “Come, let us flee before these wicked men find us.” *The Beta maṣāḥǝft line is truncated after its opening word; the event is restored from parallel witnesses.*

**29.** But the martyrs answered their brothers, “It is not fitting for us to flee after we have taken our stand for testimony. If you are afraid, go and flee by yourselves.”

**30.** Their younger brothers said, “We too will stand with you before the king, and if you die, we also will die with you.”

**31.** From the upper part of his royal house the king saw that the blessed men had been freed and that all five brothers now stood together. His officers learned that they were brothers and reported it to him, and in his fury the king roared like a wild beast. *The Beta maṣāḥǝft line breaks off mid-sentence after the king sees them standing; the remainder is restored from parallel witnesses.*

**32.** The king commanded that the five brothers be seized and thrown into prison until he could decide how to punish them. They were cast into the prison house and bound without mercy in heavy wooden restraints.

**33.** King Tsiruts-Saydan said, “These deluded young men have exhausted me! How firm are the hearts of these rebels. Their evil conduct is as stubborn as their strength. Whenever I think they will turn back, they harden their hearts all the more.

**34.** “I will repay them according to the evil of their conduct. I will burn their flesh with fire until it becomes ash, and I will scatter the ashes of their bodies like dust upon a great mountain so that the wind may carry them away.”

**35.** After saying this he waited three days, then commanded that the holy men be brought. When they came near, he ordered a great furnace and a deep pit to be set ablaze, loading it with resin, pitch, fat, sulfur, and other substances that would make the fire burn fiercely.

**36.** When the furnace was blazing, the king’s servants went to him and said, “We have done what you commanded. Send the men who are to be cast into it.”

**37.** He ordered that they be taken and thrown into the fiery furnace. The soldiers carried out the king’s command, and when the blessed men entered the fire, they gave their souls to the LORD.

**38.** Angels received them before the eyes of those who had cast them in, and carried their souls into the bosom of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, into the paradise of joy.

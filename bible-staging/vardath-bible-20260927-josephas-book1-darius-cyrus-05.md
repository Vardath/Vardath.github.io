### Darius the Mede and Cyrus the Persian

After the dominion of the Chaldeans had grown proud, two great kings rose against them: Darius king of Media and Cyrus king of Persia. Cyrus had taken the daughter of Darius as his wife, and the two kingdoms were joined in alliance.

Belshazzar, who ruled Babylon after Nebuchadnezzar, persisted in rebellion against them. Darius and Cyrus therefore marched against him with great armies.

When Belshazzar heard that they were approaching, he sent an army to meet them. Darius and Cyrus defeated that force and drove it back until they came to a place about a day’s journey from Babylon, where they made their camp.

Belshazzar then sent out another large force under trusted commanders. They left Babylon late in the day, marched through the night, and came upon the camp of Darius and Cyrus before dawn. They struck the army suddenly, killed many men, and drove the kings’ forces back.

Belshazzar’s soldiers returned to Babylon with spoil and with news of victory. Belshazzar rejoiced greatly and prepared a magnificent feast for his commanders and nobles. He honored them richly, sat with them at the drinking table, and continued drinking deep into the night.

When wine had overcome him, Belshazzar commanded that the vessels of gold and silver which Nebuchadnezzar had taken from the holy house of God in Jerusalem should be brought before him. The vessels were brought, and Belshazzar drank from them with his commanders, his women, and the people of his household. They praised their idols and gave thanks to them for the victory.

Because Belshazzar profaned the vessels of the holy place, God sent a sign against him. A hand appeared opposite the lamp and wrote upon the wall of the palace. Belshazzar saw the form of the hand and its fingers writing, and terror seized him.

The writing was brought before the wise men, but its meaning was not made clear until Daniel interpreted it. Daniel declared that Belshazzar’s kingdom had reached its end and that his dominion would pass to the kings of Media and Persia.

When Darius and Cyrus learned what Daniel had declared concerning the fall of Belshazzar, they took courage and prepared again for war. They acknowledged the power of God and gave thanks to him. Cyrus also vowed that, if God delivered Babylon into his hand, he would rebuild the house of God in Jerusalem, return the sacred vessels, release the Jewish captives, and permit them to return to their own land.

Darius and Cyrus marched again against Babylon. They entered the city and struck its people with a great slaughter. Thus the words spoken concerning the vengeance of God upon the Chaldeans and the people of Babylon were fulfilled.

The two kings divided the Chaldean dominion between them. Darius took the city of Babylon and its royal palace, while Cyrus received the remainder of the Chaldean territories around it. Their authority was established over the land.

Darius was already an old man, and he did not live long after these events. When he died, the nobles of Media and Persia agreed together and placed the whole dominion in the hand of Cyrus.

### The Decree of Cyrus

In the first year of his sole rule, Cyrus summoned the elders and leaders of the Jewish exile. He told them of the vow he had made concerning Jerusalem and the captives of Israel.

Cyrus said to them, “Whoever among the whole captivity of Judah desires to go up to Jerusalem to rebuild the house which Nebuchadnezzar destroyed, let him go. Let him seek help from God, for God is with him. I, Cyrus, am the servant of the great God, and I will provide what is required for the building of his house, for he gave me victory over the Chaldeans and delivered their kingdom into my hand.”

When the Jews heard the words of Cyrus, they rejoiced greatly and gave thanks to God for his kindness toward them.

A great company went up to Jerusalem. With them were Ezra the priest, Nehemiah, Mordecai, Joshua son of Jozadak, Zerubbabel, and the chiefs of the exile.

They rebuilt the house of God according to the measure Cyrus had commanded, established the altar upon its foundations, and offered sacrifices before it.

Each year Cyrus supplied what they required for the service of the house of God: wheat, oil, wine, bulls, sheep, and other provisions. He also gave them much money. This support continued under the Persian dominion.

God stretched out his hand to Cyrus and gave him victory over nations and kingdoms. Strong fortresses fell before him, and the treasures and hidden wealth of lands were opened to him. Wherever he turned, he prospered and prevailed.

The author says that God granted these victories to Cyrus because of the kindness he had shown to the people of Israel and because he had rebuilt the holy house of God.

### Kinanus the Roman Commander

There was at that time a Roman commander named Kinanus.

He had left the land of Rome and was traveling toward Syria.

When word reached him of what Alexander son of Aristobulus had done, Kinanus marched toward Jerusalem to make war against him.

Hyrcanus and those who were with him joined Kinanus.

Alexander came out from Jerusalem to meet them in battle, but they defeated him.

He fled and took refuge in a fortress called Alexandrion, where he fortified himself.

Kinanus advanced against the fortress, surrounded it, and pressed Alexander hard.

At last Alexander came out seeking terms and safety.

Kinanus received him and treated him kindly.

### Aristobulus Escapes from Rome

During those days Aristobulus, brother of Hyrcanus and father of Alexander, escaped from Rome.

One of his sons, Antigonus, escaped with him.

They returned to the land of the Jews, and a great multitude gathered to Aristobulus.

Kinanus marched against him and defeated him in battle.

Many of the men with Aristobulus were killed.

Aristobulus was captured and sent back as a prisoner to Rome.

### Caesar Releases Aristobulus

Pompey remained powerful among the Romans until Caesar rose against him and gained the upper hand in the struggle for rule.

When Caesar learned that Pompey was gathering his forces to make war against him, he released Aristobulus from imprisonment and treated him with favor.

Caesar placed two commanders with Aristobulus and gave him twelve thousand men.

He ordered him to go to Syria and to the land of the Jews.

Aristobulus was to bring the people there away from obedience to Pompey and into obedience to Caesar, and to secure their help in the war against Pompey.

### The Death of Aristobulus

When Aristobulus left Rome, Pompey feared what he might accomplish.

Pompey therefore wrote to Antipater and ordered him to devise a way to remove Aristobulus.

Antipater summoned several leading men from Jerusalem.

He instructed them to go out to meet Aristobulus and to say that the leaders of Jerusalem had sent them to greet him, offer their obedience, and welcome his return.

Antipater also gave them deadly poison and told them to find an opportunity to administer it to Aristobulus.

The men traveled into Syria and met Aristobulus there.

Aristobulus received them gladly and honored them.

They remained with him for several days.

Then they carried out the scheme that had been entrusted to them and secretly gave him the poison.

Aristobulus died and was buried in Syria.

The account says that his former reign had lasted three years and a half.

It remembers him as a courageous, powerful, and generous man.

### Kinanus Restores Roman Authority

Before these events Kinanus had written to the authorities in Rome asking that the remaining children of Aristobulus be released.

Their mother had appealed to him on their behalf.

They were therefore released and returned to Jerusalem.

After Pompey had gone far from Jerusalem, some of the Jews broke the agreement of obedience they had made with Rome.

Kinanus marched against them and fought them.

He defeated them and brought them again under Roman authority.

### Kinanus in Egypt

Afterward Kinanus received word that the people of Egypt had rebelled against their king Ptolemy.

They had driven him out and had refused to continue sending tribute to Rome.

Kinanus therefore marched into Egypt, and Antipater went with him.

They fought against the Egyptians and killed a great many of them.

Ptolemy was restored to his kingdom, and the affairs of Egypt were brought again into order.

Kinanus then returned to Jerusalem.

He renewed the authority of Hyrcanus over the Jews.

He also advanced Antipater, raised his standing, and gave him greater honor.

After this Kinanus returned to Rome.

### Queen Alexandra and Her Sons

When Alexandra became queen, she summoned the leading Pharisees.

She entrusted to them the direction of the people and the ordering of many public affairs.

She gave them authority, honored their leaders, released those of their party who were in prison, and showed kindness to them.

She also sent for those who had fled from Jerusalem during the days of Hyrcanus and during the reign of her husband Alexander and permitted them to return.

She turned away from the teaching of the Sadducees and restored the customs and teaching of the Pharisees.

When her sons had grown, Alexandra appointed the elder, Hyrcanus, to be high priest.

Hyrcanus was humble, gentle, and peaceable.

She appointed the younger son, Aristobulus, to command the army, for he was bold and powerful in war.

Alexandra strengthened the army and sent forces among the peoples who were subject to her.

She took leading men from among them as hostages for their obedience.

They continued to bring tribute and gifts each year.

In this way her kingdom became settled and her power increased.

### The Pharisees Take Vengeance

As the authority of the Pharisees grew, their leaders came to Alexandra together with her son Hyrcanus.

They reminded her of the suffering that King Alexander had inflicted upon them after he followed the counsel of the Sadducees.

They spoke of the elders and leaders of their party who had been killed and crucified.

They asked the queen to permit them to take vengeance upon those Sadducee leaders whom they held responsible.

Alexandra answered that they should act according to what they judged necessary.

The Pharisees then seized a prominent Sadducee who had been among those who urged Alexander against them.

They killed him and also killed others whom they blamed for the former persecution.

### The Sadducees Appeal to Alexandra

When the leading Sadducees and many of the commanders learned what was happening, they went together to Alexandra.

Aristobulus went with them.

They said to her:

“You know the hardships we endured with King Alexander in his wars.

“We continually gave ourselves to his service, fought his enemies, and remained loyal to him until he prevailed and his kingdom was established.

“How then can your majesty forget all this and show no regard for the service we gave?

“You have raised our enemies, the Pharisees, over us and have given them power until they have begun to accomplish their desire against us.

“You know that they were the enemies of Alexander and that they fought him while he lived.

“If our former service and loyalty have any value before you, protect us now.

“Restrain the Pharisees from harming us.

“If you will not do this, then permit us to leave Jerusalem and dwell in distant estates where we will not see our enemies and they will not see us.”

They wept bitterly before the queen.

Alexandra also wept with them.

Aristobulus supported their plea.

The queen was troubled and did not know how best to resolve the conflict.

At last she said to the Sadducees:

“Leave Jerusalem and go wherever you choose.

“The Pharisees are not to pursue you there.”

The Sadducees therefore departed from Jerusalem.

With them went many of the leading soldiers, commanders, and mighty men of the army.

They spread themselves through the estates and strong places outside the city.

Alexandra had hoped in this way to separate the parties and end their hostility.

Instead, the removal of so many commanders and influential men from Jerusalem weakened her position and gathered a large body of supporters away from the capital.

### The Last Days of Alexandra

After some time Alexandra became seriously ill with the sickness from which she would die.

She had ruled for nine years and had reached the age of seventy-three.

The account describes her as devout, upright in conduct, and generally wise in government.

It records no great fault against her administration except the troubles that arose from her handling of the Sadducees and the conflict between the parties.

When Aristobulus learned that his mother was gravely ill, he left Jerusalem secretly by night.

He went to the estates where the Sadducees and their supporters had settled.

He told them of the queen's condition and called upon them to support him in taking the kingship.

They agreed.

When news of this reached Alexandra, she ordered that the wife and children of Aristobulus be seized and held under guard.

But the strength of Aristobulus increased as the Sadducee leaders and soldiers gathered to him.

Men also came to him from several districts of the Jews, and he assembled a large army.

Those who remained with Alexandra became greatly afraid.

They entered the chamber where she lay ill and told her how strong Aristobulus had become and how weak the position of Hyrcanus appeared.

Alexandra answered:

“I am at the edge of death, and concern for the matters of this life is leaving me.

“What more am I able to do in this condition?

“Our soldiers, our resources, and the royal treasury are in your hands.

“Arrange the matter according to what you judge right, and seek the help of God in what you do.”

That same day Queen Alexandra died.

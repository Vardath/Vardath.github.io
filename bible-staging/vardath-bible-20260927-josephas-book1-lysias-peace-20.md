### Lysias Comes Against Judah

When Eupator son of Antiochus heard what Judah and his companions had done, he sent Lysias, a kinsman of the king and commander of his army, against the Jews.

Lysias came with a great force and laid siege to a city of Judah.

When Judah heard of it, he commanded his companions to fast and pray.

They asked God for help against their enemies.

Then Judah went out with his men to meet the army of the Greeks.

When the Jews saw how numerous the enemy was, fear came upon them.

As they looked, they saw what appeared to be a horseman clothed in shining garments like gold, moving toward the army of the Greeks as though he were going to fight against them.

Judah said that this was a messenger sent by God to strengthen them.

The hearts of those with him took courage.

That night they attacked the camp of the Greeks.

They struck many of the enemy, and fear fell upon the rest.

The Greek army was broken, and Lysias fled.

### Peace under Eupator

Afterward Lysias sent to Judah and asked for peace.

He promised that the Greeks would no longer persecute the Jews or interfere with them in the observance of their religion.

Judah answered that he would accept the agreement if Eupator the king confirmed it.

Lysias therefore wrote to Eupator and told him what had happened.

He described the courage of the Jews and the firmness with which they defended their law and their land.

He told the king that continued war against them would consume the royal army without bringing the Jews into submission.

Lysias advised Eupator to make peace with them and to leave them free in the practice of their religion.

Eupator agreed.

He wrote to Judah and to the leaders of the Jews, confirming the peace and promising that they would not be opposed in matters of their law.

The agreement was established between them.

For a time the Jews had rest, and Judah gave his attention to the welfare of the people and the ordering of their affairs.

### Aretas Takes Up the Cause of Hyrcanus

Antipater then began to urge Aretas to make war against Aristobulus and to help Hyrcanus recover the kingdom.

At first Aretas refused.

He remembered the injuries that Alexander, the father of Hyrcanus and Aristobulus, had formerly inflicted upon him.

Alexander had taken lands from the Arab king, killed many of his men, and ravaged his territory.

Antipater did not cease persuading him.

He spoke continually against Aristobulus and told Aretas that many among the Jews hated Aristobulus and desired the return of Hyrcanus.

At last Aretas agreed to support Hyrcanus.

He required Hyrcanus to restore all the estates and lands that his father Alexander had taken from the Arabs.

Hyrcanus promised to do so and entered into covenant with Aretas.

Aretas then marched with Hyrcanus at the head of a great army against Aristobulus.

Aristobulus came out to meet them with a large force of his own.

When the armies confronted one another, many of the men who had been with Aristobulus deserted to Hyrcanus.

Only a smaller company remained faithful to Aristobulus.

Seeing this, Aristobulus withdrew from the battle.

He fled by night into Jerusalem and fortified himself there.

Hyrcanus and Aretas came after him with their armies.

Those who had deserted Aristobulus joined them.

They surrounded Jerusalem, and war continued between the two parties.

Many of the priests and a large part of the people remained with Aristobulus inside the city.

### The Passover Animals during the Siege

While the siege continued, the Feast of Passover approached.

The priests with Aristobulus needed cattle and sheep for the sacrifices required by the law, but they had none within the besieged place.

They called out to the Jews who were with Hyrcanus:

“You know what God has commanded concerning the sacrifices.

“We do not have among us the cattle and sheep needed to offer what is required.

“Fear God and do not cause his appointed service to cease.

“Give us from the cattle and sheep outside according to what is required, and we will pay you the price demanded.”

Those outside answered that they would provide an animal only if a thousand dinars were paid for each head.

Aristobulus and the priests agreed.

They gathered the money and lowered it from the fortified place to those outside.

But when the besiegers received the money, they did not send up the animals they had promised.

The priests were greatly distressed.

They cried out to God concerning the deceit that had been done and concerning the interruption of the appointed offerings.

### The Prayer of Yohanan

At that time there was an elderly man named Yohanan.

He was renowned for righteousness, devotion, and prayer.

The people of his generation honored him greatly and believed that God often answered his petitions.

Some of the Jews who were with Hyrcanus found Yohanan near the city and brought him into their camp.

Their leader said to him:

“We know that your prayer is accepted.

“We want you to call upon God against Aristobulus and against the priests who are with him, asking God to give us victory over them.”

Yohanan answered:

“How could I pray against them?

“They are the people of God and his priests.

“What is right is that we pray for all of them and ask God to set their affairs in order.”

The men continued pressing him to curse Aristobulus and the priests.

Yohanan refused.

When they threatened him with death, he raised his hands toward heaven and prayed:

“O Lord our God, you created these people, and their hearts are in your hand to turn wherever you will.

“I ask you to set right the hearts of your people and the hearts of their leaders.

“Bring their purposes together in obedience to you.

“Do not let one faction prevail over another in wickedness.

“Make them helpers of one another in goodness and righteousness.

“Remove evil from among them, put an end to their hatred, and turn the heart of each toward the other so that together they may do what pleases you and draws them near to you.

“Do not aid the people against your priests, and do not aid your priests against your people so that either may bring destruction upon the other.”

When the men saw that Yohanan would not pray as they demanded, they fell upon him and killed him.

The account says that God was angered by the murder of the righteous man and that many among them afterward died.

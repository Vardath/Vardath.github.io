Demetrius, the commander at Antioch, marched against Simon.

Simon met him in battle, defeated him, and killed the greater part of his army.

After this the Romans did not return to make war against the Jews for the rest of Simon's days.

Simon ruled for eight years.

Then Ptolemy, Simon's brother-in-law and the husband of his sister, rose against him.

He killed Simon and seized Simon's wife and two of his sons.

After Simon, his son Hyrcanus took authority.

His own name was Yohanan.

During one of his father's wars he had killed a mighty warrior named Hyrcanus, and his father afterwards called him by that name because of the courage and strength he had shown.

### Hyrcanus son of Simon

Hyrcanus was the fifth ruler from the house of the Hasmoneans and the first among the Maccabees to be called king.

When Hyrcanus learned what Ptolemy had done in killing his father and seizing his mother and his brothers, he feared that Ptolemy would also seize him, and he fled to Gaza.

Ptolemy pursued him, but the people of Gaza protected Hyrcanus and fought against Ptolemy.

Ptolemy therefore went to Dagon and remained there with Hyrcanus's mother and brothers.

When Ptolemy withdrew from Gaza, Hyrcanus returned to Jerusalem and took the place of his father.

He gathered an army and marched against Ptolemy, the husband of his aunt, who was in Dagon.

Hyrcanus besieged him and began to break down the wall.

Ptolemy feared that the stronghold would be taken.

He brought Hyrcanus's mother and brothers up onto the fortification and ordered that they be beaten before Hyrcanus's eyes.

When Hyrcanus saw them, compassion overcame him and he was ready to withdraw.

But his mother called to him:

"My son, do not let your pity for me and for your brothers keep you from taking vengeance upon your enemy.

"Fight him, avenge your father, and complete what you came to do.

"Do not turn back until you have carried out your purpose.

"What you fear for us, and what he threatens to do to us, cannot in the end be escaped."

When Hyrcanus heard his mother's words, he renewed the attack with determination.

Ptolemy then increased the suffering of Hyrcanus's mother and brothers.

He swore that he would cast them down from the top of the stronghold to the ground if Hyrcanus did not stop fighting him.

Hyrcanus could not bear to become the cause of their death, so he ceased the attack.

While these things were happening, the Feast of Tabernacles arrived.

Hyrcanus returned to Jerusalem to keep the feast.

When Ptolemy saw that Hyrcanus had gone far from him, he killed Hyrcanus's mother and brothers and fled into a distant land.

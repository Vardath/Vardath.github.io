### Herod Is Crowned at Rome

The rulers of Rome brought Herod before them and ordered that a crown be placed upon his head.

He was led publicly through the city while trumpets sounded before him.

A proclamation was made that Augustus had appointed Herod ruler over the Jews and over the land of Jerusalem.

When this had been done, Herod returned to the presence of Augustus.

Augustus, Antony, and Herod then went together to the house of Antony.

Antony had prepared a great feast.

He invited Augustus, the elders of Rome, and many of the leading men.

They ate, drank, and rejoiced because Herod had been established as ruler.

Augustus also made a covenant concerning Herod.

It was written upon bronze tablets and placed publicly in one of the Roman temples so that the decree could be seen and remembered.

Thus the beginning of Herod's rule was publicly established at Rome.

### Herod Returns toward the East

After these things Antony departed by sea with a great army for another campaign.

Herod went with him.

When they reached Antioch, Antony continued with the greater part of the army.

Herod took the remaining forces toward Syria so that he could make war against Antigonus son of Aristobulus.

When Antigonus learned that Herod was returning, he went toward the mountains of Seir.

He desired to seize the household of Herod and his brother Joseph, who were staying in the fortress there.

Antigonus surrounded the fortress and cut off its water.

The thirst of those inside became severe.

Joseph considered breaking out of the fortress.

Others among the household began to consider surrendering themselves to Antigonus.

### Rain Saves the Fortress

But during those days a great rain fell.

For three nights the rain continued heavily.

The cisterns and reservoirs of the fortress were filled.

When the people saw that they again had water, their courage returned.

Joseph and the men with him remained in the fortress and resisted Antigonus.

Antigonus was unable to take the stronghold and eventually withdrew without gaining what he had desired.

### Herod Drives Antigonus Back to Jerusalem

News of these events reached Herod.

He hurried toward the mountains of Seir.

There he came upon Antigonus and fought him.

Joseph and his men came out from the fortress and joined the battle.

They closed upon Antigonus from both sides.

A great part of his army was killed.

Antigonus was defeated and fled toward Jerusalem.

Herod pursued him with a large force.

Jews gathered to Herod from many places as he advanced, and his strength increased.

When Herod came near Jerusalem, Antigonus shut the gates against him and fortified the city.

He continued the war from within the walls.

### Antigonus Bribes the Roman Commanders

Antigonus sent large sums of money to leading men among the Roman forces.

He asked them not to assist Herod in the war.

Some of them accepted his money and held back their support.

Even so, Herod continued the struggle with the forces that had gathered to him.

The war between Herod and Antigonus continued for a long time.

Neither side was immediately able to bring it to an end.

### Antony Sends Sosius to Herod

Herod appealed again to Antony for stronger assistance against Antigonus.

Antony received him with favor and honored him.

He sent one of his senior commanders, named Sosius, with a large army to assist Herod.

Antony also wrote to the Roman and Syrian authorities throughout the lands from Damascus toward the Euphrates and as far as the regions bordering Armenia.

He declared that Augustus, Antony, and the elders at Rome had appointed Herod ruler over the Jews.

He commanded the people under Roman authority to provide armed men and assistance for Herod's campaign.

Those who refused the order were warned that they would be punished.

Antony then continued toward Egypt by the coastal road.

Herod and Sosius gathered the Roman and Syrian forces and prepared to march again toward Jerusalem.

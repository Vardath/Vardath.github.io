### Antigonus Seeks Aid from the King of Persia

After Augustus and Antony had returned to Rome, Antigonus son of Aristobulus went with a company of Jews to the king of Persia.

He asked the king to help him take the kingdom from his uncle Hyrcanus.

He promised a great reward if the king would send an army with him against Jerusalem, place the rule of Judah in his hands, deliver Hyrcanus to him, and destroy Herod and his brother Phasael.

The account gives the promised reward as one thousand talents of coined gold together with eight hundred young women from among the daughters of Judah and of its leading families.

The king agreed to his request.

He sent a great army with Antigonus into the land of Syria.

They took cities there and killed Roman governors and others who resisted them.

The king's forces then remained in Syria while one of his commanders was sent with a large army and with Antigonus toward Jerusalem.

The commander was instructed to use gentleness and fair words until he had gained entrance into the city.

Afterward he was to seize Hyrcanus, Herod, and Phasael and hand them over to Antigonus.

### The Persian Army Enters Jerusalem

The commander came with Antigonus to Jerusalem.

He declared that he desired nothing against the city and had come only in friendship.

He said that when his business was finished he would return to his own land.

The inhabitants therefore allowed the army to enter after receiving promises and oaths of peace.

But when the soldiers were inside the city, they broke their agreement.

They began killing and plundering.

Herod hurried to the palace of Hyrcanus and fortified it.

Phasael went to the stronghold and took possession of it.

Those with them resisted the soldiers who were already inside the city and prevented the rest from freely joining them.

Many of the invading soldiers were killed, and others fled outside Jerusalem.

### The Commander Uses Deceit

When the Persian commander saw that his first plan had failed, he changed his manner.

He sent messengers to Hyrcanus, Herod, and Phasael.

He apologized for the violence that had taken place and claimed that it had happened without his knowledge or command.

He praised their courage and the strength they had shown.

He said that he desired friendship with them.

He even advised them to join him and his king in opposition to Antony.

He swore oaths and gave assurances that no harm would come to them.

Herod did not believe the commander's words.

He neither trusted the promises nor accepted the invitation.

Hyrcanus and Phasael, however, were persuaded by the commander's kindness and oaths.

They went out to meet him.

The commander received them with honor and continued to speak peacefully to them.

He then withdrew from Jerusalem and took Hyrcanus and Phasael with him.

### Hyrcanus and Phasael Are Captured

When they reached the land of Syria, the commander seized them.

Phasael, the brother of Herod, killed himself rather than remain in captivity.

Hyrcanus was bound.

Antigonus ordered that the ear of his uncle Hyrcanus be cut so that he would no longer be fit to hold the high priesthood.

The king of Persia afterward returned to his own land and took Hyrcanus with him.

When they arrived there, Hyrcanus was released from his chains and was treated with kindness.

He remained among the Jews of that country for a long time.

The account says that in later years Herod would summon him back to Jerusalem.

### Antigonus Is Established at Jerusalem

The king of Persia sent his commander again with Antigonus and ordered that Antigonus be established over the Jews in Jerusalem.

When Herod learned what had happened to Hyrcanus and learned that Phasael his brother was dead, he feared that he also would be captured if he remained in Jerusalem.

He therefore gathered his mother, his wife, and the rest of his household.

He sent them toward the mountains of Seir, where his brother Joseph son of Antipater was staying.

Herod placed his family in the fortress there.

He left the greater part of his men with Joseph and ordered him to protect the household and provide everything they required.

### Herod Goes to Egypt and Rome

Herod then traveled toward Egypt and from there sought a way to Rome.

Cleopatra, queen of Egypt, received him with honor.

She gave him money and provided ships and men to assist him on his journey.

Herod crossed the sea to Rome.

There he came to Antony, his friend.

Antony received him warmly and listened to the account of everything Antigonus and the king of Persia had done.

Antony brought Herod before Augustus Caesar.

Herod told Augustus the same things.

When Augustus had heard the matter, he consulted with Antony and with the elders of Rome.

They agreed together that Herod should be made ruler over the Jews.

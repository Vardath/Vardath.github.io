### The Beginning of Herod son of Antipater

When Antipater saw the weakness of Hyrcanus in judgment, the softness of his resolve, and his reluctance to act, he and his sons gained increasing control over the affairs of the state.

Antipater appointed his son Phasael over Jerusalem.

He appointed another son, Herod, over Galilee.

Herod was still young at that time; the numeral giving his age is damaged in the surviving working source.

The greater part of practical authority passed into the hands of Antipater and his sons, while Hyrcanus increasingly retained the name of rule without the same power in its daily exercise.

### Herod and Hezekiah

There was at that time a man named Hezekiah.

He was bold, powerful, and formidable, and a company of men of the same kind gathered around him.

They repeatedly crossed into the lands of Syria.

They plundered property, killed inhabitants, and caused great destruction.

Their attacks became a severe burden upon the people of those districts.

Caesar had appointed a governor over the Syrian lands.

When the governor saw what Hezekiah and his companions were doing, he wrote to Herod.

He reminded Herod of Caesar's favor toward Antipater, of Antipater's loyalty to Caesar, and of the help Antipater had given in Caesar's wars.

He urged Herod to follow the path of his father.

He told him that the injuries committed by Hezekiah and his men had become intolerable and asked Herod to put an end to them.

When the message reached Herod, he left Galilee with his companions.

He found Hezekiah returning from the Syrian lands and fell upon him unexpectedly.

Herod killed Hezekiah and those who were with him.

He then sent word to the Roman governor reporting what he had done.

The governor rewarded Herod generously.

Other leading Romans also sent him gifts and money and praised his action.

Herod's authority therefore grew stronger.

His name became widely known, and the number of men attached to him increased.

### The Jews Accuse Herod before Hyrcanus

When news of the killing of Hezekiah reached the Jews, many of them were deeply troubled.

They gathered before Hyrcanus and said:

“How long will you endure what Antipater and his sons are doing?

“You have allowed the kingdom to pass into their hands.

“They act in it as they choose, without your command and without seeking your judgment.

“Antipater draws near to the rulers by gifts and wealth.

“One of his sons exercises authority here in your city.

“His other son Herod has taken control of Galilee.

“And he was not satisfied with this, but killed Hezekiah, a mighty man whom the nations feared.

“If Hezekiah had committed an offense worthy of death, he should have been brought before the judges and condemned according to judgment.

“Herod instead killed him by his own authority.

“He has done this in order to draw near to foreign rulers and to receive wealth, gifts, and favor from them.

“It is therefore right, O king, that you summon him before the judges so that they may give judgment concerning what he has done.”

They continued to repeat these words to Hyrcanus.

The sons and wives of those who had died with Hezekiah also came repeatedly to the king's gate.

They cried aloud, wept, complained against Herod, and asked Hyrcanus to avenge the blood of those who had been killed without judgment.

At last Hyrcanus wrote to Herod and ordered him to come quickly to Jerusalem.

### Herod before the Council of Seventy

Herod came to Jerusalem with a company of his companions.

He appeared before the council in the presence of the seventy elders.

But he did not come dressed as a humbled defendant.

He wore fine clothing.

His hair and appearance had been carefully arranged.

He carried weapons, and his companions stood around him well dressed and armed.

When Herod entered in this manner, fear fell upon many of those present.

Those who had spoken strongly against him while he was absent became silent when they saw him before them.

The men who had accused him and complained about his actions no longer spoke with the boldness they had shown before.

The elders and Hyrcanus continued discussing the matter.

As their words passed back and forth, it appeared that judgment might finally be given against Herod.

Hyrcanus then said to the elders:

“It is better that we stop this matter for today.

“Tomorrow we will return and examine it again.”

Hyrcanus said this because he desired to protect Herod and prevent a sentence of death from being carried out against him.

### Shammai Rebukes the Council

Shammai, a disciple of Hillel, was present when these things happened.

He rebuked the elders for their fear and said:

“We have always seen that a man who is accused of a serious offense, when he is brought before the elders for judgment, comes dressed in dark clothing.

“His hair is disordered.

“He stands humbled and submissive because he knows that judgment is before him.

“But look at this young man Herod.

“He has appeared before you in exactly the opposite manner.

“He has done nothing that an accused man ordinarily does to show humility.

“He has brought his companions with him dressed finely and armed for battle.

“He has come in this manner because he holds this council and its judges in contempt.

“He has resolved that if judgment is given against him, he will defend himself by force.

“You have seen this with your own eyes.

“Yet you have abandoned the accusations that were brought against him.

“You have forgotten the words that were spoken concerning what he did.

“You have become silent because of fear.

“I tell you that a time will come when this Herod will rule over you.

“He will do among you whatever he chooses.

“He will shed your blood and lay your houses open to plunder.

“And he will not spare even Hyrcanus, who has favored him and protected him from judgment.

“He will kill Hyrcanus also and take the kingdom from him.”

The words of Shammai ended the gathering in fear and confusion.

Hyrcanus returned to his residence, and the elders and the others who had assembled departed.

### Herod Flees to Syria

During the third part of the night, Herod secretly left Jerusalem.

He went into the land of Syria and came to Caesar's governor there.

The governor received Herod, protected him, and raised him above the other Syrian commanders.

Thus Herod escaped the judgment that had been prepared against him and increased his standing under Roman authority.

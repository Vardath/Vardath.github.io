## 3 Meqabyan 3

**1.** “And as for you—” *The surviving digital Geʽez source preserves only this fragment for verse 1.*

**2.** “And to Adam were given—” *The surviving digital Geʽez source preserves only this fragment for verse 2.*

**3.** “Again, within Adam were mingled thoughts like the waves of the sea, like a storm wind that lifts dust from the earth, like a wave of the sea beaten by the wind, and like drops of rain that cannot be counted. So also are Adam’s thoughts: the thoughts within his heart are many beyond counting.

**4.** “But in you there is one thought and no other, because you are not of flesh.

**5.** “And you corrupted that—” *The surviving digital Geʽez source breaks off here before the sentence is completed.*

**6.** “And she came and led astray the first servant of the LORD through eating from the tree, and she brought death upon herself and upon her children because she transgressed the commandment of her Creator.

**7.** “By the righteous judgment of the LORD they went out from Paradise. Yet he did not cast them far away from Paradise in hostility; rather, in the land to which he sent them he comforted them through their children, through the fruit of their wombs, and through the fruit of their land.

**8.** “When you drove them from Paradise through jealousy, the LORD gave them living shoots from the trees of Paradise so that they might plant them and be comforted by fruit from Paradise and by the fruit of the land that the LORD gave them.

**9.** “Thus they might be comforted and their hearts greatly renewed by the sight of their children, by the sight of the garden, and by the fruit of the land that the earth brought forth from the dust. When they ate from it, they were greatly comforted from the sorrow of being driven from Paradise.

**10.** “They were comforted in their hearts through their children and through the fruit of the land, for the LORD knows how to comfort his servants.

**11.** “He renews their hearts with grain and with water, because they became exiles in this land of thorns and thistles.”

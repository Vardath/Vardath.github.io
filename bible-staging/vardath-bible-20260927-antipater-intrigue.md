### Antipater, Father of Herod

There was at that time a man named Antipater.

One account in the tradition says that he was descended from Jews who had come from Babylon with Ezra the priest.

He was a man of intelligence, judgment, courage, and great ability.

But the account also describes him as exceedingly crafty, subtle in deceit, and skilled in schemes.

He possessed great wealth in money, estates, and livestock.

Some said that King Alexander son of Hyrcanus had appointed Antipater over Edom and the mountains of al-Sharah and that he remained there for many years.

Antipater married a woman from Edom.

She bore him four sons: Phasael, Herod, Pheroras, and Joseph.

They also had a daughter named Salome.

Another report preserved in the tradition says that Antipater was not originally of the children of Israel, but came from the servants attached to the Maccabean priests of the house of the Hasmoneans and from a people formerly devoted to idols, with a connection to Ashkelon.

Thus differing accounts were told concerning his origin.

When Alexander son of Hyrcanus died and Alexandra took the kingdom, she removed Antipater from his former authority in the mountains of al-Sharah.

Antipater then remained in Jerusalem.

A strong friendship existed between him and Hyrcanus son of Alexander.

He was frequently with Hyrcanus and rarely separated from him.

Aristobulus, however, disliked Antipater.

When Antipater perceived the hostility of Aristobulus, he feared for himself and began to devise a way to remove Aristobulus from power.

### Antipater Turns the Leaders toward Hyrcanus

Antipater secretly approached the leading men of the state.

He spoke against Aristobulus and belittled his claim to the kingdom.

He told them that Aristobulus had seized the kingship while his elder brother Hyrcanus was still living and that Hyrcanus therefore possessed the stronger claim.

Antipater argued that it was unjust to leave the elder brother deprived of what belonged to him and that those who feared God should restore the right to its proper owner.

He went privately from one influential man to another until he had persuaded many of them to favor Hyrcanus and to oppose Aristobulus.

He praised Hyrcanus before them and condemned Aristobulus.

He used flattery, gifts, persuasion, and every device he possessed to bring them to his purpose.

Hyrcanus himself knew nothing of what Antipater was arranging.

### Antipater Frightens Hyrcanus

When Antipater had prepared the leading men, he went privately to Hyrcanus and said:

“I have learned with certainty that your brother Aristobulus intends to kill you.

“He does not believe his kingdom can be secure while you remain alive.

“He knows that you are the elder brother and that many people believe you have the better claim to rule.

“For this reason he waits only for the opportunity to destroy you.

“You must therefore guard your life and be cautious of him.”

At first Hyrcanus did not believe Antipater.

His heart was gentle and unsuspecting, and he still trusted the affection of his brother.

Antipater therefore repeated the warning again and again.

He also gave money to some of the friends and trusted companions of Hyrcanus and asked them to speak the same words to him.

They continually warned Hyrcanus that Aristobulus intended to kill him.

At last the repeated accusations entered his heart.

Hyrcanus became afraid of his brother and began to distrust him.

### Hyrcanus Flees to Aretas

When Antipater saw that his words had taken effect, he advised Hyrcanus to leave Jerusalem and go to Aretas, king of the Arabs.

He told him that distance from Aristobulus would preserve his life.

Before giving this advice, Antipater had already gone secretly to Aretas.

He told the Arab king that Hyrcanus desired his friendship and no longer wished to remain near Aristobulus.

Aretas was pleased and agreed to receive Hyrcanus.

Antipater and Aretas pledged that they would not abandon Hyrcanus and that they would protect and support him.

Antipater then returned to Jerusalem and reported the agreement to Hyrcanus.

He urged him to depart without delay.

Hyrcanus left Jerusalem by night, and Antipater went with him.

They came to Aretas, king of the Arabs.

Aretas received them with honor, and they remained with him for a time.

### The Rise of Roman Power

In those days the kingdom of Rome became stronger than the kingdom of the Greeks.

The Romans increased in power among the nations, and their name became feared throughout many lands.

The author compares their kingdom with the fourth beast seen by Daniel: a kingdom that devoured, crushed, and trampled the kingdoms around it.

The Romans fought Antiochus king of the Greeks, who came against them with elephants, cavalry, infantry, and a great army.

They defeated him and compelled him to submit to tribute.

### Hannibal and the Romans

There also arose against Rome Hannibal, king of the Africans, whose royal city was Carthage.

Hannibal gathered a very great army from Africa and from the peoples allied with him.

He crossed from Africa into the lands beyond the narrow sea and advanced through the western countries until he came into Italy.

The Romans went out against him.

For many years there was fierce war between Hannibal and Rome.

Again and again Roman armies were defeated, and many of their warriors fell.

Among the Roman commanders were Æmilius and Varro.

In one great battle Æmilius was killed, while Varro escaped and withdrew to a fortified city.

Hannibal pursued the Romans and brought the war close to Rome itself.

The counsellors of Rome began to fear that the city would fall.

Some proposed opening the gates and making peace with Hannibal so that the people might live.

Then a young Roman commander named Scipio stood before the three hundred and twenty counsellors.

He said:

“Do not surrender yourselves to Hannibal.

“Give me soldiers, and I will go into Africa and strike his own country.

“When Hannibal hears that his land and his city are in danger, he will be compelled to leave Rome and return to defend them.”

The counsellors accepted his plan.

Scipio sailed to Africa and attacked the country of Hannibal.

In the fighting Hasdrubal, the brother of Hannibal, was killed.

Scipio caused word of this to be brought to Hannibal and continued laying waste the lands around Carthage.

The people of Carthage sent to Hannibal, saying:

“Why do you remain in a foreign land while your own country is being destroyed?

“If you do not return quickly, we will be forced to surrender the city and your palace to Scipio.”

When Hannibal received their message, he grieved and lifted his siege.

He returned with his army toward Africa.

Scipio met him there in battle.

Hannibal was defeated.

After further defeats he fled from place to place and at last went into Egypt.

Scipio pursued him.

The ruler of Egypt delivered Hannibal into the power of the Romans.

Hannibal then drank poison and died.

Scipio brought the land of Africa under Roman authority.

From that time the power of Rome was exalted still further among the nations.

### The Romans Write to Judah

The rulers of Rome heard of Judah son of Mattathias, of his battles, and of the agreements that Antiochus Eupator and Lysias had made with the Jews.

They sent a letter to Judah and to the elders of Judah.

The letter said:

“Qinṣius Minius, Scipio, and Menelaus, rulers of Rome, to Judah, the anointed one of battle, and to the elders of Judah: greeting.

“We have heard of your strength and of your wars, and we have heard what Antiochus and Lysias granted to you and what they wrote concerning the Jews.

“We therefore ask whether you will become our allies and friends rather than the friends of the Greeks who afflicted you.

“Tell us who are your enemies and who are your friends.”

Judah and the elders received the Roman message.

### The Covenant between Rome and Judah

The Romans and the Jews established a covenant of mutual aid.

Its terms were these:

If war came first against Rome, whether by sea or by land, the Jews were to assist the Romans with all the strength available to them.

They were not to provide Rome’s enemies with weapons, grain, food, or other aid.

Likewise, if war came first against the nation of the Jews, the Romans were to assist the Jews with all the strength available to them.

They were not to provide the enemies of the Jews with weapons, grain, food, or other aid.

The covenant was established by the ruling authority of Rome and its three hundred and twenty counsellors.

Neither side was to alter the agreement deceitfully.

If afterward both sides agreed together to add something to the covenant or remove something from it, the change would be valid only by their common agreement.

Thus friendship and alliance were established between Rome and Judah.

After this the land had rest for a time, and Judah turned his attention to judging the people and removing wickedness from among them.

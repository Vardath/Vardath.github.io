### Judah’s First Victories

When Apollonius, commander of the Macedonian forces, heard that Judah had risen in rebellion, he gathered an army and marched against Israel.

Judah went out to meet him.

A fierce battle followed.

Judah saw Apollonius standing among the Greek soldiers and forced his way toward him through the fighting.

He struck Apollonius down.

When their commander fell, the soldiers with him broke and fled.

Judah and his men pursued them and took their spoil.

Judah also took the sword of Apollonius and afterward carried it in his battles.

### Seron at Beth-horon

When Seron, commander of the Syrian forces, heard what Judah had done, he determined to attack him and gain honor by putting down the revolt.

He gathered an army and came toward Beth-horon.

Judah’s men saw that the enemy was much more numerous than they were and became afraid.

They said, “How can so few of us stand against such a multitude?”

Judah answered:

“Cry to heaven and do not measure the outcome by the number of soldiers.

“Victory does not depend upon whether those who fight are many or few. God is able to save by a great company or by a small one.”

Judah moved against Seron’s army and attacked suddenly.

Confusion fell upon the enemy.

Seron was struck down, and his remaining soldiers fled toward the land of the Philistines.

After this the fear of Judah spread among the surrounding peoples.

### Antiochus Sends Lysias

When Antiochus heard that Judah had defeated his commanders and broken the forces sent against him, he became exceedingly angry.

At the same time rebellion had also arisen against his authority in the east.

Antiochus divided his strength.

He himself marched toward Persia to deal with the revolt there.

He entrusted the war in Judah to Lysias, a man of his own royal house, and placed his son under Lysias’s care.

Lysias selected commanders for the campaign, including Ptolemy, Nicanor, and Gorgias, and sent a great army against Judah.

Merchants also came with the army, expecting that many captives from Judah would be taken and sold.

When Judah and the elders of Israel learned that this force was approaching, they proclaimed a fast.

The people clothed themselves in mourning, placed dust upon their heads, and cried to God for help.

Judah then arranged his men for battle.

He appointed leaders over the companies and allowed those who had newly built a house, planted a vineyard, become betrothed, or were overcome by fear to return home.

Those who remained were men prepared to fight.

### The Defeat of Nicanor’s Army

Nicanor advanced with confidence, believing that Judah would be easily destroyed and that captives from Israel would be sold to the merchants who had followed his army.

Judah gathered the faithful who remained with him and prayed:

“O God, ruler from the beginning, power and might are in your hand.

“Bring down those who come against your people, and deliver the proud into the hands of the lowly.”

The priests sounded the trumpets, and the people raised a shout.

Judah rushed into battle.

The army of Nicanor was broken and fled before him.

Judah’s men pursued them and returned with the spoil of the camp.

They found the money the merchants had brought to purchase Jewish captives.

The spoil was distributed among the people and the poor.

Thus those who had come expecting to buy the sons and daughters of Judah as slaves returned empty-handed, while Judah and his men were strengthened.

### Caesar Takes Rule in Rome

The opening lines of this section are damaged in the surviving working source.

Caesar did not turn aside from the purpose he had formed.

He armed himself for war against the Roman rulers who opposed him.

He drove his adversaries from the city, overcame the governing men who stood against him, and took possession of the state.

He became ruler and was called Caesar.

Pompey was at that time still powerful among the Romans.

When he learned what Caesar had done, he gathered his army and marched against him.

Caesar met him in battle.

Pompey was defeated, and the account says that Caesar prevailed over him and put him to death.

Caesar then took control of the Roman dominion.

### Caesar, Mithridates, and Egypt

Caesar desired to go into Egypt because commanders and supporters of Pompey had gathered there and remained strong.

He decided first to go through Syria.

When Mithridates, ruler of the Syrians, learned that Caesar had come, he received him and declared his obedience.

Caesar ordered Mithridates to march into Egypt against the commanders who still supported Pompey.

Mithridates obeyed.

He came with his forces as far as Ashkelon.

### Antipater Joins Caesar's Cause

When Hyrcanus heard of Caesar's victory and his coming into Syria, he became afraid.

Hyrcanus had been known as a supporter and ally of Pompey.

He therefore judged that he should do something that would bring him into Caesar's favor.

He sent Antipater, his companion, with a large army to join Mithridates at Ashkelon.

Mithridates welcomed Antipater and honored him.

Together they marched into Egypt.

The Egyptian army met them in great strength.

At first the Egyptians gained the advantage.

They defeated Mithridates and surrounded him so that he was in danger of being killed.

Antipater came to his aid with his men.

They fought fiercely against the Egyptians, broke their encirclement, and defeated them.

Mithridates then entered the country and gained control there.

Antipater received many wounds in the fighting.

His endurance and courage became known because of what he had done.

Mithridates wrote to Caesar concerning him and reported that Antipater had been the chief cause of turning the war in Egypt.

### Antipater before Caesar

When Caesar read the message of Mithridates, he thanked Antipater for his service and sent for him.

Antipater came to Caesar together with Mithridates.

Caesar received him warmly, honored him, advanced him, and promised him favor.

At that time Antigonus son of Aristobulus came before Caesar with accusations against his uncle Hyrcanus and against Antipater.

He reminded Caesar that his father Aristobulus had been released from Rome to make war against Pompey.

He accused Hyrcanus and Antipater of conspiring against Aristobulus and causing his death when Aristobulus had intended to assist Caesar.

Caesar therefore summoned Antipater and asked him concerning these accusations.

Antipater answered:

“I formerly honored Pompey because he was commander of the Roman army and held authority over us.

“But I did not fight the Egyptians in these present days, nor endure these great hardships and wounds, in payment of loyalty to Pompey after his death.

“What I have done I have done in obedience to Caesar and in his service.”

Then Antipater uncovered his head and body and showed Caesar the wounds and visible scars he had received in battle.

He said:

“These bear witness to my devotion to Caesar.

“My obedience to him has exceeded the obedience I once showed to Pompey, for every man obeyed Pompey when he held authority over us.”

### Caesar Honors Antipater

When Caesar heard Antipater's words and saw his wounds, he was pleased.

He said to him with good will:

“May you remain safe, brave man.

“Your obedience has been made plain, your courage has been confirmed, and these marks upon your body testify to your sincere friendship, loyalty, and diligence in our service.

“I have advanced you among my companions and entrusted you with authority among my forces.

“I desire that you travel with me into the eastern lands and remain with me until I return.

“Afterward you shall return to your own country in a better condition than when you left it.”

Caesar then marched to war in the eastern lands, and Antipater went with him.

The account says that Caesar fought against the Persians.

Antipater again displayed courage, strength, and endurance.

His standing with Caesar increased, and Caesar honored him still more.

When Caesar returned from those lands, he sent Antipater back to his country with great honor and favor.

Caesar himself returned to Rome.

### Hyrcanus after Caesar's Victory

The affairs of Hyrcanus in Jerusalem then became settled.

His standing increased.

He conducted himself well among the people and was remembered as a good and upright man.

Yet the account says that he was not strong in the conduct of war or in the management of armies.

He preferred ease, peace, and quiet, and this disposition became well known among those around him.

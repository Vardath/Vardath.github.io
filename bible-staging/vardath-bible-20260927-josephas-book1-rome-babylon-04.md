### The Government of Rome

After the kings were abolished, the Romans chose seventy counsellors from among themselves and appointed them to govern the kingdom. They set one man at their head and called him the Elder, and placed trusted counsellors beside him to direct the affairs of Rome.

As Rome grew stronger, the governing body was enlarged until the Elder stood with three hundred and twenty administrators over the Roman state. Their power increased, and they subdued the peoples who bordered them in the west.

The government remained in this form until the first Caesar prevailed over them and made himself ruler. From his time onward those who held the supreme rule were again reckoned as kings, as will be related in its place.

### Rome and Babylon

After two hundred and five years, war arose by sea and by land between the Romans and the Chaldeans of Babylon. The cause was a war between the Greeks and the Chaldeans. The Romans gave aid to the Greeks, and because of this the Chaldeans became angry and made war upon Rome.

The Romans feared the power of the Chaldeans. They altered the course of the river Tiber where it passed through their city and strengthened the riverbed from one gate of Rome to the other. The defended stretch extended eighteen miles. They laid metal upon the bed and banks of the river so that hostile ships could not pass freely into the city.

The Tiber ran through the midst of Rome. Three quarters of the inhabitants lived on one side of the river and one quarter on the other. The Romans fortified and paved the channel between them, and the ships and boats of the king of Babylon could no longer enter as they had intended.

When the Romans heard that the king of Babylon had taken Jerusalem, their fear increased. They sent messengers with gifts to Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon, asking for peace and declaring their submission to the treaty they sought from him.

Nebuchadnezzar accepted their embassy and made peace with them. Thus the war between Rome and Babylon ceased and did not rise again during that period, until the dominion of the Medes and Persians.

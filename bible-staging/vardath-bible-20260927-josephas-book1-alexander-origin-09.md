### Alexander son of Philip

An enemy rose against Philip and entered his territory. Philip sent one of his commanders with a large army against the invader, while he sent his son Alexander with another part of the forces toward a different region.

When Fāris, one of the commanders, saw that Philip’s army had been divided and that many soldiers were no longer with him, he became bold enough to attack the king. He gathered a company of men, fell upon Philip, and struck him many times until Philip was cast to the ground.

Confusion spread among the people and the city was thrown into disorder.

Alexander was away when these things happened. As he approached the city, he saw the disturbance among the people and asked what had occurred. When he was told what had happened to his father, he hurried in.

He found Philip lying wounded and in the power of Fāris the commander. Alexander killed Fāris, but Philip died from his wounds.

Alexander became king after his father when he was sixteen years old.

He had been trained in wisdom and good conduct, and his teacher was Aristotle the philosopher.

In appearance Alexander resembled both his father and his mother. It was said that his face had something of the appearance of a lion. His right eye was dark and inclined downward, while his left eye was of another, lion-like color and inclined upward. His teeth were small and sharp like the teeth of a dog.

From his youth he was courageous, daring, and greatly inclined toward warfare.

### The Death of Cyrus

After these things, Cyrus learned that the king of the Shittim had rebelled against him. Cyrus marched into their land, killed their king, and struck down many of his warriors.

Those who escaped fled with Tamirah, their queen, and with her son into strong fortresses. They shut themselves within them, and Cyrus found that he could not easily draw them out.

Cyrus therefore used cunning. He withdrew with his army as though he were abandoning the country. When the Shittim saw him depart, they came out from their fortresses with the son of Tamirah and pursued him.

When they had come out into the open country, Cyrus suddenly turned back against them. He struck them with a great slaughter. The son of Tamirah was killed among them, many of the Shittim were taken captive, and those who escaped fled into the mountains.

When Tamirah learned that her son had been killed, she was filled with grief and wrath. She gathered the men who remained to her and moved through the mountains and valleys, watching for Cyrus and waiting for a place to strike him.

Cyrus departed from the land of the Shittim confident in his victory. The greater part of his army went ahead of him on the road, while Cyrus remained behind with a smaller company.

Tamirah came upon them by night and attacked them suddenly. Cyrus was killed together with a company of his companions.

Tamirah found the body of Cyrus, cut off his head, and placed it in a vessel filled with the blood of the slain. She said, “Drink now, Cyrus, and fill yourself with the blood which you desired to shed without pity or mercy.”

Thus ended the life and warfare of Cyrus.

### Cambyses

After the death of Cyrus, Cambyses his son reigned in his place.

When his kingdom had been established, Cambyses marched into the land of the Shittim against Tamirah, who had killed his father. He destroyed those who remained of her people, broke their power, and avenged the death of Cyrus.

Cambyses then went against the other nations that had rebelled after his father’s death. He subdued them and brought them again under his authority, and his kingdom became strong.

Throughout this period the Jews remained obedient to the kings of Persia. The Persian kings treated them with favor and continued to provide the gifts and provisions which Cyrus had granted for sacrifices and for the service of the house of God.

They honored the house of God and regarded it with reverence, and they asked the Jews to pray there on their behalf.

This state of affairs continued until the days of Ahasuerus.

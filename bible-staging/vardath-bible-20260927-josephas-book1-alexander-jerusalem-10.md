After Alexander became king over the Greeks, he sent to Darius seeking peace and proposed to send him valuable gifts.

Some among the peoples under Alexander’s authority resisted him, but he moved against those who rebelled until they returned to obedience and his rule was established.

Alexander then marched into the lands of the west. Their inhabitants submitted to him, and he took possession of their territories by agreement and without destroying them.

### The Apparition on the Road

While Alexander was traveling with his army, he saw a man standing before him with a sword in his hand. The blade shone like lightning.

The man raised the sword over Alexander, and the king was seized with fear.

Alexander said, “My lord, forgive your servant, for I have done wrong. If you do not wish me to continue along this road, I will turn back to my own country.”

The heavenly messenger told him not to fear. He had been sent by God and had gone before Alexander in his victories over kings and nations.

But the messenger warned him concerning Jerusalem and the people and priests of God. He told Alexander to continue toward the city, where he would see a man whose appearance resembled the messenger himself.

“When you see him,” the messenger said, “fall upon your face before him and listen to what he tells you. Do not oppose his words, for if you reject them you will perish.”

Alexander departed from that place and continued toward Jerusalem.

### Alexander Comes to Jerusalem

When the Jews heard that Alexander was approaching, they were afraid.

The high priest commanded the people to fast, pray, give alms, and turn to God, asking him to protect them.

When Alexander drew near the city, the high priest and a great company of the Jews went out to meet him.

As Alexander approached, he looked upon the high priest and recognized in him the likeness of the messenger who had appeared to him on the road.

Alexander immediately came down from his horse, fell upon his face before the high priest, greeted him, and treated him with great honor.

The kings, commanders, and nobles traveling with Alexander were astonished. They said, “You are the king before whom rulers bow. Why have you descended and bowed before this man? He has no authority over you and is counted among your subjects.”

Alexander told them about the messenger who had appeared to him on the road, the warning he had received, and the command that had been given concerning the man he would meet in Jerusalem.

The high priest received Alexander with honor and led him into Jerusalem.

When Alexander entered and saw the holy house, he marveled at it and gave thanks to God.

Alexander said to the high priest, “I desire to leave something in this house by which I may be remembered. I will provide gold so that an image may be made for me here.”

The high priest answered, “It is not lawful for us to place an idol or an image in the holy house of God. Instead, let the money you have offered be used for the needs of the priests, the poor, the orphans, and the widows who are cared for here.

“And I will establish another remembrance for you. The male children born among us during this year shall be called Alexander, so that your name will be remembered among the people and before God.”

Alexander was pleased with this answer.

He gave a large sum of money to the house of God and presented rich gifts to the high priest.

Then Alexander asked the high priest to inquire of God concerning his war with Darius. He wanted to know whether God would grant him victory or whether he should return to his own country.

The high priest said to him, “Go on your way, O king. God is with you. He will give Darius into your hand and will grant you victory over him.”

The high priest then brought out the book of Daniel the prophet and showed Alexander the vision in which a goat came against a ram, broke its power, cast it down, and trampled it so that the ram could not escape.

He said to Alexander, “You are represented by the goat, and Darius by the ram. You will overcome him.”

Alexander rejoiced greatly at these words.

He commanded that the account of what had happened to him on the road, what he had seen in Jerusalem, and what the high priest had shown him from Daniel should be written down and sent to Rome and Macedonia.

Then Alexander departed from Jerusalem and continued on his way toward Darius.

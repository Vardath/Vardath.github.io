### The Religious Conflict under Alexander

After these things Alexander ordered a wall to be built separating the altar from the place where the people gathered for prayer.

No one was to approach the altar except the priests and those of the king's own household who were permitted to enter.

The common people were to remain on the other side of the wall.

Because of this, the hostility between the Pharisees and the Sadducees became still more bitter.

Alexander strengthened the Sadducees against the Pharisees, and war continued between the parties for six years.

During those years fifty thousand men of the Pharisaic party were killed.

Afterward Alexander tried to reach an agreement with them and to restore peace, but he was unable to do so.

A group of the Pharisees therefore went to Demetrius son of Seleucus.

They asked him to help them against Alexander and brought many accusations against the king.

Demetrius came with an army, and a number of Jews joined him.

Alexander went out to fight them.

Demetrius defeated Alexander and killed many of his men.

Alexander fled into the mountains and remained there with those companions who had escaped with him.

Then many of the Jews who had joined Demetrius changed sides and came to Alexander.

His army grew strong again.

When Alexander advanced once more against Demetrius, Demetrius withdrew and returned to his own country.

### Alexander's Vengeance upon His Opponents

After Demetrius had departed, the war between Alexander and the Pharisees continued.

Alexander defeated them in battle and killed many.

The survivors fled, and Alexander pursued them.

He captured eight hundred of their leading men.

They were put to death and crucified before him.

After this Alexander gained the upper hand over those who had opposed him, and fear of him spread among his enemies.

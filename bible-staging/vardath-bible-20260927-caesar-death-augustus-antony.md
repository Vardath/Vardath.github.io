### Caesar Is Killed

There were among those around Caesar two men who had formerly belonged to the party of Pompey.

Their names were Cassius and Brutus.

They formed a plan together to kill Caesar.

They concealed themselves in a place at Rome where Caesar was accustomed to enter without fear.

When Caesar came there without expecting danger, they fell upon him and killed him.

### Cassius and the Tribute from Judah

After Caesar had been killed, Cassius gathered a great army.

He crossed the sea because he feared those in Rome who remained loyal to Caesar.

He entered the lands of Asia and laid waste to them.

From there he came into the land of Judah.

Antipater at first desired to resist him.

But when he saw that his strength was not sufficient, he made peace with Cassius.

Cassius imposed upon the land of Judah a tribute of seven hundred talents.

Antipater made himself responsible for the payment.

He charged his son Herod to gather the money from the land and deliver it to Cassius.

Herod carried out the command.

Cassius received the tribute and afterward marched toward Macedonia, where he remained because of his fear of the Roman forces that were coming against him.

### The Death of Antipater

At that time some of the leading men of Judah conspired against Antipater.

They secretly chose a man named Malichus to accomplish his death.

The attempt did not immediately succeed.

When Antipater learned that Malichus was plotting against him, he sought to seize him.

Malichus denied the accusation.

He swore that the report was false and persuaded Antipater to put away his suspicion.

But Malichus did not abandon the plan.

He gave a large sum of money to the cupbearer of Hyrcanus.

He arranged with him to put poison into the drink of Antipater while Antipater was reclining at a feast in the presence of Hyrcanus.

The cupbearer did as he had been instructed.

Antipater drank the poison and died that same day.

The account says that Hyrcanus had neither planned nor known of the deed.

After the death of Antipater, Hyrcanus raised Malichus to the place of authority that Antipater had held.

### Herod Takes Vengeance on Malichus

When Herod learned that Malichus had caused the death of his father, he desired to attack him openly.

His brother restrained him and advised him to use a more careful plan.

Herod therefore went to Cassius and told him what Malichus had done.

Cassius answered that when he came to Tyre, and Hyrcanus and Malichus were present with him, Herod should take vengeance upon Malichus.

Cassius later went to Tyre.

Hyrcanus came to him, and Malichus came with Hyrcanus.

Cassius invited them and his other companions to a feast.

He privately ordered his servants to do whatever Herod commanded them.

Herod and his brother also stood among the companions of Hyrcanus.

Herod arranged with the servants that a signal from his eye would tell them when to strike Malichus.

After they had eaten and drunk, the company rested during the afternoon.

When Hyrcanus awoke, he ordered a couch to be prepared outside before the place where they had eaten.

Hyrcanus sat there and told Malichus to sit beside him.

Herod and his brother were also seated nearby.

The servants of Cassius stood around them.

Herod gave the agreed signal.

The servants immediately fell upon Malichus and killed him.

Hyrcanus was terrified by what had happened and fainted.

After Malichus had been carried away and Hyrcanus recovered, he asked Herod why the man had been killed.

Herod answered:

“I know nothing of it, nor do I know why it happened.”

Hyrcanus said no more about the matter.

### Octavian and Antony March against Cassius

News then reached Cassius that Octavian, the kinsman and successor of Caesar, and Antony, commander of his army, had left Rome with a force too great to number.

Cassius therefore marched toward Macedonia to meet them.

Octavian and Antony came against him there.

Cassius was defeated.

The account says that they pursued him, destroyed his power, and put him to death.

Octavian then took the Roman rule in the place of Caesar.

He was called Augustus because his authority and dominion had increased.

He also bore the name Caesar after his predecessor.

### Hyrcanus Sends to Augustus

When Hyrcanus heard that Cassius was dead and that Augustus had taken the Roman rule, he sent messengers to Augustus and Antony.

He sent valuable gifts, money, and precious stones with them.

Hyrcanus asked that the covenant which Caesar had made with the Jews be renewed.

He also asked that every Jew who had been taken captive during the days of Cassius be released.

He asked that the Jews who were living in the lands of the Greeks and throughout Asia be permitted to return to their own country without anyone preventing them or demanding payment from them.

When the messengers came to Augustus, he received them with honor.

He accepted the gifts and granted the requests of Hyrcanus.

He then wrote to Hyrcanus.

### Augustus Renews the Covenant

Augustus wrote:

“From Augustus Caesar, king of kings, and Antony, commander of his army, to Hyrcanus, ruler of the Jews: peace.

“Your letter has reached us, and we rejoiced when we received it.

“We have granted what you asked concerning the renewal of the covenant and concerning the Jews in all the lands under our authority.

“We were prevented from writing sooner because we were occupied in war against Cassius, who rose against Caesar and killed him.

“We were compelled to pursue him and to seek justice for the blood of Caesar.

“God gave us victory over him.

“We defeated him, destroyed his power, and delivered the lands of Asia from the oppression and destruction that had come upon them through him.

“Therefore rejoice, Hyrcanus, and let the priests in the house of God and all the Jews rejoice with you.

“Receive the gift that we have sent for the great house of God, and pray for the life and safety of Augustus.

“We have written throughout all our provinces that no Jewish man or woman is to remain held as a slave because of the wars of Cassius.

“Every such captive is to be released without price and without ransom.

“No one is to hinder them from returning to the land of Judah and to their own homes.

“This is the command of Augustus Caesar and of Antony, commander of his army.”

Augustus also wrote to the authorities at Tyre and Sidon and in the surrounding places.

He commanded them to restore whatever had been taken from the land of Judah during the days of Cassius.

He ordered them to live peaceably with the Jews, to oppose them in nothing that had been lawfully granted, and to continue everything Caesar had established in his covenant with them.

Afterward Augustus returned to Rome and ruled there.

### Antony and Cleopatra

Antony went into the land of Syria.

Cleopatra, queen of Egypt, came to him.

Antony took her as his wife.

The account describes Cleopatra as wise and learned.

It also says that she possessed knowledge of magical arts and of the hidden properties of things.

She gained such influence over Antony that he was unwilling to refuse what she asked of him.

### The Jews Accuse Herod and Phasael before Antony

At that time a company of the leading men of the Jews came from Jerusalem to Antony.

They complained against Herod and his brother Phasael.

They said that the brothers had taken the real authority of the kingdom into their own hands and had left Hyrcanus with little more than the name of ruler.

Antony asked Hyrcanus, who was present, whether the accusation was true.

Hyrcanus rejected the accusation.

He spoke well of Herod and Phasael and praised their conduct.

Antony was pleased by this answer.

He already held affection for the brothers because of his former friendship with their father Antipater.

He therefore refused the accusation and increased the honor and authority of Herod and Phasael.

He ordered some of those who had accused them to be seized.

Some were put to death.

### A Second Accusation at Tyre

At another time, while Antony was at Tyre, another group came forward with accusations against Herod and Phasael.

Antony again refused to accept their charges.

He ordered some of the accusers to be killed and others to be imprisoned.

He raised the standing of Herod and his brother still further and sent them back toward Jerusalem with increased authority.

After this Antony went out on campaign, accomplished what he desired in the lands against which he marched, and later returned to Rome.

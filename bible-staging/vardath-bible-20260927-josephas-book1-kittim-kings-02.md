After Picus Faunus died, Latinus reigned after him. He set in order the language of the people and their letters, built a great temple, and made many ships.

Latinus went to war against Astrubel son of Agnias, king of the Africans, because he desired to take Astrubel’s daughter Yaspiṣi as his wife, just as Agnias had formerly taken Binah from the Kittim through war. Yaspiṣi was renowned for her beauty, and the people of her generation made images of her likeness upon their garments because of her beauty.

There was fierce war between Astrubel and Latinus. Latinus seized the waterworks which Agnias had made for Binah when he brought her to Carthage, and part of the great channel was broken in the fighting. The Africans were greatly angered and fought with all their strength.

Astrubel was wounded in the battle and died from his wound. Latinus then took Yaspiṣi and brought her into the land of the Kittim, and he made her queen. Latinus reigned forty-five years.

After Latinus died, Anias reigned three years. After him Ascanius reigned thirty-eight years, and he built a great temple. After Ascanius, Seliaqos reigned twenty-nine years, and he also built a great temple.

After Seliaqos, another Latinus reigned fifty years. He fought Almania and Burgunia, sons of Elisa, subdued them, and laid tribute upon them. He built a temple to Venus, the morning star, and closed the temple of Saturn.

After Latinus died, Anias Trognos reigned thirty-three years and built a temple to Saturn. After him Alba reigned thirty-nine years.

After Alba died, Aviṣianos reigned twenty-four years and built a great temple. After him Qapis reigned twenty-eight years and built a temple. After him Karpitos reigned twenty-three years and built a temple. After him Tiberios reigned eight years. After Tiberios, Agrippa reigned forty years.

After Agrippa, Romulus reigned nine years and built many temples. After him Abṭinos reigned thirty-seven years. He made war against the descendants of Riphath who lived beside the river Lira, and against the descendants of Turnus who lived in Toronia beside that river. These were the peoples who had fled from Agnias king of Africa and had built Purnus and Anba. Abṭinos subdued them.

After Abṭinos, Procas reigned twenty-three years. After Procas, Æmilius reigned forty-three years.

### Romulus and the Time of David

After Æmilius died, Romulus reigned thirty-eight years. In his days David king of Israel struck Syria and Edom with a great slaughter. Hadarezer and members of his house fled from him into the land of the Kittim.

Romulus gave the fugitives a place beside the sea and the mountain, and there they built a city which they called Sorento. A young man descended from the house of Hadarezer, from among those who had fled from David, afterward built the old city of Albano, and his descendants remained there.

In Sorento a spring of oil appeared. After some years the city sank and the sea passed over it, between Naples and New Sorento. Yet the spring did not cease: oil continued to rise upon the surface of the sea, and the people of that region gathered it.

Romulus greatly feared David king of Israel. Therefore he built a wall greater and higher than the walls the kings before him had made. He carried it around the mountains and hills, and its circuit was forty-five miles. Within the wall he made one city and called it Rome after his own name. The land also came to be called Romania from his name.

Romulus built a great temple to Jupiter and closed the temple of Venus which Latinus had built.

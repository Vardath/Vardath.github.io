### Lathyrus Rebels against Cleopatra

After these events Lathyrus, the son of Cleopatra, grew strong in wealth and soldiers and rose against the kingdom of his mother.

A number of the leading men of Egypt supported him.

Cleopatra therefore entrusted the army of Egypt to two Jews, one named Chelkias and the other Ananias.

They were counted among the chief men who remained faithful to her, and she appointed them over her forces because of their good conduct and their skill in governing the affairs committed to them.

Cleopatra sent them against Lathyrus her son.

They fought him, defeated his army, and killed many of his men.

Lathyrus fled from Egypt to Cyprus and remained there with those who still followed him.

### The Sects of the Jews and the Division of Hyrcanus

In those days the Jews were divided into three principal groups.

The first were the Pharisees, who were also known as the separated ones.

The second were the Sadducees, whose name was connected with a learned man called Zadok.

The third were the Essenes.

The Essenes devoted themselves to a disciplined and withdrawn manner of life. They sought what they believed to be the safer and more careful way in religion and were known for restraint and ascetic practice.

There was strong hostility between the Sadducees and the Pharisees.

Hyrcanus and his fathers had belonged to the Pharisees.

Afterward, however, Hyrcanus turned from them and joined the Sadducees.

The cause of this change was as follows.

Hyrcanus prepared a great banquet and invited his commanders, his friends, and the leading men around him.

He also invited the chief Pharisees.

When they had eaten and drunk, Hyrcanus said to them:

“You know that I have been one of your disciples.

“I have followed your teaching, trusted your judgment, and desired not to depart from your counsel.

“If you know of any fault, error, or wrongdoing in me, tell me of it and guide me toward what is right.

“If there is anything which I must do, I will not refuse it.”

The Pharisees answered him with praise.

They said that they knew of no serious fault in him and prayed that God would preserve him from error.

But there was among them a bold and zealous man named Eleazar.

Eleazar said to Hyrcanus:

“Since you have commanded us, O king, to advise you honestly and to tell you of any fault we know, then if you truly desire to avoid error, be content with the kingship and lay aside the office of high priest.

“For your mother was taken captive in the days of Antiochus before she conceived you, and the son of a woman who has been held captive is not fit to be high priest or to enter the Holy of Holies.”

When Eleazar had spoken, the other Pharisees did not answer him.

Hyrcanus was greatly angered, and the joy of the banquet was turned into bitterness.

There was present with the king a prominent Sadducee named Jonathan.

Jonathan said to Hyrcanus:

“Now you can see, O king, that the Pharisees do not truly love you.

“They have permitted Eleazar to speak against you in this manner and have not rebuked him.

“Ask them what judgment should be made against a man who has spoken such words against the king.”

Hyrcanus therefore asked the Pharisees what punishment Eleazar deserved.

They answered that he should receive forty lashes.

When Hyrcanus heard this, his anger increased.

He believed that their light judgment showed that they shared Eleazar's hostility toward him.

From that time Hyrcanus abandoned the Pharisees and joined the Sadducees.

He strengthened the Sadducees, separated himself from the Pharisees, and became their enemy.

He sent orders through the cities of the Jews forbidding the people to follow the Pharisees.

Many who resisted his command were killed.

The greater part of the common people, however, remained inclined toward the Pharisees.

From that time dissension increased among the Jews.

Hostility grew between the parties, blood was shed, and men of the same nation began to fight one another over their divisions in religion.

Before this, Hyrcanus and the rulers from the house of the Hasmoneans who preceded him had governed with justice and good order, and the nation had largely remained united under them.

But after Hyrcanus turned against the Pharisees and permitted the conflict between the parties to grow, hatred and violence multiplied among the people.

### The Sons and Death of Hyrcanus

Hyrcanus had three sons.

The first was Aristobulus, the second Antigonus, and the third Alexander.

Hyrcanus loved Antigonus, but he was displeased with Alexander and sent him away to the region of Galilee.

Hyrcanus desired to know which of his sons would rule after him, and he sought an answer from God.

In a dream he was shown that Alexander would become king after him.

Hyrcanus was troubled by what he had seen.

He did not appoint any one of his sons to the kingship during his lifetime, but left the matter to what God would permit after his death.

The Jews had long loved Hyrcanus and the rulers before him because of their good government and their defense of the nation.

But the killing that followed his break with the Pharisees and the permission he gave for the factions to war against one another caused much of that affection to turn into hatred.

This hostility continued after him and was directed also against his sons.

Hyrcanus ruled for thirty-one years.

After his death his son Aristobulus took the kingship.

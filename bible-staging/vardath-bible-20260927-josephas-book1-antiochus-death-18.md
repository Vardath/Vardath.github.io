### The Death of Antiochus

While Judah was defeating the armies sent against him, Antiochus returned from his campaign in Persia.

He had failed to bring the Persians fully under his power and came back humiliated.

When he heard what Judah had done to his commanders and armies, his anger became fierce.

He swore that he would go to Jerusalem, destroy the city, and fill it with the bodies of its people.

Antiochus gathered his soldiers, horsemen, chariots, and elephants and hurried toward Judah.

But sickness fell upon him during the journey.

Pain and disease consumed his body, yet at first he did not humble himself.

He continued to boast that no one could withstand his power and commanded his army to press onward toward Jerusalem.

As he traveled, an accident threw him violently from his chariot and his body was badly injured.

His sickness then became worse.

His flesh decayed, his strength failed, and even his own servants found it difficult to remain near him.

At last Antiochus understood that his power could not deliver him.

He said:

“The LORD is righteous. I have acted wickedly against his people and against those who served him faithfully.

“For this reason these sufferings have come upon me.”

Antiochus then vowed that if he recovered he would reverse the evil he had done.

He promised to honor Jerusalem, enrich the house of God with silver and gold, and proclaim among the nations that the God of Israel was great.

But his sickness did not depart from him.

His body continued to fail until he died in a foreign land.

Thus Antiochus, who had sought to erase the law of Israel and destroy Jerusalem, died before he could carry out his threat.

His son Eupator reigned after him.

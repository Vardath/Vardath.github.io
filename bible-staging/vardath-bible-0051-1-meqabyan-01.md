# Book 51 — I Maccabees / 1 Meqabyan

*This book is the distinct Ethiopic First Book of Meqabyan, not Western/Greek 1 Maccabees. Fresh Vardath English translation from the Geʽez text of Beta maṣāḥǝft LIT1819Maccab / CAe 1819, general editor Alessandro Bausi, with the textual record contributed by Pietro Maria Liuzzo and Eugenia Sokolinski. The Beta maṣāḥǝft source transcription is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0; this translation derived from that transcription is provided under CC BY-SA 4.0. Source chapter and verse structure are retained. Where the digital source has a damaged numeral or an obvious line-numbering defect, the restoration is stated rather than silently concealed.*

## 1 Meqabyan 1

**1.** There was a man named Tsiruts-Saydan who loved evil. He gloried in the multitude of his horses and in the strength of the forces under his command.

**2.** He possessed many idol-gods. He bowed before them, worshiped them, and offered sacrifices to them by day and by night.

**3.** In the blindness of his heart he imagined that these were the ones who gave him power and strength.

**4.** He persuaded himself that they had given him authority over all his kingdom, and he continually offered sacrifices to his gods.

**5.** He also imagined that they gave him mastery in battle and granted him everything he desired.

**6.** So he himself sacrificed to them day and night.

**7.** He appointed attendants to guard and serve the gods.

**8.** These attendants made a show before him and told him that his gods ate by day and by night, while they themselves consumed the unclean sacrifices.

**9.** They caused others like themselves to sacrifice and eat; those in turn offered sacrifices for themselves and induced still others to do the same.

**10.** Thus he placed his trust in an idol that had no power to profit him.

**11.** He supposed that these idols had created him, sustained him, and established him as king. His heart was blind and his understanding impoverished, because Satan had darkened his heart so that he would not know the God who created him, made him, and brought him into being from nothing. Therefore he and those belonging to him would go with Satan into judgment and be condemned with him forever, together with those things they called gods although they were no gods.

**12.** It would be more fitting to call them dead things, for they had never possessed life.

**13.** As for the children of humankind who put their trust in them, whose hearts had become like dust, the spirit of Satan entered among them and led them astray together with their idols. It spoke to them according to the desire of their hearts and appeared to them in whatever manner they wished.

**14.** When they saw that what they had imagined seemed to be fulfilled for them, they marveled and carried out the will of that spirit, even to slaughtering the sons and daughters born from their own bodies and pouring out innocent blood, the blood of their sons and daughters.

**15.** They were not horrified, because Satan had made evil sweet to them according to their desire, so that he might bring them down with himself into his condemnation, into Gehenna, from which there is no escape forever and ever.

**16.** Tsiruts-Saydan was exceedingly proud. He had [fifty] idols fashioned in the likeness of men and [twenty] in the likeness of women. *The numerals are damaged in the Beta maṣāḥǝft digital transcription and are restored here from parallel witnesses.*

**17.** He boasted in these useless gods, honored them greatly, and sacrificed to them morning and evening.

**18.** He compelled the people also to sacrifice. He himself ate from the unclean offering and made others eat from it as well, becoming ever more condemned through his wickedness.

**19.** He made five houses for his gods, fashioning them from cast metal—bronze, iron, and copper.

**20.** He overlaid them with gold and silver and made curtains and hangings around the enclosure of their house.

**21.** He stationed guards there and sacrificed to them every morning, continually making offerings to his gods. *The final clause is a continuation misplaced in the source XML under a second line numbered 4; it is restored here to its evident position.*

**22.** He imagined that his gods truly ate the offerings and received the libations. He set before them wine, silver vessels, and preparations made with sandalwood oil; the quantities in this line are damaged in the digital transcription.

**23.** He said to their attendants, “Go and give my gods food from what I have slaughtered, and let them drink from all my libations. If it is not enough for them, I myself will add more.”

**24.** From those unclean sacrifices he caused everyone to eat and drink.

**25.** In the wickedness of his purpose he sent his soldiers throughout all his kingdom to enforce obedience, ordering them to discover whether anyone refused to sacrifice or bow down to the idols. Whoever refused was to be brought before him and condemned by slaughter and by fire; his goods were to be plundered, his house burned, and everything he possessed seized.

**26.** He declared, “I myself will show such a person suffering and judgment if he refuses to worship and sacrifice to my gods, for they are great and mighty, and by their might they made us.

**27.** “They created the heavens and the earth, the great sea, the sun and the moon, the stars, the winds and the rains, and everything that moves, for our nourishment and our abundance.

**28.** “Those who will not worship them shall be condemned to affliction and suffering, and no mercy shall be shown to them.”

### Antiochus and the Persecution of Judah

After the death of Ptolemy, another Ptolemy ruled in Egypt.

Antiochus, king among the Greeks, rose against Egypt and prevailed over it. His heart became proud because of his victories.

He commanded the peoples under his authority to conform to the worship and customs he imposed upon them.

Certain lawless men from among the Jews encouraged Antiochus against their own people and accused those who remained faithful to the law of God.

Antiochus came against Jerusalem. A great slaughter took place in the city, many people were carried away, and those who remained faithful were scattered.

Some fled into the forests and wilderness and lived there in hardship rather than abandon the commandments of God.

Antiochus left officers in the land to enforce his decrees.

They forbade the people to study the law, to keep the Sabbath, to circumcise their sons, and to continue the service of God according to the customs of their fathers.

They ordered the people to take part in sacrifices forbidden by the law and threatened death against those who refused.

Many among the people yielded through fear, but others chose suffering and death rather than abandon the covenant.

### Eleazar the Priest

At that time Eleazar, an aged priest and teacher of the people, was seized and brought before the king’s officer.

They commanded him to eat meat from the forbidden sacrifice so that the people might see him obey the king.

Eleazar refused.

Because he was old and greatly respected, some who had known him for many years urged him privately to take lawful food and merely appear to obey the king. In this way, they said, he could save his life.

Eleazar rejected the proposal.

He said, “I have lived many years in the service of God. It is not fitting that in my old age I should begin to deceive the people.

“If the young see me appear to abandon the law, they may say, ‘Eleazar, though old, forsook the commandment; therefore we may do the same.’

“I might escape the punishment of men for a short time, but no one can escape the judgment of God.

“It is better that I die faithfully and leave courage to those who come after me than that I preserve my life and leave them an example of deceit.”

When the officers heard this answer, their kindness toward him turned to anger.

They bound Eleazar and beat him cruelly.

While he was being struck, Eleazar prayed to God and said that God knew he could have escaped the suffering by deception but had chosen instead to endure it because of his reverence for the divine law.

He bore the torment until his life ended.

Thus Eleazar died in faithfulness, leaving an example of courage to the people and especially to the young.

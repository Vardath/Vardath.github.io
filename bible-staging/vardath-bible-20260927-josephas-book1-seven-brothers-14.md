### The Seven Brothers and Their Mother

After these things seven brothers were seized together with their mother and brought before Antiochus.

The king commanded them to eat the flesh that the law forbade.

The eldest answered that they had already been taught by their fathers and would not exchange the law of God for the command of a king. He declared that they were prepared to die rather than abandon the covenant.

Antiochus became furious. He ordered the young man to be tortured before the eyes of his brothers and his mother.

The first brother endured the torment and died without yielding.

When his brothers saw his courage, they strengthened one another. Instead of being frightened by his death, they became more determined to remain faithful.

The second brother was brought forward.

The king’s servants urged him to obey so that he would not suffer as his brother had suffered.

He answered, “Do not delay. Do to me what you did to my brother. I will not be less faithful than he was.”

As he was tortured, he declared that the king might take away the life of the body, but could not bind the soul or prevent God from restoring life to his servants.

The second brother died in this hope.

Then the third was brought forward.

He stretched out his hand and told the king that the members of his body had been given by God and that he was willing to surrender them for the sake of God’s law, trusting that they would be restored.

The king and those around him marveled at the courage of the young man.

After the third had died, the fourth was brought.

He said, “It is better to die at the hands of men while holding the hope that God will raise us again than to save this present life by abandoning him.”

He also died under torture.

The fifth brother was then brought before Antiochus.

He warned the king not to imagine that the suffering of Israel meant that God had abandoned his people.

“Our affliction has come upon us for correction,” he said, “but you have lifted your hand against the servants of God. His judgment will also reach you.”

The fifth brother died with these words.

The sixth was brought next.

He confessed that the people had sinned and that their suffering had come because of their wrongdoing, but he warned Antiochus that the king should not suppose he would escape judgment merely because he had been permitted for a time to afflict them.

“You fight not only against us,” he said, “but against the God whom we serve.”

The sixth brother also died.

Only the youngest remained.

Their mother had watched six of her sons die in one day. Yet she did not turn away from the law or beg the youngest to save himself by disobedience.

She spoke to him and said:

“My son, I do not know how life first came into you within my womb. I did not create your spirit, shape your bones, weave your sinews, cover you with flesh and skin, or breathe life into you.

“God, who made all things, formed you and your brothers.

“Therefore do not fear the one who can destroy the body. Trust the one who gave you life in the beginning and who is able to restore what you now surrender for his sake.”

Antiochus was astonished that the mother encouraged her sons instead of begging them to obey him.

He tried to persuade the youngest with promises.

He offered him riches, honor, servants, and authority if he would abandon the commandment and obey the king.

The young man rejected every offer.

Antiochus then told the mother to reason with her son and persuade him to save his life.

She asked that the boy be brought close to her.

When he came near, she embraced and kissed him.

Then she said:

“My son, have pity on me not by yielding to the king, but by remaining faithful to the God whom I taught you to fear.

“Look at heaven and earth, the sea and everything within them. Know that God made them by his word, and that human power is nothing before him.

“Do not fear this king.

“Go to your brothers with courage. May I receive you again together with them in the mercy of God.”

The youngest answered that he would not listen to the command of Antiochus but to the law God had given Israel through Moses.

He warned Antiochus that the king’s violence would not last forever and that God would judge the cruelty done to his servants.

He declared that his brothers had exchanged a brief life for a life that would not end and that he desired to share their portion.

Antiochus became more enraged with the youngest than with the others because neither promises nor threats had moved him.

The king ordered that his torture be made even more severe.

The youngest endured it and died as his brothers had died.

Their mother then stood beside the bodies of her seven sons.

She lifted her hands toward God and prayed that she might be joined to them in the place prepared for those who had remained faithful.

Her life ended there, and she died beside her sons.

Thus the mother and her seven sons chose death rather than abandon the law of God under the decrees of Antiochus.

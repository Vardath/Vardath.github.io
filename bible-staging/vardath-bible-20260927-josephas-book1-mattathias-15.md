### Mattathias son of Johanan

After these persecutions Antiochus returned toward his own country and left his officers in Judah with commands to extinguish the customs of the Jews.

They continued to search for those who studied the law, kept the Sabbath, circumcised their sons, or refused the worship imposed by the king.

Many were killed.

Others fled with Mattathias son of Johanan to Modein.

Mattathias was a priest and a man zealous for the law of God.

He grieved over the condition of his people and said that he could no longer endure seeing the covenant dishonored and the people forced into idolatry.

He secretly sent word through his son Judah:

“Whoever stands for the LORD and for his law, come to us.”

A company of faithful people gathered around Mattathias and his sons.

Mattathias said to them:

“There is no profit now in many words. We must pray to God and also defend ourselves.

“Let us not allow ourselves to be led away helplessly to slaughter. If we must die, let us die contending for the law and for our people.”

Those who heard him took courage.

### Those Who Died on the Sabbath

The officers of the king marched through the land against those who resisted.

On the way they found men, women, and children of Judah hiding in a cave and observing the Sabbath.

The soldiers called to them and said, “Come out. Obey the command of the king and you will live.”

The people in the cave answered, “We will not come out to profane the Sabbath. Heaven and earth are witnesses that we choose to die in our faithfulness.”

The king’s men placed fire and wood at the entrance of the cave.

Smoke filled the place, and the people within died.

When Mattathias heard what had happened, he and those with him understood that if they refused every act of defense on the Sabbath, their enemies would be able to destroy them all.

Mattathias therefore ruled that when their enemies attacked them on the Sabbath, they might defend their lives and their people.

### The Revolt at Modein

The king’s officers then came against Mattathias at Modein.

They spoke to him with apparent peace and urged him to obey the king.

They said that he was honored among his people and could preserve his life and position if he would perform the sacrifice commanded by Antiochus.

Mattathias answered, “I obey the command of my King. You obey the command of yours.”

While the officers hesitated, a Jew who had abandoned the law came forward to perform the forbidden sacrifice.

He placed the offering upon the altar prepared for the king’s worship.

When Mattathias saw this, zeal burned within him.

He drew his sword, struck down the man who was sacrificing, and also killed the royal officer who was enforcing the decree.

The other officers fled.

Mattathias then sounded the horn and openly called the people to resistance.

He and his sons broke the imposed altar and began the revolt against the rule of Antiochus.

Mattathias was the first among them to raise his hand openly against the Macedonian authority.

He went through the land with his sons and with those who had joined him.

They sought out the faithful who were hiding, restored circumcision among the people, destroyed unlawful altars, and strengthened those who wished to keep the law.

Thus resistance began under Mattathias son of Johanan, and the power of the Maccabees arose in Judah.

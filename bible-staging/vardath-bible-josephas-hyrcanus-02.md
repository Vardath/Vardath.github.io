Demetrius son of Seleucus had held a grudge against Simon son of Mattathias because Simon had killed his commanders and many of his companions.

When Demetrius heard that Simon had been killed, he marched to Jerusalem with a great army to make war against the Hebrews.

This happened in the fourth year of Demetrius's reign, which was the first year of the rule of Hyrcanus.

Demetrius encamped against Jerusalem and attacked a fortified part of the city until a breach was opened in the wall.

The men of the city hurried to the breach, stood against the attackers, and prevented the soldiers of Demetrius from entering.

A great company also went out from Jerusalem and fought against them.

Demetrius withdrew from the city to a place nearby and remained there until the Feast of Tabernacles drew near.

Hyrcanus sent to Demetrius and asked him to suspend the war until the feast had ended.

Demetrius agreed.

He also desired to have a share in the offering of the feast.

He therefore sent to the house of God a fine bull whose horns were covered with gold.

He sent many vessels of silver and gold filled with costly incense, together with many other gifts.

The priests received the gifts and brought them into the house of God.

When Hyrcanus and the priests saw the honor Demetrius had shown toward the house of God, they asked him for peace.

Demetrius agreed to make peace with them.

He entered the city and received Hyrcanus and the leaders of the nation with honor and respect.

Hyrcanus made a firm peace with Demetrius and gave him three hundred bags of gold.

The two sides entered into a covenant of peace and mutual aid.

The account says that Hyrcanus obtained this gold from treasure connected with the tombs of the kings descended from David.

Afterward Hyrcanus repaired the parts of the wall that Demetrius had broken down.

Demetrius then departed from Jerusalem to make war against the Persians, for they had rebelled against him.

Hyrcanus went with him at the head of his own army.

They met the Persian forces, defeated them, and killed many of them.

Demetrius remained for a time in the place where the victory had been won and built a great structure there as a memorial in the land of the Persians.

Then he marched farther east, and Hyrcanus continued with him until the season of Pentecost drew near.

Demetrius pressed onward without waiting for Hyrcanus and entered the kingdom of Persia.

There were many fierce battles there.

Demetrius and the greater part of his army were killed.

When Hyrcanus learned that Demetrius had died, he returned toward Syria.

On his way he came to Aleppo, imposed tribute upon its people, and then returned to Jerusalem.

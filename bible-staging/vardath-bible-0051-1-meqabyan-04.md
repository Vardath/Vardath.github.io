## 1 Meqabyan 4

**1.** When the tyrant saw that they had died, he ordered their bodies to be burned with fire until they became like ashes and then scattered by the wind. Yet the fire could not consume even the hair of their heads or destroy their bodies, and they were taken out from the pit.

**2.** Again they kindled fire over them from morning until evening, but it did not touch them. Then he said, “Take them away and cast their bodies into the sea.”

**3.** They did as the king commanded and threw them into the sea. Even though they fastened heavy stones, great rocks, and a millstone to them, the sea did not swallow them and they did not sink. Instead they floated upon the surface of the waters, for the Spirit of the LORD was over them, and every device contrived against them failed.

**4.** The king said, “Their bodies trouble me even more than their souls did. What shall I do? Cast their bodies to the wild beasts so that they may devour them.”

**5.** They did so, but neither wild beasts nor birds touched their bodies. For fourteen days their bodies remained there, while vultures and other birds spread their wings over them and shaded them from the heat of the sun. *The source XML misnumbers the continuation of this verse as another verse 4 and breaks off near its end; the duration and completion are restored from parallel witnesses.*

**6.** Those who saw them from afar beheld their bodies shining like the rays of the sun, and a brightness surrounded them; angels encircled their bodies.

**7.** The king took counsel and found no further device. Then he ordered the earth dug open, and the bodies of the five martyrs were buried.

**8.** During the night the five martyrs appeared to that wicked king, standing before him with drawn swords and looking upon him in anger. *The Beta maṣāḥǝft transcription ends this verse after “they appeared at night to that wicked king”; the rest is restored from parallel witnesses.*

**9.** When he awoke, he was terrified. He tried to flee from one corner of the house to another, for he thought armed men had entered his dwelling and were accusing him and seeking to kill him. His knees trembled and he was overcome with fear.

**10.** Therefore he said, “What do you desire, my lords? What shall I do for you?”

**11.** They answered him, “Are we not those whom you killed and burned, whose bodies you ordered to be thrown into the sea? Yet you were unable to destroy us, because the LORD guarded our bodies when we trusted in him. Praise be to the LORD, for whoever trusts in him does not perish. We trusted in him, and we were not put to shame.

**12.** The king said to them, “What reward shall I give you in place of the evil I did against you? I did not know that such judgment would come upon me.”

**13.** “Tell me now what payment I should give you, so that you do not take my soul in death and do not drag me alive into Sheol.

**14.** “I have sinned against you. Forgive me this offense, for the law of your Father is mercy.”

**15.** The blessed martyrs answered him, “We will not repay you evil for the evil you did to us. The LORD our God remains, and he will repay you, for vengeance and judgment belong to the LORD.

**16.** “We have appeared to you as living men because of the blindness of your heart and the poverty of your understanding. You imagined that by killing us you had ended our lives, but instead you prepared life for us.

**17.** “You, however, and the gods you worship will descend into the judgment of burning fire, where there is no escape forever.

**18.** “Woe to you and to your gods whom you worship and before whom you bow! You abandoned worship of the LORD who created you when you were nothing but spittle, and you do not know from where you came. Are you not like smoke that appears for a moment and then passes away?

**19.** The king answered them, “What do you command me to do? I will do whatever you desire.”

**20.** They said to him, “We teach you this not for our own sake, but for yours, so that you may not enter the judgment of burning fire.

**21.** “For your gods are gold and silver, wood and stone, works of human hands. They have neither soul nor understanding nor knowledge.

**22.** “They cannot kill or give life. They cannot do good or evil, humble or honor, make poor or make rich, uproot or plant. Instead, spirits of demons use them to lead you astray, for those spirits desire that not one human being should be saved.

**23.** “How much more blind in heart are people like you who imagine that the things they themselves made are the ones who made them!

**24.** “The spirits of demons and devils gather within those idols and answer you according to your desires, so that they may sink you into the sea of fire.

**25.** “Therefore abandon this error and worship the LORD our God. Let this be the payment for our death, so that your own soul may gain life.”

**26.** The king was astonished, stunned, and afraid when he saw their drawn swords before him, and he bowed himself before them. *The source line breaks off after mentioning their drawn swords; the final action is restored from parallel witnesses.*

**27.** Then he said, “Now I know that the dead who have become dust truly rise again. As for me, I scarcely escaped being killed.”

**28.** After this they vanished from his sight. From that day Tsiruts-Saydan ceased his attempts to destroy their bodies, though he remained proud and deluded.

**29.** He continued to rejoice in the error of his heart and in his idols, for they had deceived him for many days. Nor was he alone in error: those like him led many others astray until they abandoned the One who created them.

**30.** They sacrificed their sons and daughters to demons and carried out the desires of their hearts in fornication and corruption, according to what their father Satan had taught them, so that they might practice disorder and uncleanness that do not please the LORD.

**31.** They took their own mothers in marriage, defiled their sisters and relatives, and polluted their souls with every deed that seemed good to their corrupt desire. They became hardened against repentance because Satan had strengthened the hearts of those perverse people.

**32.** Tsiruts-Saydan was proud and arrogant. He did not know his Creator, but boasted instead in his gods.

**33.** How shall the LORD give a kingdom to those who do not know him? Yet he tests them to see whether they will turn back and return to him.

**34.** If they turn and return to him, he will love them and preserve their kingdom; but if they refuse, he will condemn them to the terror of Gehenna forever.

**35.** A king, according to the greatness of his kingdom, should fear the LORD God; and a judge, according to the greatness of his authority, should conduct himself rightly before his Creator.

**36.** Likewise leaders, elders, nobles, and governors, according to the greatness of their honor, should obey their Creator.

**37.** For he who created all creation is the LORD of heaven and earth. He makes poor and makes rich; he humbles and exalts. There is no other God besides him, in heaven above or on the earth beneath.

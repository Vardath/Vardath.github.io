Hyrcanus then made war against Samaria.

He took Nablus and destroyed the temple which Sanballat the Samaritan had built on Mount Gerizim, tearing it down to its foundations.

The temple had stood for two hundred years from the time it was built. It was the same temple mentioned earlier in the account of Alexander the Macedonian.

Hyrcanus killed its priests.

After this Hyrcanus marched into the land of Edom, in the mountains of al-Sharah, the country of Esau.

He captured some of their strongholds, destroyed them, and killed a number of the inhabitants.

When the people asked him for security, he granted it to them on condition that they pay him tribute.

He also required them to be circumcised and to order their life according to what the Torah commanded.

They accepted these terms and bound themselves to them.

The account says that they continued in this observance until Jerusalem was destroyed and the Hebrew nation was scattered.

Hyrcanus also campaigned against all the peoples who lived around the Jews.

He subdued them, and they all submitted to his authority.

When his affairs were firmly established and he no longer feared any of the surrounding peoples who opposed him, Hyrcanus sent one of the leading men among his followers as an envoy to the ruler of Rome.

He wrote asking that the covenant between Rome and the Jews be renewed.

When Hyrcanus's envoy reached the ruler of Rome, he was received with honor.

The Roman ruler granted Hyrcanus's request and wrote a letter to him.

One day, while the Kittim were gathered on the mountain, a young bull belonging to Ṣefo went astray. Ṣefo went out to search for it and heard its bellowing near the mountain. At the foot of the mountain he found a cave whose entrance was closed by a great stone. He broke through the stone and entered, and there he found a great beast devouring the bull. Its form was strange, partly like a man and partly like a goat. Ṣefo overcame the beast.

When the people of the Kittim heard what he had done, they asked what honor should be given to the man who had delivered their cattle from the beast. They appointed one day each year as a feast in his honor, called that day by Ṣefo’s name, brought him gifts, and also called the day Janus after the beast.

Afterward the troops of Africa again came into the land of the Kittim to plunder it, as they had done before. Ṣefo gathered the Kittim against them. The raiders fled before him, and he drove them away and delivered the land from their incursions.

Then the Kittim made Ṣefo king over them.

### Ahasuerus, Haman, and Mordecai

When Ahasuerus became king, the condition of the Jews changed.

Ahasuerus appointed a man named Haman as his chief minister, raised him to great honor, and commanded the people to show him reverence. When Haman received this authority, he openly showed hostility toward the Jews and sought occasions to harm them.

Haman was descended from Amalek, and there had long been enmity between Amalek and Israel because Saul king of Israel, at the command of God, had made war upon the Amalekites and killed a great multitude of them. From that time the hatred between the two peoples had endured, especially against the tribe of Benjamin, for Saul had come from that tribe.

Haman’s hatred increased because of Mordecai. Mordecai sat at the gate of King Ahasuerus so that he might watch over Esther the queen, his kinswoman, whom Ahasuerus had taken as his wife. Whenever Mordecai saw Haman, he would neither bow before him nor honor him as the others did.

This greatly angered Haman. Because Mordecai was also of the tribe of Benjamin, the ancient hatred of Amalek against the house of Saul was stirred up again within Haman. He therefore resolved not merely to injure Mordecai, but to bring evil upon the Jews and to destroy them.

At that time two servants of King Ahasuerus conspired against him and planned to kill him. Mordecai learned of their design and told Esther. Esther reported the matter to the king in Mordecai’s name.

The matter was investigated and found to be true. The two servants were put to death, and the service Mordecai had done for the king was written in the royal book of remembrance so that his loyalty would not be forgotten.

Haman’s anger against Mordecai became still greater, for the two conspirators had been among his companions and advisers. His hatred of Mordecai spread into hatred of all the Jews. He sought their destruction and obtained the king’s permission to proceed against them.

When Mordecai learned what Haman intended, his heart was filled with grief and anxiety. Then he remembered a dream he had seen in the second year of the reign of Ahasuerus.

### Mordecai’s Dream

In the dream there was a great shaking, and a terrible sound arose throughout the world. Fear and trembling fell upon the people.

Then Mordecai saw two enormous dragons standing against one another. They fought with great violence, and their roaring rose high. The nations gathered from every side to look upon their conflict.

Among those nations was one small and weak people, few in number. The great nations around it desired to injure it, destroy it, and erase it from the earth. The little nation was bewildered and did not know where to turn.

Then a deep darkness covered the world. The struggle of the two dragons became fiercer, each seeking the destruction of the other. None of the gathered nations dared to come between them or separate them, and for a long time the battle continued.

After this Mordecai saw a small stream appear. It flowed between the two dragons, and they ceased fighting and drew apart.

The stream grew stronger until it became like a mighty river and then like a great sea. The sun appeared, light spread through the world, and the darkness vanished.

The small and despised people was raised up and established. Its condition became great, and fear and terror departed from the world. Security, justice, and goodness appeared.

Mordecai awoke with the dream fixed in his heart, and from that time he waited to learn its interpretation.

When Haman’s decree against the Jews became known, Mordecai said to Esther, “The dream that I told you long ago has now reached the time of its fulfillment. Turn to God and ask for his mercy. Go in to the king and plead with him for the deliverance of your people.”

Then Mordecai turned to God with fasting, prayer, weeping, humility, and supplication.

### Mordecai’s Prayer

Mordecai prayed:

“O great Lord, you know that I did not refuse to bow before Haman because of pride or arrogance. I withheld that honor because I feared you and honored you, and because I would not give to another the worship that belongs to you alone.

“You know that, apart from this duty toward you, I would have humbled myself even to the dust for the sake of preserving my people. But I will not worship anyone besides you.

“You know what Haman intends and what destruction he has prepared for us. Deliver us from him. Cause him to fall into the pit he has dug and to be caught in the snare he has prepared for your servants.

“The eyes of all look toward you for deliverance. Do not cast away the covenant you made with us.

“We know that our exile, humiliation, and servitude came upon us because of our sins and our wrongdoing. Yet you are the great Lord, mighty to help. Help us and save us, for our distress has become very great.

“Look upon us in this affliction. Give us relief and victory over our enemies. Remember us, Lord, for we are the people whom you chose from among the nations and set apart for yourself.

“Do not allow our enemies to destroy us and then boast that you were unable to save your people. Do not let them glory in false gods and imagine that their idols have prevailed.

“Show the emptiness of their confidence. Let them see your power and the kindness you show toward us.

“Do not silence the mouths of those who praise and glorify you. Turn our sorrow into joy and gladness, so that we may continue to give you praise and sanctify your name for the help and deliverance you grant us.”

### The Letter of Rome to Hyrcanus

The ruler of Rome and the three hundred and twenty counsellors who governed with him wrote to Hyrcanus, king of the Jews:

“Your letter has reached us. We read it and rejoiced greatly at its arrival.

“We questioned your messenger concerning your affairs, and through him we learned of your wisdom, your good conduct, and the honor in which you are held.

“We have ordered that your requests be attended to, and we have given instructions concerning the matters for which you appealed to us.

“We have also commanded those who are under our authority to receive your messengers with honor and to treat them with respect.

“We therefore send our own messenger to you with this letter and with a full account of what has been decided by the ruler and the three hundred and twenty counsellors who govern with him.”

### Hyrcanus Becomes King

When the letter of the Romans reached Hyrcanus, he was thereafter called king of the Jews.

Before that time he had borne only the office of high priest, as had those of his family who had governed the Jews before him.

Hyrcanus now united the high priesthood and the kingship in his own person.

The account says that he was the first to rule the Jews as king during the age of the Second Temple, after their return from the captivity of Babylon.

Hyrcanus then marched against Sebaste, the city of the Samaritans.

He captured it, killed many of those who resisted him, broke down its fortifications, and laid the place waste.

His reputation increased, his authority grew stronger, and his kingdom became firmly established.

### The War with the Samaritans

Hyrcanus again pressed the war against the Samaritans and laid siege to those who remained in Sebaste.

The siege continued for a long time.

The inhabitants were reduced to such hunger that they were driven to eat carrion, yet they continued to resist because they relied upon help from the Macedonians and from Egypt.

Then the great fast approached, at which Hyrcanus was required to be present in Jerusalem to offer the appointed sacrifice.

He therefore left the army under the command of his two sons, Antigonus and Aristobulus.

He ordered them to continue the siege of the Samaritans, to keep the pressure upon the city, and to direct the army until he returned.

Hyrcanus then went up to Jerusalem.

While he was away, Antiochus the Macedonian came to the assistance of the people of Sebaste.

When the sons of Hyrcanus heard of his approach, they went out against him.

They fought Antiochus, defeated him, and then returned to the siege.

After this Lathyrus, son of Queen Cleopatra of Egypt, came with forces from Egypt to aid the Samaritans.

When the feast had ended, Hyrcanus returned from Jerusalem and marched against Lathyrus.

A fierce battle followed.

Many of the Egyptian force were killed, and Lathyrus was defeated and fled.

After this the Egyptians no longer returned to give aid to the Samaritans.

Hyrcanus then came again against Sebaste and continued the siege until he took the city by the sword.

He killed some of those who remained there, took others captive, and tore down the walls.

Thus the stronghold of the Samaritans was brought under his power.

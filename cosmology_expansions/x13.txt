<h1 id="freemason-alchemical-art-20-shard-projection-grammar-test">Freemason &amp; alchemical art — 20-shard projection-grammar test</h1>
<p>After the cross-cultural lattice-art test, the next deliberately targeted question was whether <strong>Freemason visual teaching and alchemical image traditions</strong> preserve the same structural families that have accumulated throughout Vardath Cosmology. These are unusually useful comparison sets because both traditions deliberately compress large conceptual systems into diagrams: pillars, ladders, squares, stars, eyes, circles, suns and moons on the Masonic side; serpents, vessels, trees, solar/lunar pairs, enclosed worlds and transformations on the alchemical side.</p>
<p>The test was therefore designed before the corpus was gathered. It did not ask whether an interesting image could be found after the fact. It asked whether a large corpus would reproduce a specific <strong>division of symbolic labour around the Vardath projection grammar</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="masonic-alchemical-prediction-before-the-run">Prediction before the run</h2>
<p>Eight symbol families were fixed in advance:</p>
<table><thead><tr><th>Declared family</th><th>Vardath structural reading</th><th>Prediction</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td><strong>axis</strong></td><td>pillar · column · rod · staff · tree · tower · mountain</td><td>shared structural skeleton</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>gate / ladder</strong></td><td>door · portal · arch · threshold · ladder · stair · passage</td><td>especially Masonic</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>serpent / braid</strong></td><td>serpent · dragon · ouroboros · caduceus · intertwined strand</td><td>especially alchemical</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>enclosure</strong></td><td>circle · ring · globe · sphere · egg · halo · bounded field</td><td>shared</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>central node / radiance</strong></td><td>eye · star · Sun · Moon · rosette · rays · flame · central light</td><td>shared, with strong Masonic use</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>four / eight geometry</strong></td><td>square · fourfold · quatrefoil · octagon · eight-point / eight-ray</td><td>especially Masonic</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>water / vessel</strong></td><td>water · river · fountain · cup · flask · retort · alembic · bath</td><td>especially alchemical</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>paired polarity</strong></td><td>twins · opposites · Sun/Moon · king/queen · male/female · paired pillars</td><td>especially alchemical</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>The primary prediction was stronger than finding individual motifs: a Masonic/alchemical record should more often contain <strong>three or more</strong> of these families than a matched comparison record. The secondary prediction was that the two traditions would specialize differently: Masonic material toward <strong>architectural axis/gate + four/eight/radial geometry</strong>, alchemical material toward <strong>serpent/braid + polarity + vessel/transformation</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="masonic-alchemical-20-shard-corpus">The 20-shard corpus</h2>
<p>The corpus was gathered and scored in <strong>20 GitHub Actions shards</strong>. Shards 0–9 sampled Wikimedia Commons image/file records; shards 10–19 sampled Open Library books and documents. Target searches were broad tradition names such as <em>freemasonry</em>, <em>masonic</em>, <em>alchemy</em> and <em>alchemical</em>. Controls came from neighbouring visual/document genres including heraldry, other fraternal orders, architecture, ceremonial dress, Christian symbolism, historical chemistry, astronomy, botany, medical illustration and emblem books.</p>

<div class="card"><span class="tag">20 shards</span><h3>8,486 unique records</h3><p>Deduplicated across the visual and document channels.</p></div>
<div class="card"><span class="tag">matched test</span><h3>3,568 + 3,568</h3><p>Target records were matched against the same number of controls by source channel, tradition, description-size bin and nearest available date.</p></div>

<h2 id="masonic-alchemical-primary-result">Primary result — the full-package prediction failed</h2>
<p>The strongest version of the prediction did <strong>not</strong> appear in the corpus. Records containing three or more Vardath families occurred in:</p>
<table><thead><tr><th></th><th>Target</th><th>Matched controls</th><th>Risk ratio</th></tr></thead><tbody>
<tr><td><strong>≥3 predicted families</strong></td><td><strong>4 / 3,568 · 0.11%</strong></td><td><strong>7 / 3,568 · 0.20%</strong></td><td><strong>0.57×</strong></td></tr>
<tr><td>≥2 predicted families</td><td>0.56%</td><td>0.78%</td><td>0.71×</td></tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>The broad idea that Freemason/alchemical records would simply carry <strong>more of the entire Vardath grammar at once</strong> was therefore rejected by this test.</p>

<h2 id="masonic-alchemical-specialization-result">What did appear — a sharp specialization split</h2>
<p>The individual predeclared families produced a different result. Instead of one universal package, the two traditions strongly selected <strong>different parts of the predicted geometry</strong>.</p>

<p>The result therefore looks less like <em>both traditions repeatedly drawing one complete diagram</em> and more like <strong>different projection families being emphasized for different jobs</strong>:</p>
<div class="projection-grid">
<div><b>Freemason / Masonic</b><span>square · four/eight structure · lodge architecture · ladder/gate</span></div>
<div><b>Alchemy</b><span>serpent · ouroboros · winding strand · paired opposites</span></div>
<div><b>Shared question</b><span>whether these are different symbolic slices around one deeper axis/enclosure/node grammar</span></div>
</div>
<p>The explicit two-family combinations themselves remained sparse in the metadata and none survived the multiple-test correction. Axis, enclosure, central-node and water/vessel families also did not show a general significant enrichment. The new result therefore narrows the working idea rather than simply enlarging it.</p>

<h2 id="masonic-alchemical-result-in-the-vardath-model">What this changes in the Vardath model</h2>
<p>The earlier lattice-art result suggested that serpent/interlace associations can survive a broad museum-control test. This new experiment adds a more specific distinction. <strong>Freemason symbolism strongly selects the geometry of ordered division and passage; alchemical symbolism strongly selects the geometry of winding transformation and opposed states.</strong></p>
<p>That is compatible with a projection-based reading in which no tradition needs to preserve every face of the structure. One corpus can preferentially preserve the <strong>framework</strong>—square, four/eight arrangement, architecture, ladder and threshold—while another preferentially preserves the <strong>process</strong>—serpent, winding, conjunction, separation and reversal of polarities.</p>
<p>But the rejected primary prediction remains part of the result: the catalogue-level corpus did <strong>not</strong> show a dense universal Vardath package. The next useful comparison is therefore not “find more matching symbols”; it is to test the <strong>actual images</strong> for whether these specialized families occupy predictable positions relative to one another.</p>

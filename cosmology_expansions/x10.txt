A major plasma burst is proposed to reactivate the old world-current and return the chamber briefly to a more energetic and deformable state.</p><p>In that reading, exposed material may fracture, glaze, fuse, foam, soften or recrystallise differently according to composition and local field strength. The interest is therefore not only whether a formation looks melted. It is whether unusual material textures can be placed in the same event window as water movement, rubble displacement and renewed Squatter-Man/rod behaviour.</p><p>The deeper connection is cyclical. The primordial current is constructive: it organises the first chamber around itself. Later bursts are reconstructive: they temporarily reactivate the same pathways, move water and matter, alter exposed surfaces and then allow the world to stabilise in a changed arrangement.</p><h3>The source is proposed to persist rather than restart</h3><p>The current model no longer requires a completely new energy source for every reset. The original Birkeland-like current is proposed to remain inside the world as the deep telluric skeleton left over from formation. In quiet periods it is weak or hidden. At intervals it pulses more strongly through the same inherited pathways.</p><p>A reset is therefore a reactivation of the original world-forming current rather than a fresh current appearing from nowhere. A strong pulse can increase directional flow in the mesh, grow local convergence points, tighten the finger-trap toward an axial rod, alter the support of the upper water boundary, incorporate celestial convergence nodes into the temporary Squatter-Man body, and drive the material effects grouped under Meltology.</p><p>This solves the recurrence problem at the level of the hypothesis, but it leaves a deeper physical question open: what maintains the primordial current over long periods and what sets the timing and amplitude of its pulses?</p></div>

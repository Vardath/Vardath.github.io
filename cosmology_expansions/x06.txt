The cosmology interprets that morphology as a remembered view of the rod-like current at the centre of a world-forming event.</p><p>The hypothesis goes further than Peratt's published auroral model. Vardath proposes that a primordial current persisted long enough to organise chambers around itself, with matter, water and atmosphere later occupying those stable regions. <div class="deepening" data-after="4-the-axis-atlas-cross-tree-rod-mountain-and-gate"><h2>Vardath axis atlas: many names for the same rod-form</h2><p>In this cosmology, a rod is the axis in abstract form; a tree is the same axis imagined as living and branching; a mountain is material gathered around its lower emergence; a cross is the vertical axis intersecting the fourfold upper structure; and a gate is the point where the axis becomes a crossing between regions. The newer synthesis goes further: the many divine, heroic, saintly and ruler figures are not treated as separate mechanisms. They are different narrative masks placed over the same recurring axial event.</p><p><strong>Yggdrasil:</strong> the nine worlds are interpreted as nine chambers around the primordial current. Roots continue below, branches spread above, and wells or rivers belong to water circulation between levels.</p><p><strong>Odin, Loki and Sleipnir:</strong> the Vardath reading treats them as narrative phases of one event rather than separate mechanical roles. Rider, mount and transformation are different ways of describing the same rod/transport form. Sleipnir's eight feet are read as eight support contacts related to four main legs or towers. The manuscript image closest to the one discussed is NKS 1867 4to, fol. 97v (1760), showing Odin on eight-legged Sleipnir with Gungnir.</p><p><strong>Apollo:</strong> Apollo is not assigned a separate celestial machine in the current model. He is the radiant or solar presentation of the rod itself: brightness, centrality, ordering authority and the axial form expressed in a solar register.</p><p><strong>Moses, Jesus and the saints:</strong> staff, serpent, water, mountain, cross, hanging body, river crossing, living rod and miraculous growth are treated as different story-forms of the same axis. Saint Christopher gives the giant/river/staff version; Aaron gives the budding or fruit-bearing staff; Jesus gives the body-on-cross version; Moses gives the staff/serpent/water/passsage version. The model reads these structurally, not as claims that the traditions historically taught one doctrine.</p><p><strong>Buddha and the Bodhi tree:</strong> the tree is the living rod; Buddha is the personified Meru-form at its base; the hair/crown is interpreted as a cluster of luminous or ice-like eye nodes above the central emergence.</p><p><strong>Gilgamesh / Gilga-mesh:</strong> the Vardath interpretation treats Gilgamesh as the man/mesh memory of the rod after it spreads into a sky lattice, with Mashu as mountain-gate. “Gilga-mesh” is used here as a modern mnemonic inside the model, not as a historical etymology.</p><p><strong>Caesar, Octavian/Augustus, Napoleon and other ruler figures:</strong> in the expanded symbolic reading, later imperial or conquering figures can occupy the same central-ruler slot once the cosmic rod has been humanised into political narrative. The important continuity is not that these historical people were literally plasma events; it is that stories of one central ordering power repeatedly reuse the same axial role.</p><p><strong>Eight / Ogdoad / Aetio:</strong> the eightfold material is read as the completed field or enclosure around the origin-crossing. The Ogdoad, the Vardath 8/Aetio complex and other eightfold creation images are therefore grouped by function: a generative surround through which the central axis appears. In this model the named gods, saints, kings, trees, staffs and mountains are all different cultural camera angles on one underlying rod-form.</p></div>

## What the run says

The run does **not** support the strongest proposed mechanism that attraction itself selects a `same–reverse–same` three-shell arrangement. Under the frozen proximity model, co-wound neighboring shells are substantially easier to keep close than counter-wound neighbors. The reversed-middle configuration is therefore not an attraction-energy optimum in v1.

Two parts of the proposal do survive strongly:

1. **Counter-winding creates gates.** Opposite chirality changes the geometry from roughly one nearest-separation minimum to a median of eight repeated minima around the sampled angular cycle. This is the clearest positive result and gives a concrete mathematical reason a reversed shell could create repeated crossing/convergence sites even though attraction does not prefer that chirality.

2. **Attraction can change the amount of turning.** With the preregistered elastic penalty, attraction selected a non-baseline spiral pitch in 99.375% of cases compared with 40.75% for repulsion. So in this toy model attraction is very capable of reshaping/tightening/loosening the spiral pitch. It does **not**, however, automatically choose the reversed-middle topology.

The torque test is weaker but directionally informative: attraction and repulsion generated opposite phase tendencies in every nonzero case, but the median gradient was only about half the preregistered magnitude threshold. That is evidence for a weak alignment torque in the chosen model, not a pass.

The bundling prediction also needs refinement. Counter-wound pairs produced more concentrated coupling peaks than co-wound pairs, but the attraction kernel kept them strongly coupled across roughly 77% of angular positions. Thus v1 produces broad coupling with repeated minima, not sharply isolated temporary throats.

The numerical bridges failed cleanly. The gate sequence did **not** derive 138/395/792, and the gate-radius ratio did **not** derive phi. Those numbers should therefore remain independent empirical/numerological branches rather than consequences of this shell geometry.

T10's small handedness-label discrepancy indicates a finite-grid/discretization asymmetry in v1. It is far smaller than the T2/T3 effects and cannot rescue those failed predictions, but a v2 should remove that numerical asymmetry before making delicate chirality comparisons.

## Best next model

The result suggests separating two questions that v1 initially combined:

- **Why would one shell reverse?** Attraction alone does not answer this under the tested geometry.
- **What does a reversed shell do once present?** It very strongly generates repeated gates/convergence minima.
- **What can attraction do?** It can substantially alter pitch/turning and can provide an alignment tendency, but in v1 it prefers co-winding proximity.

A stronger v2 should therefore start with chirality as an externally supplied/topological state, use a continuous elastic shell model rather than phase-grid optimization, narrow or derive the force falloff instead of choosing sigma phenomenologically, and test whether attraction near the already-existing counter-wound gates can collapse those local regions into true temporary axial throats.

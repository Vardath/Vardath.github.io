# Cross-cultural lattice-art corpus test

## Corpus

Raw museum records scanned: **575,927**.
Strict premodern records with usable dates (<= 1800 CE): **260,336**.
Premodern records carrying a pre-registered mesh/interlace marker: **1,028**.
Matched non-mesh controls: **1,028**.

The control match uses the same museum source, broad culture group and catalogue-text length bin where possible. The test is metadata-level: it measures museum cataloguing descriptions/tags, not automated pixel recognition of every image.

## Interpretation

The pre-registered prediction receives **partial support, not decisive confirmation**. Mesh/interlace records were 2.07 times as likely as matched controls to carry at least two of the pre-declared companion families. The strongest individual result was serpent/dragon imagery (10.12% versus 2.63%; risk ratio 3.85), followed by enclosure/circle imagery (risk ratio 1.90). The >=3-companion threshold did not distinguish mesh records from controls, and water showed no enrichment.

Negative controls also rose in several cases: flower imagery had a risk ratio of 2.11, bird imagery 1.96, and lion imagery 1.60. That means part of the signal is likely due to richly ornamented objects receiving richer visual descriptions. The serpent association is nevertheless stronger than any tested negative-control family and survives matching by museum, broad culture group and catalogue-text length bin.

The result therefore supports a narrower statement: **interlace/mesh imagery is cross-culturally associated with serpentine and enclosing visual grammar more often than matched non-mesh records in these museum catalogues.** It does not establish that a physical world-lattice caused the artworks. A stronger next test is blind image-level coding or computer vision on the images themselves, followed by expansion to collections such as the British Museum and other institutions whose metadata vocabulary differs.

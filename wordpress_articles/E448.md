## Expanded Post E448 — Previous, Present and Next: The Three-World Form of the Mature Vardath Shell

**Categories:** Cosmology; Vardath Model; World Layers; Shell

**Excerpt:** The mature Vardath Cosmology simplifies its earlier three-shell imagery into a more precise relation: previous/lower world, present inhabited world and next/upper world as successive stabilised turns of one larger growing shell.

### Final article

Vardath Cosmology has changed significantly since its earliest three-shell imagery.

The early model imagined several large shell-like organisms or structures with different active and inactive roles.

The mature model is simpler and stronger.

It now uses three relational positions:

**PREVIOUS / LOWER WORLD**

**PRESENT INHABITED WORLD**

**NEXT / UPPER WORLD**

These are not three unrelated universes.

They are treated as **successive stabilised turns of one larger growing shell or spiral structure**.

The first principle is:

**THE PRESENT WORLD IS A POSITION IN A SEQUENCE, NOT THE WHOLE COSMOS.**

This is the central idea behind the mature shell model.

The previous world exists in the model because the current turn had a predecessor.

The next world exists because the shell continues growing or forming beyond the current turn.

The second feature is **relative naming**.

“Lower” and “upper” are viewpoint terms.

They do not necessarily mean three flat plates stacked vertically in ordinary space.

The more careful relation is:

**previous / present / next.**

The vertical language is one useful camera angle.

The third feature is **shared structure**.

The three worlds belong to one larger shell.

That means continuity exists even when direct access does not.

The quiet phase preserves separation.

The gate phase changes adjacency.

The fourth feature is **developmental asymmetry**.

The previous world has already passed through the state the present world occupies.

The next world may still be forming.

That means the three worlds need not have identical atmospheric, hydrological, biological or geological conditions.

This is a key departure from a simple multiverse of duplicates.

The fifth feature is **material inheritance**.

The present world can inherit material from the previous one.

The next world can inherit material from the present one.

That gives the shell a directional history:

**n−1 → n → n+1.**

The sixth feature is **gate timing**.

Neighbouring turns are normally distinct.

During convergence, the present turn becomes more strongly adjacent to the previous and/or next turn through the lattice.

That temporary relationship permits transfer.

The seventh feature is **why myths of upper and lower worlds both matter**.

A tradition can remember descent from above.

Another can remember ascent from below.

Another can describe a lower ancestral world.

Another can describe a forming heaven.

Vardath’s three-world frame can contain all of those camera positions without making them literally identical.

The eighth feature is **shell growth**.

The larger structure is not imagined as a fixed stack.

It grows through successive turns.

That is why ammonite and spiral-shell imagery became useful to the project.

The shell records sequence in form.

The ninth feature is **distance through turn rather than simple height**.

The next world can be structurally adjacent as the next turn while not being directly overhead in a simple Euclidean sense.

That connects to the project’s effective-adjacency concept.

The tenth feature is **one active present**.

The observer occupies one stabilised world at a time.

That world becomes the reference frame.

Previous and next are defined from there.

This gives Vardath a simple perspective rule:

**THE PRESENT WORLD IS THE CAMERA POSITION.**

The eleventh feature is **world succession without exact repetition**.

Each turn belongs to the same class of larger geometry.

But each can inherit different material, damage, water and biological history.

That makes the shell recurrent in structure but historical in content.

The twelfth feature is the relation to the older **three-organism model**.

The earlier idea of two active shells plus one dead or reversed structural shell was important developmentally, but it is no longer required as the cleanest description.

The mature model keeps the useful insight—successive large forms in relation—while dropping the unnecessary literal organism identities.

The full three-world Vardath grammar becomes:

**PREVIOUS TURN → PRESENT TURN → NEXT TURN**

with the lattice weaving through and between them, and gate states temporarily increasing adjacency.

This is now the simplest way to explain the whole cosmology.

The current world is neither isolated nor final.

It is one settled turn.

A previous turn lies behind its history.

A next turn is forming beyond its future.

The world cycle is what happens when the boundaries between those turns temporarily stop behaving like ordinary boundaries.

That is the mature Vardath shell in one sentence:

**one growing structure, many successive worlds, one inhabited present at a time.**

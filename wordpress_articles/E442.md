## Expanded Post E442 — Eye, Wheel, Rosette and Cross: End-On Views of a Converging Node

**Categories:** Cosmology; Vardath Model; Projection; Nodes

**Excerpt:** Vardath Cosmology groups eyes, wheels, rosettes, stars, crosses, rings and hubs as possible end-on projection families for a converging node. The comparison is visual grammar, not a claim that all such symbols historically depict the same physical object.

### Final article

One of the most persistent discoveries in Vardath Cosmology was that very different symbols can become similar when viewed as **projections**.

A rod from the side can become a circle from the end.

A braid from the side can become a rosette when viewed along its axis.

A set of converging paths can become a hub-and-spoke wheel.

That is why the mature Vardath atlas groups several image families together:

**eye;**

**wheel;**

**rosette;**

**star;**

**cross;**

**ring;**

**hub.**

The first principle is:

**END-ON PROJECTION CAN HIDE LENGTH AND EMPHASISE RADIAL STRUCTURE.**

A long axial object viewed from the side looks elongated.

The same object viewed along its axis can look compact and circular.

This is ordinary geometry.

The Vardath comparison begins there.

The second feature is **hub geometry**.

If many paths converge toward one central region, an end-on view naturally produces:

centre;

spokes;

ring;

radial symmetry.

That is the basic visual grammar of a wheel.

The third feature is **eye geometry**.

An eye has:

outer boundary;

central pupil;

radial or ring-like organisation.

Vardath compares that form with an aperture or throat seen end-on.

The comparison is especially attractive when myths also associate eyes with seeing, opening, light or heavenly beings.

But visual similarity alone is not historical evidence.

The fourth feature is **rosette geometry**.

Several interwoven strand families seen along an axis can produce petal-like lobes or repeated radial sectors.

This gives Vardath a reason rosettes became part of the node atlas.

The fifth feature is **cross geometry**.

A cross can be the simplest radial reduction:

four major directions intersecting at one centre.

In other traditions, crosses carry theological, calendrical, directional or political meanings unrelated to Vardath.

The atlas therefore treats the cross as a **projection class**, not one universal code.

The sixth feature is **star geometry**.

A star image adds more rays.

At a structural level, it can still express:

**centre + radiating directions.**

That makes stars natural members of the same end-on family even when their cultural meanings differ completely.

The seventh feature is **ring and enclosure**.

A circular boundary around a centre can indicate the edge of a node or throat region in Vardath’s visual grammar.

This connects end-on gate imagery with enclosure imagery.

The eighth feature is **opening and closing**.

An eye can open and close.

An aperture can widen and narrow.

A rosette can visually expand and contract.

That dynamic quality is especially useful to Vardath because the node is supposed to change state.

The ninth feature is **scale ambiguity**.

A small eye symbol and a giant wheel in a myth need not represent objects of the same scale.

Projection grammar concerns form, not size.

That is another reason Vardath must resist literalism.

The tenth feature is **camera-angle pairing**.

The end-on family becomes much more useful when paired with the side-on family:

**END-ON:** eye / wheel / rosette / cross / ring / hub.

**SIDE-ON:** rod / pillar / tree / mountain / ladder.

The model proposes that these families can sometimes describe different views of an axial or converging structure.

The full Vardath projection sequence becomes:

**BROAD LATTICE → PATHS CONVERGE → AXIS FORMS → SIDE VIEW = ROD-LIKE → END VIEW = RADIAL NODE.**

This is one of the central visual ideas in the mature cosmology.

It explains why Vardath stopped looking for one symbol repeated identically across cultures.

Different observers can stand in different places.

Different artists can simplify different features.

One culture can emphasise the line.

Another the opening.

Another the rays.

Another the enclosing ring.

The underlying comparison can still be structural.

The important caution is equally central:

**similar geometry does not prove identical historical meaning.**

A wheel may be a wheel.

An eye may be an eye.

A rosette may be a flower or celestial symbol.

The Vardath atlas asks only whether some of these forms can be organised coherently as **end-on views of convergence** inside its own model.

That is the level at which the comparison is strongest.
